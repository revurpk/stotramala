Bhagavad Gītā · Chapter 3

Karma Yoga · the yoga of action · with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya

Arjuna asks why, if understanding is higher than action, he is urged into this terrible deed. Kṛṣṇa teaches action as sacrifice, done for the welfare of the world and without claim to its fruit, and names desire and anger as the enemy. Śaṅkara's preface to the chapter argues against combining knowledge and ritual action as a single path.

bhāṣya · the chapter's opening

śāstrasya pravṛttinivṛtti viṣayabhūte dve buddhī bhagavatā nirdiṣṭe, “sāṅkhye buddhiryoge buddhiriti ca”. tatra “prajahāti yadā kāmā”ni (2.55) tyārabhya adhyāyaparisamāpteḥ sāṅkhyabuddhyāśritānāṃ sannyāsaṃ kartavyamuktvā teṣāṃ tanniṣṭhatayaiva ca kṛtārthatoktā “eṣā brāhmī sthiti” (2.72) riti, arjunāya ca “karmaṇyevādhikāraste” “māte saṅgo'stvakarmaṇī” ti (2.47) karmaiva kartavyamuktavān yogabuddhimāśritya, na tata eva śreyaḥprāptimuktavān, tadetadālakṣya paryākulībhūtabuddhirarjuna uvāca. “kathaṃ bhaktāya śreyo'rthine yatsākṣācchreyaḥprāptisādhanaṃ sāṅkhyabuddhiniṣṭhāṃ śrāvayitvā māṃ karmaṇi dṛṣṭānekānarthayukte pāramparyeṇāpyanaikāntika śreyaḥ prāptiphale niyuñjyāditi yuktaḥ paryākulībhūta bhāvorjunasya, tadanurūpaśca praśnaḥ “jyāyasī cedi” (3.1) tyādiḥ. praśnāpākaraṇaṃ ca vākyaṃ bhagavataḥ yuktaṃ yathoktavibhāgaviṣaye śāstre.

The Lord has pointed out two understandings, the subjects of the scripture's teaching on engagement and on withdrawal: 'the understanding in Sāṅkhya' and 'the understanding in Yoga'. From 'when a man casts off all desires' (2.55) to the end of the chapter he declared that those who rest on the Sāṅkhya understanding should renounce, and that they reach their goal through steadfastness in it alone — 'this is the Brāhmī state' (2.72). But to Arjuna he said, on the basis of the yoga-understanding, that action alone was to be done — 'your right is to action alone', 'let not your attachment be to inaction' (2.47) — and he did not say that the highest good is attained from that itself. Noticing this, Arjuna, his understanding confused, spoke. 'Why should he, having taught me — his devotee, who seeks the highest good — steadfastness in the Sāṅkhya understanding, which is the direct means of attaining the highest good, set me to action, which is seen to involve many evils and whose result, the highest good, is uncertain even indirectly?' Arjuna's confusion is reasonable, and so is the question that fits it, 'If you hold that understanding is superior…' (3.1); and the Lord's reply, removing the question, is fitting in a scripture whose subject is divided as described.

kecittu arjunasya praśnārthamanyathā kalpayitvā tatpratikūlaṃ bhagavataḥ prativacanaṃ varṇayanti, yathā cātmanā sambandhagranthe gītārtho nirūpitaḥ tatpratikūlaṃ ceha punaḥ praśnaprativacanayorarthaṃ nirūpayanti, kathaṃ? tatra sambandhagranthe tāvat, sarveṣāmāśramiṇāṃ jñānakarmaṇossamuccayo gītāśāstre niścito'rtha ityuktaṃ. punarviśeṣitañca yāvajjīvaṃ śruticoditāni karmāṇi parityajya kevalādeva jñānānmokṣaḥ prāpyata ityetadekāntenaiva pratiṣiddhamiti, ihatvāśramavikalpaṃ darśayatā yāvajjīvaśruticoditānāmeva karmaṇāṃ parityāga uktaḥ, tatkathamīdṛśaṃ viruddhamarthamarjunāya brūyādbhagavān, śrotā vā viruddhamarthaṃ kathamavadhārayet.

Some, however, imagine the meaning of Arjuna's question differently and describe the Lord's reply as contrary to it; and they explain the meaning of question and answer here in a way contrary to the meaning of the Gītā as they themselves set it out in their introductory text. How? In that introductory text they said that the settled meaning of the Gītā is the combination of knowledge and action for those in all stages of life; and they specified further that liberation through knowledge alone, abandoning the actions enjoined by śruti for life, is absolutely denied. But here, showing the stages of life as alternatives, they speak of the abandonment of just those actions enjoined by śruti for life. How could the Lord tell Arjuna something so contradictory, or how could the hearer grasp a contradictory meaning?

tatraitatsyāt, – gṛhasthānāmeva śrautasmārtakarmaparityāgena kevalādeva jñānānmokṣaḥ pratiṣidhyate, na tvāśramāntarāṇāmiti. etadapi pūrvottaraviruddhameva, kathaṃ? sarvāśramiṇāṃ jñānakarmaṇossamuccayo gītāśāstra niścito'rtha iti pratijñāya iha kathaṃ tadviruddhaṃ kevalādeva jñānānmokṣaṃ brūyāt āśramāntarāṇām? atha mataṃ, śrautakarmāpekṣayaitadvacanaṃ – “kevalādeva jñānācchrautakarmarahitāt gṛhasthānāṃ mokṣaḥ pratiṣidhyata” iti ; tatra gṛhasthānāṃ vidyamānamapi smārtaṃ karmāvidyamānavadanapekṣya jñānādeva kevalādityucyata iti, etadapi viruddhaṃ. kathaṃ? gṛhasthasyaiva smārtakarmaṇā samuccitāt jñānānmokṣaḥ pratiṣidhyate natvāśramāntarāṇāmiti kathaṃ vivekibhiśśakyamavadhārayituṃ kiñca yadi mokṣasādhanatvena smārtāni karmāṇyūrdhvaretasāṃ samuccīyante tathā gṛhasthasyāpīṣyatāṃ smārtaireva samuccayo na śrautaiḥ.

It might be said: liberation through knowledge alone, abandoning the rites of śruti and smṛti, is denied only for householders, not for those in the other stages. This too contradicts what comes before and after. How? Having declared that the settled meaning of the Gītā is the combination of knowledge and action for all stages of life, how could he here speak, against that, of liberation through knowledge alone for the other stages? If the view is that this statement is made with regard to śruti rites — 'for householders liberation through knowledge alone, without śruti rites, is denied' — and that the smṛti rites that householders do have are disregarded as if absent, so that it is called knowledge alone; this too is contradictory. How? How could the discerning determine that liberation through knowledge combined with smṛti rites is denied only for the householder and not for the other stages? Moreover, if smṛti rites are combined as a means to liberation for celibate ascetics, then let combination with smṛti rites alone, and not with śruti rites, be accepted for the householder too.

atha śrautaiḥ smārtaiśca gṛhasthasyaiva samuccayo mokṣāya, ūrdhvaretasāṃ tu smārtakarmamātra samuccitāt, jñānānmokṣa iti, tatraivaṃ sati gṛhasthasyaivāyāsabāhulyāt, śrautaṃ, smārtaṃ ca bahuduḥkharūpaṃ karma śirasyāropitaṃ syāt. atha gṛhasthasyaiva āyāsabāhulyakaraṇānmokṣassyāt, na āśramāntarāṇāṃ śrautanityakarma rahitatvāditi. tadapyasat, sarvopaniṣatsu itihāsapurāṇayogaśāstreṣu ca jñānāṅgatvena mumukṣossarvakarma sannyāsavidhānāt, āśramavikalpasamuccayavidhānācca śrutismṛtyoḥ. siddhaḥ tarhi , sarvāśramiṇāṃ jñānakarmaṇossamuccayaḥ na mumukṣo ssarvakarmasannyāsa vidhānāt, putraiṣaṇāyā vittaiṣaṇāyā lokaiṣaṇayāśca vyutthāyātha bhikṣācaryaṃ caranti (bṛ.u.3.5.1) tasmānnyāsameṣāṃ tapasāmatiriktamāhuḥ “nyāsa evātyarecayaditi, (ma.nā.u.21.2;3) “na karmaṇā na prajayā dhanena tyāgenaike amṛtatvamānaśu” riti ca “brahmacaryādeva pravraje” (jā bāla.u.4) (dityādyāḥ śrutayaḥ, “tyaja dharmamadharmaṃ ca ubhe satyānṛte tyaja ubhau satyānṛte tyaktvā yena tyajasi tattyaja (śāntiparva 329.40; 331.44) “saṃsāramevaṃ nissāraṃ dṛṣṭvā sāradidṛkṣayā, pravrajantyakṛtodvāhāḥ paraṃ vairāgyamāśritāḥ”(nāradapari. u.3.15) iti bṛhaspatiḥ “karmaṇā badhyate janturvidyayā ca vimucyate, tasmātkarma na kurvanti yatayaḥ pāradarśinaḥ” (śāntiparva 241.7) iti śukānuśāsanam. ihāpi ca “sarvakarmāṇi manasā sannyasya” ityādi. (5.13)

If it is said that combination with both śruti and smṛti rites leads to liberation for the householder alone, while for celibates liberation comes from knowledge combined with smṛti rites only — then the householder alone, because of his great labour, would have to carry on his head ritual of both śruti and smṛti, which is very painful. And if it is said that liberation belongs to the householder alone because he takes on great labour, and not to the other stages because they lack the obligatory śruti rites — that too is wrong, because all the Upaniṣads, the Itihāsas, the Purāṇas and the yoga scriptures enjoin the renunciation of all action for the seeker of liberation as an auxiliary of knowledge, and because śruti and smṛti enjoin the stages of life both as alternatives and in succession. Then it is established that knowledge and action are not combined for all stages of life, because the renunciation of all action is enjoined for the seeker of liberation: 'Having risen above the desire for sons, the desire for wealth and the desire for worlds, they lead the life of mendicants' (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.5.1); 'therefore they say renunciation surpasses these austerities', 'renunciation alone excelled' (Mahānārāyaṇa 21.2–3); 'not by action, not by offspring, not by wealth, but by renunciation some attained immortality'; 'one may renounce from studentship itself' (Jābāla 4); and so on in the śrutis. 'Give up dharma and adharma; give up both truth and untruth; having given up both truth and untruth, give up that by which you give them up' (Śānti Parva 329.40; 331.44). 'Seeing saṃsāra thus without substance, and wishing to see the substance, those who have not married renounce, resorting to supreme dispassion' (Nāradaparivrājaka 3.15) — so says Bṛhaspati. 'A creature is bound by action and freed by knowledge; therefore ascetics who have seen the other shore do not perform action' (Śānti Parva 241.7) — this is the instruction to Śuka. And here too: 'renouncing all actions with the mind' (5.13) and so on.

mokṣasya ca akāryatvānmumukṣoḥ karmānarthakyaṃ. nityāni pratyavāyaparihārārthamanuṣṭheyārthānīti cet, na, asannyāsaviṣayatvāt pratyavāyaprāpteḥ nahyagnikāryā dyakaraṇātsannyāsinaḥ pratyavāyaḥ kalpayituṃ śakyaḥ, yathā brahmacāriṇāṃ asannyāsināmapi karmiṇām. na tāvannityānāṃ karmaṇāṃ abhāvādeva bhāvarūpasya pratyavāyasyotpattiḥ kalpayituṃ śakyā, “kathamasataḥ sajjāyeta” (chāṃ.u.6.2.2) iti asatassajjanmāsambhavaśruteḥ. yadi vihitākaraṇādasambhāvyamapi pratyavāyaṃ brūyādvedaḥ tadānarthakaro vedopramāṇamityuktaṃ syāt; vihitasya karaṇākaraṇayoḥ duḥkhamātraphalatvāt. tathā ca kārakaṃ śāstraṃ na jñāpakamiti anupapannārthaṃ kalpitaṃ syāt. na caitadiṣṭaṃ. tasmāt na sannyāsināṃ karmāṇi. ataḥ jñānakarmaṇo– ssamuccayānupapattiḥ.

And since liberation is not something to be produced, action is pointless for the seeker of liberation. If it is said that obligatory rites must be performed to avoid the sin of omission — no, because the incurring of that sin applies only to one who has not renounced. One cannot suppose that a renouncer incurs sin by not tending the sacred fire and the like, as students and men of action who have not renounced may. Nor indeed can one suppose that sin, something positive, arises merely from the absence of obligatory rites, for the śruti 'how could the existent come from the non-existent?' (Chāndogya 6.2.2) declares that the existent cannot arise from the non-existent. If the Veda declared a sin, impossible as it is, from not doing what is enjoined, it would amount to saying that the Veda brings evil and is not authoritative, since both doing and not doing what is enjoined would bear only pain. And the scripture would then be one that makes something happen, not one that makes known — a meaning that cannot hold; nor is this accepted. Therefore renouncers have no actions; and so the combination of knowledge and action is impossible.

“jyāyasī cedityakarmaṇaste matā buddhiḥ” (3.1) iti arjunasya praśnānupapatteśca. yadi hi bhagavatā dvitīye'dhyāye jñānaṃ karma ca samuccitya tvayā anuṣṭheya mityuktaṃ syāt, tato'rjunasya praśnonupapannaḥ. “jyāyasī cetkarmaṇaste matā buddhiḥ” iti arjunāya cet buddhikarmaṇī tvayā anuṣṭheye ityukte, yā ca karmaṇaḥ jyāyasī buddhiḥ sā'pi uktā eva iti “tatkiṃ karmaṇi ghore māṃ niyojayasi keśava (3.1) ityupālambho vā praśno vā na kathañcanopapadyate. na cārjunasyaiva jyāyasī buddhiḥ. na anuṣṭheyā iti bhagavatā uktaṃ pūrvam iti kalpayituṃ yuktaṃ, yena “jyāyasī cet” iti praśnaḥ syāt.

It is impossible also because Arjuna's question, 'if you hold that understanding is superior to action' (3.1), would make no sense. If the Lord had said in the second chapter that knowledge and action were to be practised by Arjuna together, Arjuna's question would make no sense. If Arjuna had been told 'you are to practise both understanding and action', then the understanding that is superior to action had also been prescribed for him, and neither the complaint nor the question 'why then do you urge me into this terrible deed, Keśava?' (3.1) could make any sense. Nor is it right to imagine that the Lord had earlier said to Arjuna in particular that the superior understanding was not for him to practise, so that the question 'if you hold that understanding is superior' might arise.

yadi punarekasya puruṣasya jñānakarmaṇorvirodhādyugapadanuṣṭhānaṃ na sambhavatīti bhinnapuruṣānuṣṭheyatvaṃ bhagavatā pūrvamuktaṃ syāt, tato'yaṃ praśna upapannaḥ “jyāyasīce” dityādiḥ, avivekataḥ praśnakalpanāyāmapi bhinnapuruṣānuṣṭheyatvena jñānakarmaniṣṭhayoḥ bhagavataḥ prativacanaṃ nopapadyate, na cājñānanimittaṃ bhagavataḥ prativacanaṃ kalpyaṃ. asmācca bhinnapuruṣānuṣṭheyatvena jñānakarmaniṣṭhayorbhagavataḥ prativacanadarśanāt jñānakarmaṇoḥ samuccayānu'papattiḥ. tasmāt kevalādeva jñānānmokṣa ityeṣo'rtho niścito gītāsu sarvopaniṣatsu ca.

If, on the other hand, the Lord had said earlier that because knowledge and action conflict they cannot be practised at the same time by one person, and so are to be practised by different people, then the question 'if you hold that understanding is superior' does make sense. Even if the question is imagined to arise from lack of discrimination, the Lord's reply, which assigns the steadfastnesses of knowledge and action to different people, would not fit; and one cannot suppose that the Lord's reply arises from ignorance. And from seeing the Lord's reply, which assigns the steadfastnesses of knowledge and action to different people, it follows that knowledge and action cannot be combined. Therefore the settled meaning in the Gītā and in all the Upaniṣads is that liberation comes from knowledge alone.

jñānakarmaṇoḥ “ekaṃ vada niścitye” ti (3.2) caikaviṣayaiva prārthanā'nupapannā, ubhayossamuccayasambhave. “kurukarmaiva tasmāttva” (4.15) miti ca jñānaniṣṭhāsambhavamarjunasyāvadhāraṇena darśayiṣyati.

And the request concerning knowledge and action, 'tell me for certain the one' (3.2), asking for only one of them, would make no sense if both could be combined. And with 'therefore perform action' (4.15) he will show, by a restriction, that steadfastness in knowledge is not possible for Arjuna.

word by word · translation

jyāyasī superior cet if karmaṇaḥ to action te by you matā is held buddhiḥ understanding janārdana O Janārdana tat then kim why karmaṇi in action ghore terrible mām me niyojayasi do you engage keśava O Keśava

Arjuna said: If you hold that understanding is superior to action, Janārdana, why then do you urge me into this terrible deed, Keśava?

bhāṣya

jyāyasī iti. jyāyasī śreyasī, cet yadi, karmaṇaḥ sakāśāt, te tava, matā abhipretā, buddhiḥ jñānam, he! janārdana! yadi buddhikarmaṇī samuccite iṣṭe tadā evaṃ śreyaḥ sādhanam iti “karmaṇo jyāyasī buddhiḥ” iti karmaṇaḥ atiriktakaraṇaṃ buddheḥ anupapannam arjunena kṛtaṃ syāt. na hi tadeva hi tasmāt phalataḥ atiriktaṃ syāt. tathā ca, karmaṇaḥ śreyaskarī bhagavatā uktā buddhiḥ, aśreyaskaraṃ ca karma kuru iti māṃ pratipādayati, tat kiṃ nu kāraṇam, iti bhagavataḥ upālambham iva kurvan, tat kiṃ kasmāt karmaṇi, ghore krūre hiṃsālakṣaṇe, māṃ niyojayasi keśava iti ca yat āha tat ca, na upapadyate. atha smārtenaiva karmaṇā samuccayaḥ sarveṣāṃ bhagavatā uktaḥ arjunena ca avadhāritaḥ cet “tat kiṃ karmaṇi ghore māṃ niyojayasi” ityādi kathaṃ yuktaṃ vacanam? kiñca –

'If you hold that understanding is superior…' If understanding, knowledge, is held by you to be superior — better — than action, O Janārdana. If understanding and action were meant to be combined as one means to the highest good, Arjuna's setting understanding apart from action, 'understanding superior to action', would make no sense; for the same thing cannot be superior to itself in its result. And so — since the Lord declared understanding to be better than action, yet tells me 'do action', which is not conducive to the highest good — what is the reason? So, as if complaining against the Lord, he says 'why then do you urge me into this terrible, cruel deed, involving harm, Keśava?' — and that too would make no sense. And if the Lord had taught the combination with smṛti action alone for everyone, and Arjuna had understood it so, how could the words 'why then do you urge me into this terrible deed' be fitting? Moreover —

vyāmiśreṇeva vākyena buddhiṃ mohayasīva me |
tadekaṃ vada niścitya yena śreyo'hamāpnuyām

word by word · translation

vyāmiśreṇa iva with seemingly mixed vākyena words buddhim understanding mohayasi iva you seem to confuse me my tat that ekam one thing vada tell niścitya for certain yena by which śreyaḥ the good aham I āpnuyām may attain

With words that seem to mix things together you confuse my understanding. Tell me for certain the one thing by which I may attain the highest good.

bhāṣya

vyāmiśreṇeti : vyāmiśreṇeva yadyapi viviktābhidhāyī bhagavān tathāpi mama mandabuddheḥ vyāmiśram iva bhagavadvākyaṃ pratibhāti. tena mama buddhiṃ mohayasi iva. mama buddhivyāmohāpanayāya hi pravṛttaḥ tvaṃ tu kathaṃ mohayasi? ataḥ bravīmi buddhiṃ mohayasīva, me mama, iti. tvaṃ tu bhinnakartṛkayoḥ jñānakarmaṇoḥ ekapuruṣānuṣṭhānāsambhavaṃ yadi manyase tatra evaṃ sati tat tayoḥ ekaṃ buddhiṃ karma vā idam eva arjunasya yogyaṃ, buddhiśaktyavasthānurūpam iti niścitya vada brūhi. yena jñānena karmaṇā vā anyatareṇa śreyaḥ aham, āpnuyāṃ prāpnuyām. iti yaduktaṃ tadapi nopapadyate.

'With words that seem to mix things together…' Although the Lord speaks distinctly, to me, of slow understanding, the Lord's words appear as if mixed; by them you seem to confuse my understanding. You set out to remove the confusion of my understanding; how is it that you confuse it? So I say 'you seem to confuse my understanding'. If you hold that knowledge and action, having different agents, cannot be practised by one person, then decide which one of them — understanding or action — suits Arjuna, according to his capacity and state of understanding, and tell me the one by which, knowledge or action, I may attain the highest good. This too would not fit [on the combination view].

yadi hi karmaniṣṭhāyāṃ guṇabhūtamapi jñānaṃ bhagavatā uktaṃ syāt tat kathaṃ tayoḥ ekaṃ vada iti ekaviṣayaiva arjunasya śuśrūṣā syāt? na hi bhagavatā pūrvaṃ uktam “anyataradeva jñānakarmaṇoḥ vakṣyāmi naiva dvayam” iti yena ubhayaprāptyasambhavam ātmanaḥ manyamānaḥ ekameva prārthayet.

For if the Lord had taught knowledge even as subordinate within steadfastness in action, how could Arjuna wish to hear about only one, saying 'tell me the one of them'? The Lord had not said earlier, 'I shall tell you only one of knowledge and action, not both', so that Arjuna, thinking it impossible for himself to attain both, should ask for one alone.

word by word · translation

loke in world asmin this dvi-vidhā twofold niṣṭhā steadfastness purā of old proktā was declared mayā by me anagha O sinless one jñāna-yogena by the yoga of knowledge sāṅkhyānām of the Sāṅkhyas karma-yogena by the yoga of action yoginām of the yogins

The Blessed Lord said: In this world a twofold discipline was taught by me of old, sinless one: the yoga of knowledge for the Sāṅkhyas, and the yoga of action for the yogins.

bhāṣya

praśnānurūpameva prativacanaṃ śrī bhagavānuvāca –

The Blessed Lord gave a reply that fitted the question:

loke'sminniti ḥ– loke asmin śāstrārthānuṣṭhānādhikṛtānāṃ traivarṇikānāṃ, dvividhā dviprakārā, niṣṭhā sthitiḥ anuṣṭheyatātparyam, purā pūrvaṃ sargādau prajāḥ sṛṣṭvā tāsām abhyudayaniḥśreyasaprāptisādhanaṃ vedārthasampradāyam āviṣkurvatā, proktā, mayā sarvajñena īśvareṇa, he anagha apāpa. tatra kā sā dvividhāniṣṭhā ityāha – tatra jñānayogena jñānam eva yogaḥ tena, sāṅkhyānām ātmānātma viṣayavivekajñānavatāṃ, brahmacaryāśramāt eva kṛtasannyāsānāṃ vedāntavijñānasuniścitārthānāṃ paramahaṃsaparivrājakānāṃ brahmaṇyeva avasthitānāṃ, niṣṭhā proktā. karmayogena – karmaiva yogaḥ karmayogaḥ tena karmayogena, yogināṃ karmiṇāṃ niṣṭhā proktā ityarthaḥ.

'In this world a twofold discipline…' In this world, for those of the three classes qualified to practise the teaching of scripture, a twofold steadfastness — standing, the purport of what is to be practised — was taught of old, in the beginning of creation, when, having created beings, I set forth the tradition of the Veda's meaning, the means of attaining prosperity and the highest good, by me, the omniscient Lord, sinless one. What is that twofold steadfastness? He says: by the yoga of knowledge — knowledge itself is the yoga — steadfastness is taught for the Sāṅkhyas: those who have discriminating knowledge of Self and non-self, who have renounced from the stage of studentship itself, whose understanding of the meaning of Vedānta is certain, the paramahaṃsa wandering ascetics established in Brahman. By the yoga of action — action itself is the yoga — steadfastness is taught for the yogins, the men of action: that is the meaning.

yadi ca ekena puruṣeṇa ekasmai puruṣārthāya jñānaṃ karma ca samuccitya anuṣṭheyaṃ bhagavatā iṣṭam, uktaṃ vakṣyamāṇaṃ vā gītāsu, vedeṣu ca uktaṃ katham iha arjunāya upapannāya priyāya viśiṣṭabhinnapuruṣakartṛke eva jñānakarmaniṣṭhe brūyāt? yadi punaḥ “arjunaḥ jñānaṃ karma ca dvayaṃ śrutvā svayameva anuṣṭhāsyati, anyeṣāṃ tu bhinnapuruṣānuṣṭheyatāṃ vakṣyāmi” iti mataṃ bhagavataḥ kalpyeta, tadā rāgadveṣavān, apramāṇabhūtaḥ bhagavān kalpitaḥ syāt. tacca ayuktam. tasmāt kayāpi yuktyā na samuccayaḥ jñānakarmaṇoḥ.

If the Lord had wished knowledge and action to be practised together by one person for one human goal, and had said so or would say so in the Gītā, and if it were said so in the Vedas, how could he here tell Arjuna, who had come to him and was dear to him, of steadfastnesses in knowledge and action that are to be practised by distinct, different people? And if it were imagined that the Lord thought, 'Arjuna, having heard of both knowledge and action, will practise both himself; to others I will say they are to be practised by different people', then the Lord would be imagined to have attachment and aversion and to be unreliable — which is not right. Therefore knowledge and action cannot be combined by any reasoning.

bhāṣya

yat arjunena uktaṃ karmaṇaḥ jyāyastvaṃ buddheḥ, tacca sthitam, anirākaraṇāt. tasyāḥ ca jñānaniṣṭhāyāḥ sannyāsināmeva anuṣṭheyatvaṃ, bhinnapuruṣānuṣṭheyatvavacanāt. bhagavataḥ evameva anumatam iti gamyate. “māṃ ca bandhakāraṇe karmaṇyeva niyojayasi” iti viṣaṇṇamanasam arjunaṃ “karma nārabhe” iti manvānam ālakṣya āha bhagavān – na karmaṇāmanārambhāditi. athavā – jñānakarmaniṣṭhayoḥ parasparavirodhāt ekena puruṣeṇa yugapat anuṣṭhātum aśakyatve sati itaretarānapekṣayoḥ eva puruṣārthahetutve prāpte karmaniṣṭhāyāḥ jñānaniṣṭhāprāptihetutvena puruṣārthahetutvaṃ na svātantryeṇa, jñānaniṣṭhā tu karmaniṣṭhopāyalabdhātmikā satī svātantryeṇa puruṣārthahetuḥ, anyānapekṣā ityetam arthaṃ pradarśayiṣyan āha bhagavān –

What Arjuna said about understanding being superior to action stands, since it is not refuted. And from the statement that they are to be practised by different people it is understood that the Lord approves this: that steadfastness in knowledge is to be practised by renouncers alone. Seeing Arjuna dejected, saying 'you set me to action, the cause of bondage', and thinking 'I will not undertake action', the Lord says 'not by abstaining from actions'. Or else: since steadfastness in knowledge and in action conflict and cannot be practised by one person at the same time, one might think each is a cause of the human goal independently of the other. To show that steadfastness in action is a cause of the human goal by leading to steadfastness in knowledge, not independently, while steadfastness in knowledge, attained by means of steadfastness in action, is a cause of the human goal independently, needing nothing else, the Lord says:

na karmaṇāṃ iti. na karmaṇām anārambhāt aprārambhāt, karmaṇāṃ kriyāṇāṃ yajñādīnām iha janmani janmāntare vā anuṣṭhitānām upāttaduritakṣayahetutvena sattvaśuddhikāraṇānāṃ, tatkāraṇatvena ca jñānotpattidvāreṇa jñānaniṣṭhāhetūnāṃ. “jñānamutpadyate puṃsāṃ kṣayātpāpasya karmaṇaḥ | yathādarśatalaprakhye paśyatyātmānamātmani || ” (śāṃ.pa.204.8) ityādismaraṇāt, anārambhāt ananuṣṭhānāt, naiṣkarmyam niṣkarmabhāvaṃ karmaśūnyatāṃ, jñānayogena niṣṭhā niṣkriyātmasvarūpeṇaiva avasthānam iti yāvat, puruṣaḥ na aśnute na prāpnoti ityarthaḥ.

'Not by abstaining from actions…' Not by not undertaking actions — rites such as sacrifice performed in this birth or another, which by destroying accumulated sin are the cause of purity of mind, and through that, by giving rise to knowledge, the cause of steadfastness in knowledge; as the smṛti says: 'knowledge arises in men when their sinful karma is destroyed; then, as in a clear mirror, they see the Self in the self' (Śānti Parva 204.8) — not by not performing them does a man attain freedom from action: the state of being without action, the steadfastness of the yoga of knowledge, abiding in the actionless nature of the Self — that is the meaning.

“na karmaṇām anārambhāt naiṣkarmyaṃ puruṣo aśnute”, iti vacanāt tadviparyayāt, teṣām ārambhāt naiṣkarmyam aśnute iti gammate. kasmāt punaḥ kāraṇāt karmaṇām anārambhāt naiṣkarmyaṃ na aśnute iti? ucyate – karmārambhasyaiva naiṣkarmyopāyatvāt. na hi upāyam antareṇa upeyaprāptiḥ asti. karmayogopāyatvaṃ ca naiṣkarmyalakṣaṇasya jñānayogasya, śrutau iha ca pratipādanāt. śrutau tāvat prakṛtasya ātmalokasya vedyasya vedanopāyatvena tametaṃ vedānuvacanena brāhmaṇā vividiṣanti yajñena” (bṛ.u. 4.4.22) ityādinā karmayogasya jñānayogopāyatvaṃ pratipāditam. ihāpi ca – “sannyāsastu mahābāho duḥkhamāptumayogataḥ” (5.6) “yoginaḥ karma kurvanti saṅgaṃ tyaktvātmaśuddhaye” (5.11) “yajño dānaṃ tapaścaiva pāvanāni manīṣiṇām” (18.5) ityādi pratipādayiṣyati. nanu ca “abhayaṃ sarvabhūtebhyo dattvā naiṣkarmyamācaret” (aśvamedhaparva 46.18) ityādau kartavyakarmasannyāsādapi naiṣkarmyaprāptiṃ darśayati. loke ca karmaṇām anārambhāt naiṣkarmyam iti prasiddhataram. ataśca naiṣkarmyārthinaḥ kiṃ karmārambheṇa? iti prāptam. ata āha – na ca sannyasanādeveti. nāpi sannyasanādeva kevalāt karmaparityāgamātrādeva jñānarahitāt siddhiṃ naiṣkarmyalakṣaṇāṃ jñānayogena niṣṭhāṃ, samadhigacchati na prāpnoti.

From the statement 'not by abstaining from actions does a man attain freedom from action', it is understood conversely that he attains freedom from action by undertaking them. Why does one not attain freedom from action by abstaining from actions? It is said: because undertaking action is the means to freedom from action, and the goal is not reached without the means. That the yoga of action is the means to the yoga of knowledge, which is freedom from action, is set forth in the śruti and here. In the śruti, in 'brāhmaṇas seek to know it through the study of the Veda, through sacrifice' (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4.4.22) and so on, the yoga of action is set forth as the means to the yoga of knowledge, as the means of knowing the world of the Self which is to be known. And here too he will set it forth: 'renunciation, mighty-armed one, is hard to attain without yoga' (5.6), 'yogins perform action, giving up attachment, for the purification of the self' (5.11), 'sacrifice, giving and austerity are purifiers of the wise' (18.5) and so on. But surely passages such as 'having granted fearlessness to all beings, let him practise freedom from action' (Aśvamedha Parva 46.18) show that freedom from action is attained even by renouncing obligatory actions; and in the world it is better known that freedom from action comes from not undertaking actions. So one might conclude: what need has one who seeks freedom from action of undertaking action? Therefore he says 'nor by mere renunciation': not by renunciation alone, mere giving up of action without knowledge, does one attain perfection — the steadfastness of the yoga of knowledge, which is freedom from action.

'Beings arise from food…' Beings arise, are born, visibly from food that is eaten and transformed into blood and semen. Food is produced from rain. Rain comes from sacrifice, according to the smṛti: 'the oblation rightly offered in the fire reaches the sun; from the sun comes rain, from rain food, and from that creatures' (Manu 3.76). Sacrifice here is the unseen potency (apūrva), and that sacrifice is born of action: the activity of the priests and the sacrificer is action, and from it arises this sacrifice, this apūrva.

word by word · translation

indriyāṇi the senses manaḥ the mind buddhiḥ the understanding asya its adhiṣṭhānam seat ucyate are said to be etaiḥ through these vimohayati deludes eṣaḥ this jñānam knowledge āvṛtya veiling dehinam the embodied one

The senses, the mind and the understanding are said to be its seat; through them it clouds knowledge and deludes the embodied one.

bhāṣya

kimadhiṣṭhānaḥ punaḥ kāmaḥ jñānasya āvaraṇatvena vairī sarvasya lokasya iti apekṣāyām āha. jñāte hi śatroḥ adhiṣṭhāne sukhena śatrunibarhaṇaṃ kartuṃ śakyate iti–

Where does desire reside, that it is the enemy of the whole world by covering knowledge? Answering this, he says — for when the enemy's stronghold is known, the enemy can easily be destroyed:

indriyāṇīti :– indriyāṇi, manaḥ buddhiḥ ca asya kāmasya, adhiṣṭhānam āśrayaḥ ucyate. eteḥ indriyādibhiḥ āśrayaiḥ, vimohayati vividhaṃ, mohayati, eṣaḥ kāmaḥ jñānam, āvṛtya ācchādya, dehinaṃ śarīriṇam. yataḥ evam.

'The senses, the mind and the understanding…' The senses, the mind and the understanding are said to be the seat, the stronghold, of this desire. Through these seats, the senses and the rest, this desire deludes the embodied one in many ways, having covered, veiled, knowledge. Since this is so —

|| 41 ||

word by word · translation

tasmāt therefore tvam you indriyāṇi the senses ādau first niyamya controlling bharata-ṛṣabha O bull of the Bharatas pāpmānam sinful thing prajahihi cast off enam this jñāna-vijñāna-nāśanam the destroyer of knowledge and realisation

Therefore, O best of the Bhāratas, first restrain the senses, and then slay this sinful thing, the destroyer of knowledge and realisation.

bhāṣya

tasmāditi ḥ– tasmāt, tvam, indriyāṇi, ādau pūrvaṃ, niyamya vaśīkṛtya, bharatarṣabha, pāpmānaṃ pāpācāraṃ, kāmaṃ, prajahihi parityaja. enaṃ vairiṇaṃ, jñānavijñānanāśanam jñānaṃ śāstrataḥ ācāryataḥ ca ātmādīnām avabodhaḥ. vijñānaṃ viśeṣataḥ tadarthānubhavaḥ, tayoḥ jñānavijñānayoḥ śreyaḥprāptihetvoḥ nāśanaṃ, prajahihi ātmanaḥ parityaja ityarthaḥ.

'Therefore, O best of the Bhāratas, first restrain the senses…' Therefore first, before anything else, restrain the senses, bring them under control, best of the Bhāratas, and slay this sinful thing, desire, of sinful conduct — give it up, this enemy, the destroyer of knowledge and realisation. Knowledge is the understanding of the Self and the rest gained from scripture and teacher; realisation is the particular experience of what it means. Give up the destroyer of these two, which are the causes of attaining the highest good — that is the meaning.

|| 42 ||

word by word · translation

indriyāṇi the senses parāṇi high āhuḥ they say indriyebhyaḥ than the senses param higher manaḥ the mind manasaḥ than the mind tu but parā higher buddhiḥ the understanding yaḥ who buddheḥ than the understanding parataḥ beyond tu but saḥ is he

The senses, they say, are high; higher than the senses is the mind; higher than the mind is the understanding; and what is higher than the understanding is He.

bhāṣya

indriyāṇyādau niyamya kāmaṃ śatruṃ jahihītyuktaṃ. tatra kimāśrayaḥ kāmaṃ jahyādityucyate –

It has been said: first restrain the senses, then slay the enemy, desire. Resting on what should one slay desire? It is said:

indriyāṇīti :– indriyāṇi śrotrādīni pañca dehaṃ sthūlaṃ bāhyaṃ paricchinnaṃ ca apekṣya. saukṣmyāntarasthitavyāpitvādi apekṣayā, parāṇi prakṛṣṭāni āhuḥ paṇḍitāḥ. tathā indriyebhyaḥ paraṃ manaḥ saṅkalpa vikalpātmakam. tathā manasaḥ tuḥ parā buddhiḥ niścayātmikā. tathā – yaḥ sarvadṛśyebhyaḥ buddhyantebhyaḥ abhyantaraḥ yaṃ dehinam indriyādibhiḥ āśrayaiḥ yuktaḥ kāmaḥ jñānāvaraṇadvāreṇa mohayati iti uktaṃ saḥ buddheḥ, draṣṭā paraḥ ātmā.

'The senses, they say, are high…' The wise say that the five senses, hearing and the rest, are high, superior, compared with the body, which is gross, external and limited, by reason of their subtlety, their inwardness, their pervasiveness and so on. Likewise higher than the senses is the mind, of the nature of resolving and doubting. Higher than the mind is the understanding, of the nature of certainty. And he who is within all that is seen, up to the understanding — the embodied one who, it was said, is deluded by desire, residing in the senses and the rest, by covering knowledge — he is higher than the understanding: the witness, the supreme Self.

word by word · translation

evam thus buddheḥ than the understanding param higher buddhvā knowing saṃstabhya steadying ātmānam the self ātmanā by the Self jahi slay śatrum the enemy mahā-bāho O mighty-armed kāma-rūpam in the form of desire durāsadam hard to approach

Thus knowing Him who is beyond the understanding, steadying the self by the Self, slay, mighty-armed one, the enemy in the form of desire, so hard to overcome.

bhāṣya

evamiti :– evaṃ buddheḥ param ātmānaṃ, buddhvā jñātvā, saṃstabhya samyak stambhanaṃ kṛtvā, ātmānaṃ svena eva ātmanā saṃskṛtena manasā samyak samādhāya ityarthaḥ. jahi enaṃ śatruṃ, he mahābāho, kāmarūpaṃ, durāsadam duḥkhena, āsadaḥ āsādanaṃ prāptiḥ, yasya taṃ durāsadam, durvijñeyānekaviśeṣam iti.

'Thus knowing Him who is beyond the understanding…' Thus knowing, understanding, the Self beyond the understanding, steadying — firmly settling — the self by the Self, by one's own refined mind, that is, collecting oneself completely, slay this enemy, mighty-armed one, in the form of desire, so hard to overcome — hard to approach, to reach — since its many features are hard to discern.

Thus, in the Bhagavad Gītā — the Upaniṣad, the knowledge of Brahman, the scripture of yoga, the dialogue of Śrī Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna — within the Bhīṣma Parva of the Mahābhārata, the collection of a hundred thousand verses by Vyāsa, ends the third chapter, Karma Yoga.

Thus ends the third chapter, Karma Yoga, of the commentary on the Bhagavad Gītā composed by Śrī Śaṅkara Bhagavat, wandering ascetic of the highest order and disciple of Śrī Govinda Bhagavatpāda.
