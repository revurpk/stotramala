1 | I meditate on him who holds the spear in his hand, of unusual (many-formed) eyes, rider of the peacock, six-faced, fierce, destroyer of enemies and of disease, whose banner is the cock. |
2 | Skanda; Guha (the hidden one, dweller in the cave of the heart); Ṣaṇmukha (six-faced); Phālanetrasuta (son of the one with the eye on his brow, Śiva); Prabhu (the lord); Piṅgaḷa (the tawny one); Kṛttikāsūnu (son of the Kṛttikās); Śikhivāha (whose mount is the peacock); Dviṣaḍbhuja (twelve-armed). |
3 | Dviṣaṇṇetra (twelve-eyed); Śaktidhara (bearer of the spear); Piśitāśaprabhañjana (shatterer of the flesh-eating demons); Tārakāsurasaṃhārī (slayer of the demon Tāraka); Rakṣobalavimardana (crusher of the demons’ host). |
4 | Matta, Pramatta, Unmatta (exultant, wildly exultant, frenzied in battle); Surasainyasurakṣaka (the good guardian of the gods’ army); Devasenāpati (general of the gods’ army); Prājña (the wise); Kṛpālu (the merciful); Bhaktavatsala (tender to his devotees). |
5 | Umāsuta (son of Umā); Śaktidhara (bearer of the spear); Kumāra (the youth); Krauñcadāraṇa (splitter of Mount Krauñca); Senānī (the army’s leader); Agnijanmā (born of fire); Viśākha (the branching one); Śaṅkarātmaja (Śiva’s son). |
6 | Śivasvāmī (master of Śiva, to whom he taught the secret of Om); Gaṇasvāmī (lord of the hosts); Sarvasvāmī (lord of all); Sanātana (the eternal); Anantaśakti (of endless power); Akṣobhya (unshakable); Pārvatīpriyanandana (the beloved son of Pārvatī). |
7 | Gaṅgāsuta (son of Gaṅgā); Śarodbhūta (born from the reeds); Āhūta (the invoked); Pāvakātmaja (the son of Fire); Jṛmbha, Prajṛmbha, Ujjṛmbha (the expanding, the spreading, the boundless); Kamalāsanasaṃstuta (praised by Brahmā, who sits on the lotus). |
8 | Ekavarṇa, Dvivarṇa, Trivarṇa (of one, two, three colours); Sumanohara (most charming); Caturvarṇa, Pañcavarṇa (of four, five colours); Prajāpati (lord of creatures); Ahaspati (lord of the day). |
9 | Agnigarbha (womb of fire); Śamīgarbha (born in the śamī tree); Viśvaretā (the seed of all); Surārihā (slayer of the gods’ enemies); Haridvarṇa (golden-hued); Śubhakara (maker of good); Vaṭu (the young student); Paṭuveṣabhṛt (wearer of a clever disguise). |
10 | Pūṣā (the nourisher); Gabhasti (the ray of light); Gahana (the profound); Candravarṇa (moon-coloured); Kalādhara (bearer of the arts, or of the moon’s phases); Māyādhara (master of illusion); Mahāmāyī (the great illusionist); Kaivalya (the absolute); Śaṅkarātmaja (Śiva’s son). |
11 | Viśvayoni (source of the universe); Ameyātmā (of immeasurable self); Tejonidhi (treasury of radiance); Anāmaya (free from ill); Parameṣṭhī (the supreme one); Parabrahmā (the highest Brahman); Vedagarbha (womb of the Vedas); Virāṭsuta (son of the cosmic Person). |
12 | Puḷindakanyābhartā (husband of the Pulinda chief’s daughter, Valli); Mahāsārasvatāvṛta (attended by great eloquence); Āśritākhiladātā (giver of everything to those who take refuge); Rogaghna (destroyer of disease); Roganāśana (remover of disease). |
13 | Anantamūrti (of endless forms); Ānanda (bliss); Śikhaṇḍikṛtaketana (who made the peacock his banner); Ḍambha, Paramaḍambha, Mahāḍambha (the splendid, the supremely splendid, the greatly splendid); Vṛṣākapi (the bull-ape, a name of Viṣṇu and Śiva). |
14 | Kāraṇotpattideha (whose body arises from the cause); Kāraṇānītavigraha (whose form is brought forth by a cause) — these two names are obscure, and the renderings tentative; Anīśvara (without a lord above him); Amṛta (immortal); Prāṇa (the life-breath); Prāṇāyāmaparāyaṇa (devoted to the control of breath). |
15 | Viruddhahantā (slayer of foes); Vīraghna (destroyer of the enemy’s heroes); Raktaśyāmagaḷa (red and dark of throat); Subrahmaṇya (dear to brahmins, the ‘good Brahman’); Graha (the seizer); Prīta (the pleased); Brāhmaṇya (devoted to the sacred knowledge); Brāhmaṇapriya (friend of brahmins). |
16 | Vaṃśavṛddhikara (increaser of the family line); Vedavedya (to be known through the Vedas); Akṣayaphalaprada (giver of imperishable reward). |
