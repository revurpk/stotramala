1 | Of this great mantra, the Sixteen Names of the blessed Subrahmaṇya: the seer is the venerable Agastya, the metre is Anuṣṭubh, the deity is Subrahmaṇya, and its use is in recitation for the fulfilment of my wishes. |
2 | Six-faced, peacock-borne, three-eyed, clothed in bright garments, bearing in his hands the spear, the thunderbolt, the sword, the trident, the gesture of fearlessness, the shield, the bow, the discus, the noose, the cock, the goad and the boon-giving gesture: let one always meditate on Skanda, the son of Śiva, worshipped by the gods, giver of the success one desires. |
3 | The first is Jñānaśaktyātmā (whose self is the power of knowledge), the second Skanda, the third Agnigarbha (born from fire), the fourth Bāhuleya (son of the Kṛttikās). |
4 | The fifth is called Gāṅgeya (son of Gaṅgā), the sixth Śaravaṇodbhava (born in the reed-thicket), the seventh Kārttikeya, and likewise the eighth, Kumāra. |
5 | The ninth is called Ṣaṇmukha, the tenth is remembered as Tārakāri (foe of Tāraka), the eleventh is Senānī (the leader of the army), and the twelfth is Guha. |
6 | The thirteenth is Brahmacārī (the celibate student), the fourteenth Śivateja (Śiva’s radiance), the fifteenth Krauñcadārī (splitter of Krauñca), the sixteenth Śikhivāhana (rider of the peacock). |
7 | Whoever, full of devotion, recites these sixteen names becomes equal to Bṛhaspati in intellect and to Brahmā in radiance. |
8 | One who seeks a maiden gets a maiden; one who seeks knowledge attains knowledge; one who seeks learning gets learning; one who seeks wealth gains wealth. |
9 | Whatever a mortal prays for, he is sure to get it all. |
