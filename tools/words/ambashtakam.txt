# Ambāṣṭakam — word by word. Long ornate compounds are broken only where the members are clear.
1 ceṭībhavan-nikhila-kheṭī-kadamba-taru-vāṭīṣu = in the kadamba groves where all the sky-goers’ women become her handmaids | nāki-paṭalī-koṭīra-cāru-tara-koṭī-maṇī-kiraṇa-koṭī-karambita-padā = whose feet are flecked with the countless rays of the gems on the lovely tips of the diadems of the host of gods | pāṭīra-gandhi-kuca-śāṭī = whose breast-cloth is fragrant with sandal | kavitva-paripāṭīm = a flow of poetry | aga-adhipa-sutā = the daughter of the Mountain King | ghoṭī-kulāt = than a troop of mares | adhika-dhāṭīm = swifter in its rush (describing the poetry) | udāra-mukha-vīṭī-rasena = by the juice of the betel in her gracious mouth | tanutām = may she spread
2 kūlāti-gāmi-bhaya-tūlāvali-jvalana-kīlā = a flame of fire to the cotton-heaps of fears that overflow every bank | nija-stuti-vidhā-kolāhala-kṣapita-kālā = who drives Time (Death) away with the clamour of her own praise | amarī = the goddess | kuśala-kīlāla-poṣaṇa-nabhāḥ = a cloud that nourishes the water of well-being | sthūlā kuce = broad in the breast | jaladanīlā kace = dark as a rain-cloud in the hair | kalita-līlā = playful | kadamba-vipine = in the kadamba forest | śūlāyudha-praṇati-śīlā = ever bowing to the bearer of the trident | vibhātu = may she shine | hṛdi = in the heart | śailādhirāja-tanayā = the Mountain King's daughter
3 yatra = wherever | āśayaḥ = my mind | lagati = comes to rest | tatra = there | aga-jā = the Mountain’s daughter | vasatu = may she dwell | kutra api = wherever it be | nistula-śukā = with her matchless parrot | sutrāma-kāla-mukha-satra-aśana-prakara-sutrāṇa-kāri-caraṇā = whose feet give sure protection to the host of gods (eaters of the sacrifice) headed by Indra and Yama | chatra-anila-atiraya-patra-abhirāma-guṇa-mitra-amarī-sama-vadhūḥ = (obscure) perhaps: whose mount outruns the wind, whose companions are goddesses, friends for their lovely qualities | kutrāsahan-maṇi-vicitra-ākṛtiḥ = whose form is brilliant with gems (the first member is obscure; perhaps “destroying base fear”) | sphurita-putra-ādi-dāna-nipuṇā = skilled in granting sons and the like
4 dvaipāyana-prabhṛti-śāpa-āyudha-tridiva-sopāna-dhūli-caraṇā = the dust of whose feet is a stairway to heaven for Dvaipāyana and the other sages, whose weapon is the curse | pāpa-apaha-sva-nu-jāpa-anulīna-jana-tāpa-apanoda-nipuṇā = skilled at dispelling the pain of people absorbed in repeating her sin-destroying mantra (the print has svanu, a syllable short, for the usual sva-manu, “her own mantra”) | nīpa-ālayā = dwelling among the kadamba trees | surabhi-dhūpa-alakā = her hair fragrant with incense | durita-kūpāt = from the well of sin | udañcayatu = may she lift | mām = me | rūpa-adhikā = surpassing in beauty | śikhari-bhūpāla-vaṃśa-maṇi-dīpāyitā = a jewel-lamp to the line of the Mountain King | bhagavatī = the Blessed Goddess
5 yā = she who | ālībhiḥ = with her companions | ātma-tanu-tālī sakṛt = (obscure; perhaps “clapping her own hands once”) | priyaka-pālīṣu = among the rows of priyaka (kadamba) trees | khelati = plays | bhaya-vyālī-nakulī = a mongoose to the serpent of fear | asita-cūlī-bharā = heavy with black tresses | caraṇa-dhūlī-lasat-muni-varā = by the dust of whose feet the best of sages shine | bālī-bhṛti = that bears an ear-ring | śravasi = in her ear | tālī-dalam = a palm-leaf | vahati = wears | yā = who | alīka-śobhi-tilakā = with a tilaka bright on her forehead | sā = she | alī-karotu = may she make into a bee | mama = my | kālī = Kālī | manaḥ = mind | sva-pada-nālīka-sevana-vidhau = in the service of the lotus of her feet
6 nyaṅka-ākare = a mine of foulness (the word is doubtful) | vapuṣi = on the body | kaṅka-ādi-rakta-puṣi = which feeds herons and their like with its blood | kaṅka-ādi-pakṣi-viṣaye = the portion of herons and other birds | tvam = you | kāmanām = desire | ayasi = go to | kim kāraṇam = for what reason | hṛdaya = O heart | paṅka-arim = the enemy of mire | ehi = come to | girijām = the Mountain’s daughter | śaṅkā-śilā-niśita-ṭaṅkāyamāna-pada-saṃkāśamāna-sumanaḥ-jhaṃkāri-bhṛṅga-ti = whose feet are sharp chisels to the rock of doubt, with bees humming over the flowers that shine there (the print is short here; the usual text has bhṛṅga-tatim, “a line of bees”) | mānupeta-śaśi-saṃkāśi-vaktra-kamalām = whose lotus face is like the moon (usual text: aṅka-anupeta-śaśi, “a moon without its mark”)
