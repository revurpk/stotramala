54 | If you, hanging with your rear half in the sky like a heavenly elephant bending to drink, should bend slantwise to her water, clear as crystal, then your shadow gliding in her stream will make her lovely as a confluence with the Yamunā in the wrong place. |
55 | Reaching the mountain that is her source, white with snow, its rocks perfumed by the musk of the deer that rest there, sit on its peak to ease the weariness of the road; you will wear a pure beauty, like the mud tossed up by the bull of the three-eyed god. |
56 | If a forest fire, born of the rubbing of pine trunks together, should spread in the wind and scorch the tail-hairs of the yaks with its sparks, you are fit to put it out with thousands of streams of water; the riches of the great are for relieving the distress of those in trouble. |
57 | The śarabhas that leap up in rage to break their own limbs on you, leaving your path, you should scatter at once with falling storms of hail; who does not become an object of scorn when his efforts are vain? |
58 | There, bowed in devotion, circle the footprint of the half-moon-crowned god, clearly seen on the rock and ever honoured with offerings by the Siddhas; seeing it, the faithful, their sins shaken off and their bodies laid down, are made fit to attain a firm place among Śiva’s hosts. |
59 | The bamboos, filled by the wind, sound sweetly; Kinnara women, moved by love, sing the victory over Tripura; if your thunder in the caves should sound like a drum, the whole concert for Paśupati’s worship will be there. |
60 | Having passed the various sights along the slopes of the Snow Mountain, go by the Swan-gate, the Krauñca pass, the road of Paraśurāma’s fame; take it northward, beautiful in your slanting length, dark like the foot of Viṣṇu raised to bind Bali. |
61 | Going higher, you will be a guest of Kailāsa, the mirror of the heavenly women, whose base-joints were loosened by the arms of the ten-headed demon; it stands, spreading across the sky with peaks white as lilies, like the loud laughter of the three-eyed god piled up day after day. |
62 | I imagine that when you rest on its slope, glossy as split collyrium, the mountain, white as a tusk freshly cut, will have a beauty to gaze at unblinking, like a dark robe laid on the shoulder of the plough-bearer. |
63 | If Gaurī, her hand in Śambhu’s, he having put aside his serpent bracelet, should walk on foot on that pleasure-mountain, then make your body a stair, your inner waters stilled in ripples, and go before her, that she may climb the jewelled slope. |
64 | There the young women of the gods will surely use you as a shower-house, striking you with their diamond bracelets so the water pours out; if you cannot escape them, friend, caught in the heat, frighten those playful girls with harsh thunder, grating to the ear. |
65 | Taking the water of Mānasa, which bears golden lotuses, giving Airāvata for a moment the pleasure of a veil over his face, shaking the leaves of the wishing-trees like garments in your winds, enjoy that king of mountains with all the playful ways of a cloud. |
66 | Seeing her in his lap, like a lover with the Ganges as her slipped silken robe, you, who go as you please, will surely know Alakā — she who in the season bears masses of water in her lofty mansions, as a woman bears her hair strung with nets of pearls, a mass of clouds. |
