1 | I ever remember the daughter of Mātaṅga, caressing her ruby vīṇā, languid with intoxication, her speech a play of sweet words, her tender limbs like a great sapphire. |
2 | Victory to you, daughter of Mātaṅga! Victory to you, shining like a blue lotus! Victory to you, who delight in music! Victory to you, who love the playful parrot! |
3 | Victory, Mother — you who love to dwell in the Kadamba grove, in the midst of the forest of bilva trees and wishing-trees growing on the jewel-island that rises from the ocean of nectar; dear to the one clad in a hide, dear to all the worlds; whose crest is bound with a garland of nīpa flowers as you attentively begin the music; daughter of the Mountain; adorned with the glow of the crescent moon in your hair and with rows of dark, glossy curls; honoured by the worlds; whose eyes, like a bow of the flowers of Love’s play, put doubt into the mind, and who rain nectar in speech; fair with the dot of fresh gorocanā; Surāmā, Ramā; with a mole of musk on your radiant cheek that draws the humming bees, whose notes thicken the deep strings of the lute — sweet-voiced, bright; wearing earrings of palm-leaf as you play the vallakī, attended by the perfected ones; with one blue lotus-ear drawn by the sway of your eyes, drunk on divine wine; granting the fruit of every wish of the worlds, Śrīphala; whose pearl nose-ring seems to be a drop of the sweat that shines on your forehead; Kālikā, soul of the whole universe; with a rain of camphor and betel from your sweetly smiling mouth; making the gesture of knowledge, giver of every prosperity, radiant with shining hands; with a rosy lip and pure teeth bright as jasmine; bearer of the lovely vīṇā, with lips like ripe bimba fruit. |
4 | You whose neck, like a lovely conch, rises from the milk-ocean of beauty at the dawn of fresh youth, the shrine of the arts, slow of step; shining with the pure rays of divine jewels and necklaces, auspicious; with creeper-arms bright with the gleam of jewelled armlets, worshipped by yogis; adorned with sparkling bracelets that fill the quarters of the world, ornamented with grace, honoured by seekers; whose two hands rival the opening lotus at dawn, ever showing compassion, without a second; whose circle of nails, like moons, rises from fingers glowing with divine rings; bowed to by Indra; with a circle of consciousness, with shining earrings; ocean of beauty, who show the waves of the folds on a waist bent by the weight of your breasts, with garlands of stars for necklaces; holding the vīṇā, granting prosperity to your servants; bowed under the weight of golden breasts, bowed to by the three worlds; adorned with a line of dark hair like moss at the edge of the pond of your deep navel; sweet of speech; with a jingling girdle that scorns the bow-string of Love’s bow, dressed in divine jewels; whose radiant hips, shining in a ruby belt, put to shame the mountain of gold; cool as moonlight. |
5 | You whose thighs, covered in divine cloth red like fresh kiṃśuka blossoms, outshine the trunk of Indra’s elephant, red as vermilion; of boundless splendour, Śyāmalā; whose calves, born of soft, glossy sapphire, seem Love’s quiver; of lovely gait; whose toenails, like moons, shine through the touch of the dark gloss of the hair of the wives of the bowing guardians of the quarters, like the dūrvā-sprouts bearing a deer; brilliant, pure; whose lotus feet bear the lac-red glow of the crowns of Indra, Viṣṇu, Śiva, Varuṇa, Sarasvatī, Yama, Kubera, the lords of the demons, yaksas, Vāyu and Agni, bowed low — fair of lotus, Umā. |
6 | Seated on a jewelled seat, steady, on a lotus throne, a throne of jewels, attended by a pair of treasures, conch and lotus; there, accompanied by Vighneśa, Durgā, Baṭuka and Kṣetrapāla; attended by crowds of intoxicated Mātaṅga maidens; honoured by the lovely Menakā and others; surrounded by the eight Bhairavas; served by the Śaktis Vāmā and the rest; joined by the eight Śaktis beginning with Dhātrī and Lakṣmī; adorned with the circles of Mothers; worshipped by hosts of yakṣa, gandharva and siddha women; made of the five arrows; not (merely) esteemed by Love with Rati; delighted by Spring, who is full of joy; you bring supreme good to your devotees; you shine in the mind of yogis; you gleam with the power of the metres; you are worshipped by Kṛṣṇa, who delights in the arts of song; praised by Brahmā, whose mind is full of devotion; sung by the Vidyādharas with the music loved by the whole world. |
7 | You are sung by Kinnaras with a vīṇā whose sweet tone charms the ear; you are worshipped by troops of yakṣa, gandharva and siddha women; you are adored by the wives of the gods who desire all fortune; delighting yourself you play with the parrot that you caress — it recites sweet verse, it has three lines of colour at the base of its throat, soft green wings, a beak so lovely that it puts to shame the kiṃśuka flower — and holding in your lotus hands a crystal rosary, a book that is the essence of knowledge, a goad and a noose. Whoever meditates on you, from his mouth speech rises, shining, in prose and verse; whoever meditates on you as red as lac, women and men are subject to him; whoever meditates on you gold-bright, he too plays with thousands of Lakṣmīs. What cannot he attain who meditates on your dark, tender form with the crest of the moon? The ocean is his pleasure-lake, the Nandana grove his playground, the earth his throne, the goddess of speech his servant, and Fortune herself does his bidding. You are all pilgrimages, all mantras, all tantras, all yantras, all powers, all sacred seats, all principles, all knowledge, all yoga, all sound, all words, the whole universe, all initiations, all in all, everywhere: protect me, protect me, protect me, goddess; homage to you, goddess, homage to you, goddess, homage to you. |
8 | May the Mother, dark as an emerald, Mātaṅgī, drunk with delight, auspicious, who dwells in the Kadamba forest, cast on me her glance. |
