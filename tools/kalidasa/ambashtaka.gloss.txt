1 | May the daughter of the Mountain King, at whose feet the diadems of countless gods touch with sparkling rays of jewels, in whose Kadamba gardens all the sky-faring women of the perfected ones are attendants, whose bodice is scented with sandalwood, and whose betel-juice from her gracious mouth excels in speed a herd of horses, spread out the full flow of my poetry with the juice of her betel. |
2 | May Śailarāja’s daughter, who is a spark of fire to the cotton-heap of fears that cross every bound, who drives away Time (death) with the tumult of her own praise, a cloud nourishing the water of well-being, broad in the breast, dark as a rain-cloud in her hair, playing in the Kadamba forest and ever bowing to the one with the trident, shine in my heart. |
3 | May she dwell wherever my mind attaches itself — the Mountain’s daughter with the matchless parrot on her hand, whose feet protect the host of sacrificers with Indra and Yama at their head, whose vehicle is swifter than the wind, whose lovely qualities are her friends, consort of the Mothers, whose wondrous form shines like the Kaustubha, and who is skilled in granting sons and the like. |
4 | May the glorious Bhagavatī, whose feet are the stair to heaven made by the dust of the curses of sages from Dvaipāyana on, who is skilled in removing the distress of people absorbed in repeating her own mantra, who dwells in the Kadamba grove, with sweet-scented hair perfumed with incense, supremely beautiful, a jewel-lamp to the line of the Mountain King, lift me out of the pit of sin. |
5 | May Kālī, who plays with her friends in the skulls of her beloved Śiva, her palm lightly beating time; terrible to fear as a mongoose to a snake; with black tresses; on whom the great sages glitter in the dust of her feet; who wears a palm-leaf ornament in her ear with earrings and a bright mark on her forehead — make my mind a bee at the lotus of her feet. |
6 | O heart, why do you crave this body, a mine of worms, nourished by the blood of kites and the like, a feast for birds like kites? Go instead for refuge to the daughter of the Mountain, destroyer of sin, whose lotus face shines like the spotless moon and whose feet are like chisels that split the stones of doubt, while bees hum around her flowers. |
7 | May the Mother, who imitates a forest woman in her ornaments, whose voice at her throat is like a fresh gourd-lute, whose body shines like musk-paste, who has long hair and lips like the bimba fruit, whose heavy breasts are pressed by the moon-like face of Kārttikeya, and to whom hosts of gods, with Indra at their head, bow in the Kadamba forest — give me happiness here. |
8 | May the daughter of the Mountain grant me the joy of her feet — she whose necklace is a string of bright green gems, who delights deeply in Śiva, the friend of the heart, who is pledged to uphold the world, who has her lofty home in the ocean of nectar, whose braided hair is covered by bees drunk on the fragrance of flowers, and whose feet outshine even the sun. |
