% The root-class concluded: ās, śās, as “be”, lih, duh, rud, śvas; the verse that the resolute do not stray from the right path
! Perry’s āi and āu are written ai and au; the accent marks that he prints under the vocabulary forms (ániti, ásti …) are not carried. The paradigm of as “be” is given cell by cell; of ās, śās, lih, duh and rud only what he prints (the first persons and the odd forms) is given, in paragraphs. His footnotes are kept: § 424 (ādhve, and so āddhvam or ādhvam in the imperative and imperfect), § 425 (see § 414; aśāt is said to be used in the 2nd person also), § 428 (cf. §§ 244, 249; and when the final sonant aspirate of a root is followed by t or th of an ending, the whole group is made sonant, and the aspiration of the root-final is not lost but is transferred to the initial of the ending), and the footnote to the vocabulary (after ud, the initial s of sthā and stambh is dropped; thus utthātum for utsthātum).
! § 424 reads āse, āsse, āste; the second form is written with the doubled s as printed. In § 427 the 2nd singular of the imperative middle of lih is read likṣva (h before s becoming k, as the rule says); the form is blurred in the scan. § 429: Perry’s “°dos, °dat or °dīt” in the imperfect of rud are written out as arodīs, arodat and arodīt, from his rule.
! In § 428 the second singular of duh is dhokṣi and the middle second singular dhukṣe, the stem showing the old guttural (dhokṣi from dohṣi); both are read from the print.

## Lesson XL
¶ **424. Verbs. Root-class, cont’d.** ⟨ās⟩ ‘sit’ (mid.): Indic. ⟨āse⟩, ⟨āsse⟩, ⟨āste⟩; ⟨āsvahe⟩ etc.; ⟨āsmahe⟩, ⟨āddhve⟩∗, ⟨āsate⟩. Impf. ⟨āsi⟩, ⟨āsthās⟩, ⟨āsta⟩ etc. Imv. ⟨āsai⟩, ⟨āssva⟩, ⟨āstām⟩ etc. Part. ⟨āsīna⟩ (unique).
¶ **425.** The root ⟨śās⟩, ‘command’ (act.), substitutes in the weak forms with consonant-endings the weakened stem ⟨śiṣ⟩; thus, indic. sing. ⟨śāsmi⟩ etc.; du. ⟨śiṣvas⟩ etc.; but 3rd pl. ⟨śāsati⟩. Impf.: ⟨aśāsam⟩, ⟨aśās⟩, ⟨aśāt⟩∗∗; ⟨aśiṣva⟩ etc.; but 3rd pl. ⟨aśāsus⟩. Imv. 2nd sing. ⟨śādhi⟩; 3rd pl. ⟨śāsatu⟩.
¶ **426.** The extremely common root 1⟨as⟩, ‘be’ (act.), loses its vowel in weak forms, except when protected by the augment. The 2nd sing. imv. is ⟨edhi⟩; in the 2nd sing. indic. one ⟨s⟩ is omitted; in the 2nd and 3rd sing. impf. ⟨ī⟩ is inserted before the ending. Thus:

¶ **427.** Roots in ⟨h⟩ (except ⟨dih⟩ and ⟨duh⟩) combine ⟨h⟩ with ⟨t⟩, ⟨th⟩ and ⟨dh⟩ into ⟨ḍh⟩, and then lengthen preceding ⟨a⟩, ⟨i⟩, ⟨u⟩; before ⟨s⟩, ⟨h⟩ becomes ⟨k⟩; in 2nd and 3rd sing. impf. act. (where the endings are dropped) the ⟨h⟩ becomes ⟨ṭ⟩. Thus, ⟨lih⟩ ‘lick’ (act. and mid.): Impf. act.: ⟨aleham⟩, ⟨aleṭ⟩, ⟨aleṭ⟩; ⟨alihva⟩, ⟨alīḍham⟩,

¶ **429.** The roots ⟨rud⟩, ‘weep’, ⟨svap⟩, ‘sleep’, ⟨an⟩, ‘breathe’, ⟨śvas⟩, ‘breathe’ (all act.), insert ⟨i⟩ before all endings beginning with a consonant, except ⟨s⟩ and ⟨t⟩ of 2nd and 3rd sing. impf., where they insert either ⟨a⟩ or ⟨ī⟩. Thus, ⟨rud⟩: Pres. indic.: ⟨rodimi⟩, ⟨rodiṣi⟩ etc., 3rd pl. ⟨rudanti⟩. Impf.: ⟨arodam⟩, ⟨arodīs⟩ or ⟨arodas⟩, ⟨arodīt⟩ or ⟨arodat⟩; ⟨arudiva⟩ etc.; 3rd pl. ⟨arudan⟩. Imv.: ⟨rodāni⟩, ⟨rudihi⟩, ⟨roditu⟩ etc. Opt.: ⟨rudyām⟩ etc. (⟨yā⟩ being mode-sign).
¶ ∗ cf. §§ 244, 249.
¶ ∗∗ When the final sonant aspirate of a root is followed by ⟨t⟩ or ⟨th⟩ of an ending, the whole group is made sonant, and the aspiration of the root-final is not lost, but is transferred to the initial of the ending.
