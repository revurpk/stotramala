## Lesson XLI
¶ **430. Verbs. Reduplicating Class.** This class forms the present-stem by prefixing a reduplication to the root.∗
¶ **431.** The rules governing the reduplication are as follows:
¶ 1. The consonant of the reduplicating syllable is in general the first consonant of the root; thus, ⟨dā⟩, ⟨dadā⟩. But, (*a*) a non-aspirate is substituted for an aspirate; and (*b*) a palatal for a guttural
@191
¶ or ⟨h⟩; thus, ⟨dhā⟩, ⟨dadhā⟩; ⟨khid⟩, ⟨cikhid⟩; ⟨hrī⟩, ⟨jihrī⟩; (*c*) if the root begin with a sibilant followed by a non-nasal mute, the latter is repeated (with observance of *a*), not the sibilant; thus, ⟨sthā⟩, ⟨tasthā⟩.
¶ 2. A long vowel is shortened in the reduplicating syllable; and ⟨ṛ⟩ is replaced by ⟨i⟩; thus, ⟨dadā⟩ and ⟨dadhā⟩ above; ⟨bhī⟩, ⟨bibhī⟩; ⟨bhṛ⟩, ⟨bibhṛ⟩.
¶ **432.** The present-stem gunates the root-vowel in the strong forms; thus, ⟨bibhī⟩, strong ⟨bibhe⟩; ⟨bibhṛ⟩, strong ⟨bibhar⟩.
¶ **433.** The verbs of this class lose the ⟨n⟩ from the endings of the 3rd pl. in the active as well as in the middle; and in the 3d pl. impf. act. always take ⟨us⟩, before which a final radical vowel has *guṇa*; thus, ⟨abibharus⟩.
¶ **434.** Root ⟨bhṛ⟩, ‘bear, carry’. For 2nd and 3rd sing. impf. act., cf. §§ 122, 414.

¶ **436.** The root 1⟨dā⟩ is inflected in precisely the same way, but with change everywhere of ⟨dh⟩ to ⟨d⟩, except where ⟨dh⟩ belongs to the ending.
¶ **437.** The root 1⟨hā⟩, ‘quit, abandon’ (act.), drops the ⟨ā⟩ in weak forms where the ending begins with a vowel, and in the opt.; thus, indic. 3rd sing. ⟨jahāti⟩, pl. ⟨jahati⟩; impf. 3rd. sing. ⟨ajahāt⟩, pl. ⟨ajahus⟩; opt. ⟨jahyāt⟩. The 2nd sing. imv. is ⟨jahīhi⟩ or ⟨jahihi⟩. In the other weak forms before consonant-endings the stem is either ⟨jahī⟩ or ⟨jahi⟩; thus, ⟨jahīmas⟩ or ⟨jahimas⟩.
¶ **438.** 1⟨mā⟩, ‘measure’ (mid.), and 2⟨hā⟩, ‘move, go’ (mid.), form
@193
¶ ⟨mimī⟩ and ⟨jihī⟩ before consonant-endings, ⟨mim⟩ and ⟨jih⟩ before vowel-endings; thus, 3rd persons indic. ⟨mimīte⟩, ⟨mimāte⟩, ⟨mimate⟩.
¶ **439.** ⟨hu⟩ ‘pour, sacrifice’ (act. and mid.), makes the 2nd sing. imv. ⟨juhudhi⟩; 3rd persons impf. ⟨ajuhot⟩, ⟨ajuhutām⟩, ⟨ajuhavus⟩.
¶ **440.** 1. ⟨bhī⟩, ‘fear’ (act.), may shorten its vowel in weak forms; thus, ⟨bibhīmas⟩ or ⟨bibhimas⟩, ⟨bibhīyāt⟩ or ⟨bibhiyāt⟩. — 2. ⟨hrī⟩, ‘be ashamed’ (act.), changes its weak stem ⟨jihrī⟩ to ⟨jihriy⟩ before vowel-endings; thus, indic. 3rd persons ⟨jihreti⟩, ⟨jihrītas⟩, ⟨jihriyati⟩.
¶ ∗ Only a small proportion of the roots of this class retain the accent on the root-syllable in the strong forms. In the great majority, the accent is on the reduplication, both in the strong forms and in those weak forms whose endings begin with vowels.
¶ ∗ In combination with ⟨t⟩ or ⟨th⟩ of an ending, the ⟨dh⟩ of ⟨dadh⟩ does not give ⟨ddh⟩, but follows the general rule of aspirate and of surd and sonant combination; and the lost aspiration is thrown back upon the initial of the root.

E 15 | Let a Snātaka carry (wear) a garland, and an umbrella-and-shoes. | snātakaḥ srajaṃ bibhṛyāc chattropānahaṃ ca | స్నాతకుడు మాలను, గొడుగును, పాదరక్షలను ధరించాలి.
E 16 | One who takes (*part.*) roots-fruits-*or*-grain from a strange-field, is to be punished. | parakṣetrān mūlaphaladhānyāni ādadāno daṇḍyaḥ | పరుల పొలం నుండి వేర్లు, పండ్లు లేదా ధాన్యం తీసుకునేవాడు శిక్షార్హుడు.
E 17 | Let the two doorkeepers close the door. | dvāḥsthau dvāram apidhattām | ఇద్దరు ద్వారపాలకులు తలుపును మూయుదురు గాక.
E 18 | Do not neglect the teacher’s command. | guror ājñāṃ mā jahīhi | గురువు ఆజ్ఞను విస్మరించకు.
E 19 | The royal-sage, who wore much jewelry, shone (⟨vi-bhā⟩) with great-brilliancy, like the sun. | prabhūtabhūṣaṇo rājarṣir mahādyutinā sūrya iva vyabhāt | అనేక ఆభరణాలు ధరించిన రాజర్షి సూర్యుని వలె గొప్ప కాంతితో ప్రకాశించాడు.
E 20 | The scholars who-have-not-learned-*their*-lessons are ashamed before their teacher (*acc. or gen.*). | anadhītapāṭhāḥ śiṣyā gurūn jihrīyanti | పాఠాలు నేర్చుకోని శిష్యులు గురువు ముందు సిగ్గుపడతారు.
