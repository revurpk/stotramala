## Lesson XVIII
¶ **215. Causative.** The chief points to be noticed in the formation of causative-stems have been given already (in Less. VII); some additional ones follow.
¶ **216.** Most roots in ⟨ā⟩ and ⟨ṛ⟩ add ⟨p⟩ before the conjugation-sign; thus, ⟨dāpayati⟩ from 1 ⟨dā⟩; ⟨dhāpayati⟩; ⟨gāpayati⟩; ⟨arpayati⟩ from ⟨ṛ⟩. ⟨śrā⟩ makes ⟨śrapayati⟩; ⟨jñā⟩ and ⟨snā⟩, sometimes ⟨jñāpayati⟩, etc., sometimes ⟨jñapayati⟩, etc. 1 ⟨pā⟩, ‘drink’, makes ⟨pāyayati⟩ (as though from ⟨pī⟩). A few roots in ⟨i⟩ and ⟨ī⟩ take the same ⟨p⟩, with various irregularities; thus, ⟨adhyāpayati⟩ from ⟨adhi-i⟩.
¶ **217.** Medial or initial ⟨a⟩ in a light syllable is commonly lengthened, but sometimes remains unchanged. Thus, ⟨pat⟩, ⟨pātayati⟩; ⟨kam⟩, ⟨kāmayate⟩; ⟨cam⟩, ⟨cāmayati⟩. But most roots in ⟨am⟩, and ⟨jan⟩, ⟨tvar⟩, ⟨prath⟩, ⟨vyath⟩, with other rarer ones, generally keep the ⟨a⟩ short; thus, ⟨gam⟩, ⟨gamayati⟩.
@94
¶ **218.** Final vowels take *vṛddhi* before ⟨aya⟩; thus, ⟨bhū⟩, ⟨bhāvayati⟩; ⟨kṛ⟩, ⟨kārayati⟩.
¶ **219.** Some verbs of causative meaning are by formation denominatives; thus ⟨pālayati⟩, ‘protect’, called causative to 2 ⟨pā⟩; ⟨prīṇayati⟩, to ⟨prī⟩; ⟨bhīṣayati⟩, to ⟨bhī⟩; ⟨ghātayati⟩, to ⟨han⟩.
¶ **220.** For the passive of causatives, see Less. X.
¶ **221.** The causatives of intransitive verbs are transitive. The causatives of transitive verbs are construed sometimes (*a*) with two accusatives, sometimes (*b*) with an acc. of the object and an instrumental of the agent. Thus, “he causes the birds to eat the cakes” may be rendered either (*a*): ⟨vihagān piṇḍān khādayati⟩, or (*b*) ⟨vihagaiḥ⟩ pi° khā° (an abbreviation for ⟨vihagaiḥ piṇḍān khādayati⟩).
¶ **222. Participles.** The general participial endings are ⟨ant⟩ (weak form ⟨at⟩) for the active, and ⟨āna⟩ for the middle. But after a tense-stem in ⟨a⟩, the active suffix is virtually ⟨nt⟩, one of the two ⟨a⟩’s being lost; and the middle suffix is ⟨māna⟩ (except ⟨āna⟩ sometimes in causative forms). Thus, ⟨bhavant⟩, ⟨tudant⟩, ⟨dīvyant⟩, ⟨corayant⟩; ⟨bhavamāna⟩, etc. For the declension of the participles in ⟨ant⟩ see below, Less. XXIII.
¶ **223. Pronoun of the First Person.** The pronominal declension exhibits some striking peculiarities which are not easily explained. The pronoun of the first person is declined thus:
T  | Sing. | Dual. | Plural.
R N. | ⟨aham⟩ | ⟨āvām⟩ | ⟨vayam⟩
R A. | ⟨mām⟩, ⟨mā⟩ | ⟨āvām⟩, ⟨nau⟩ | ⟨asmān⟩, ⟨nas⟩
R I. | ⟨mayā⟩ | ⟨āvābhyām⟩ | ⟨asmābhis⟩
R D. | ⟨mahyam⟩, ⟨me⟩ | „ , ⟨nau⟩ | ⟨asmabhyam⟩, ⟨nas⟩
R Ab. | ⟨mat⟩ | „ | ⟨asmat⟩

@95
¶ **224.** The forms ⟨mā⟩, ⟨me⟩, ⟨nau⟩, ⟨nas⟩ are enclitic, and are never used at the beginning of a sentence, or before the particles ⟨ca⟩, ⟨eva⟩, ⟨vā⟩.
¶ **225.** In pronouns of the first and second persons the plural is often used for the singular. Pronouns (and other words as well) show in Sanskrit a curious tendency to agree in form with the predicate rather than with the subject to which they refer.
