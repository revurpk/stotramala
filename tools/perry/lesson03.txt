## Lesson III
¶ **107. Verbs. Accented ⟨a⟩-class.** Roots of this class form their present-stem by adding an accented ⟨a⟩ to the root, which is not gunated. The inflection of these stems is precisely like that of stems belonging to the preceding class, except as to the position of the accent; thus, ⟨kṣip⟩, present-stem ⟨kṣipa⟩, pres. ind. ⟨kṣipāmi⟩, ⟨kṣipasi⟩, ⟨kṣipati⟩, etc.
¶ **108.** Several roots in ⟨ṛ⟩ of this class (by the Hindus written with ⟨ṝ⟩) form stems in ⟨ira⟩; e. g., ⟨kṛ⟩, ‘strew’, ⟨kirati⟩. The roots in ⟨i⟩ and ⟨u⟩ and ⟨ū⟩ change those vowels into ⟨iy⟩ and ⟨uv⟩, respectively, before the class-sign; thus, ⟨kṣi⟩, ⟨kṣiyati⟩; ⟨su⟩, ⟨suvati⟩; ⟨dhū⟩, ⟨dhuvati⟩.
¶ **109.** For the root ⟨iṣ⟩, ‘desire’, ⟨ich⟩ is regarded as a substitute in the present-stem; thus, ⟨icchati⟩ (§ 100, note). Likewise, ⟨ṛ⟩ makes its present ⟨ṛcchati⟩; and ⟨prach⟩, sometimes given as ⟨pṛch⟩, makes ⟨pṛcchati⟩.
¶ **110.** A number of roots following this class are strengthened in the present by a penultimate nasal; thus, ⟨sic⟩, present ind. ⟨siñcati⟩. The nasal is always assimilated in class to the following consonant; thus ⟨ñ⟩ is used before palatals, ⟨n⟩ before dentals, ⟨m⟩ before labials; and ⟨ṃ⟩ [Perry’s dotted *ṅ*, the anusvāra] before sibilants and ⟨h⟩.

¶ **112. Force of cases.** 1. The instrumental answers the questions wherewith? and whereby? and expresses accompaniment, agent, or means. 2. The dative denotes the remoter object, and direction. It is also used as *dativus commodi*; very frequently also to denote end or purpose. Sometimes (and oftenest with copula omitted) it is predicative, in the sense of ‘makes for, tends toward’. 3. The ablative answers the question whence? and very frequently denotes cause. 4. The genitive is *casus adjectivus*, denoting all kinds of belonging (e. g. *gen. subjectivus*, *objectivus*, *partitivus*). 5. The locative denotes the place where, or the time when, an action occurs. It is often used absolutely, in agreement with a participle expressed or understood, as the ablative is used in Latin and the genitive in Greek.
