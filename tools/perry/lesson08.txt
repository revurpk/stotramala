## Lesson VIII
¶ **152. Verbs, ⟨a⟩-conjugation. Present Indicative Middle.** The present indicative middle of verbs whose stems end in ⟨a⟩ is inflected as follows:
@64
T  | Singular. | Dual. | Plural.
R 1. | ⟨vade⟩ | ⟨vadāvahe⟩ | ⟨vadāmahe⟩
R 2. | ⟨vadase⟩ | ⟨vadethe⟩ | ⟨vadadhve⟩
R 3. | ⟨vadate⟩ | ⟨vadete⟩ | ⟨vadante⟩
¶ **153.** The ending of the 3rd pl. is properly ⟨ante⟩ (cf. ⟨nti⟩ for ⟨anti⟩ in the act.); before the ⟨e⟩ of the 1st sing. the stem-final is dropped. ⟨ethe⟩ and ⟨ete⟩ are hard to explain.
¶ **154.** With verbs inflected in both voices, the chief force of the middle is this, that the action is performed for the benefit of the actor himself; thus, ⟨yajati⟩ ‘he sacrifices’ (for some one else); ⟨yajate⟩ ‘he sacrifices for himself’. But many verbs are conjugated only in the middle, like the Latin and Greek deponents.
¶ **155.** The verb ⟨mṛ⟩, ‘die’, makes ⟨mriyate⟩ in the present; and ⟨jan⟩, ‘give birth’, substitutes as present mid. ⟨jāyate⟩, ‘be born’.
¶ **156. Combination of final and initial vowels.** Two simple vowels, either or both of them short or long, coalesce and form the corresponding long vowel. For the ⟨a⟩-vowels, see above, § 105. Thus: 1. ⟨i⟩ or ⟨ī⟩ + ⟨i⟩ or ⟨ī⟩ = ⟨ī⟩; e. g. ⟨gacchati iti⟩ becomes ⟨gacchatī 'ti⟩. 2. ⟨u⟩ or ⟨ū⟩ + ⟨u⟩ or ⟨ū⟩ = ⟨ū⟩; e. g. ⟨sādhu uktam⟩ becomes ⟨sādhū 'ktam⟩.∗
¶ **157.** The ⟨i⟩-vowels, the ⟨u⟩-vowels, and ⟨ṛ⟩, before a dissimilar vowel or diphthong, are regularly converted each into its own corresponding semivowel, ⟨y⟩ or ⟨v⟩ or ⟨r⟩. Thus, ⟨tiṣṭhati atra⟩ becomes ⟨tiṣṭhaty atra⟩ (four syllables); ⟨nadī atra⟩ becomes ⟨nady atra⟩; ⟨madhu atra⟩ becomes ⟨madhv atra⟩; ⟨kartṛ iha⟩ becomes ⟨kartr iha⟩.
¶ ∗ And theoretically 3. ⟨ṛ⟩ + ⟨ṛ⟩ = ⟨ṝ⟩, but probably this has no occurrence.
@65
¶ **158.** Final ⟨e⟩ and ⟨o⟩ remain unchanged before an initial *short* ⟨a⟩, but the ⟨a⟩ disappears. Thus, ⟨vane atra⟩ becomes ⟨vane 'tra⟩; ⟨bhāno atra⟩ becomes ⟨bhāno 'tra⟩. By far the commonest case of final ⟨o⟩ is where it represents final ⟨as⟩ (see § 118).
¶ **159.** The final ⟨i⟩ or ⟨u⟩-element of a diphthong is changed to its corresponding semivowel ⟨y⟩ or ⟨v⟩, before any vowel or diphthong, except when the rule of § 158 would apply. Thus, ⟨e⟩ becomes ⟨ay⟩, and ⟨ai⟩, ⟨āy⟩; ⟨o⟩ becomes ⟨av⟩, and ⟨au⟩, ⟨āv⟩. Thus, in internal combination, ⟨ne-a⟩ becomes ⟨naya⟩; ⟨bho-a⟩ becomes ⟨bhava⟩; so ⟨nai-aya⟩ yields ⟨nāy-aya⟩, and ⟨bhau-aya⟩ yields ⟨bhāv-aya⟩.
¶ **160.** In *external* combination, the resulting semivowel is in general dropped; and the resulting hiatus remains. Thus, ⟨vane iti⟩ becomes ⟨vana iti⟩ (through the intermediate stage ⟨vanay iti⟩); ⟨bhāno iti⟩ becomes ⟨bhāna iti⟩ (through ⟨bhānav iti⟩). The case of final ⟨e⟩ is by far the more frequent. See also § 164.
¶ **161.** Certain final vowels maintain themselves unchanged before any following vowel. Such are 1. ⟨ī⟩, ⟨ū⟩, and ⟨e⟩ as dual endings, both of declension and of conjugation; thus, ⟨girī iha⟩, ⟨sādhū atra⟩; ⟨phale atra⟩. 2. The final, or only, vowel of an interjection; thus, ⟨he indra⟩, ⟨he agne⟩.
