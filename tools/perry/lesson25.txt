## Lesson XXV
¶ **268. Declension. Perfect Active Participles in ⟨vāṃs⟩.** The active participles of the perfect tense-system are quite peculiar as regards the modifications of the stem. In the strong cases the suffix is ⟨vāṃs⟩, which becomes ⟨vān⟩ in the nom. sing. masc., and is shortened to ⟨van⟩ in the voc. sing. In the weakest cases the suffix is contracted into ⟨uṣ⟩; and in the middle cases it is changed to ⟨vat⟩. A union-vowel ⟨i⟩, if present in the strong and middle cases, disappears before ⟨uṣ⟩ in the weakest. Radical ⟨i⟩ and ⟨ī⟩, if preceded by *one* consonant, become ⟨y⟩ before ⟨uṣ⟩, but if preceded by more than one consonant, become ⟨iy⟩; whereas radical ⟨u⟩ always becomes ⟨uv⟩ before ⟨uṣ⟩, and radical ⟨ṛ⟩, ⟨r⟩. Thus, ⟨ninīvāṃs⟩, ⟨ninyuṣ⟩; ⟨śuśruvāṃs⟩, ⟨śuśruvuṣ⟩; ⟨cakṛvāṃs⟩, ⟨cakruṣ⟩. The feminine stem is formed with ⟨ī⟩ from the weakest stem-form; thus, ⟨ninyuṣī⟩. Examples:

¶ **269. Stems ⟨śvan⟩, ⟨yuvan⟩.** The stems ⟨śvan⟩ m., ‘dog’, and ⟨yuvan⟩ m., n., ‘young’, have as weakest stems ⟨śun⟩ and ⟨yūn⟩; in the strong and middle cases they follow ⟨rājan⟩; voc. ⟨śvan⟩, ⟨yuvan⟩. Fem. ⟨śunī⟩ and ⟨yuvati⟩.
¶ **270.** The stem ⟨maghavan⟩ m., ‘generous’ (in the later language almost exclusively a name of Indra), has as strong stem ⟨maghavān⟩, mid. ⟨-vat⟩, weakest ⟨maghon⟩. Nom. sing. ⟨maghavā⟩, voc. ⟨-van⟩. Fem. ⟨maghonī⟩.
¶ **271.** The stem ⟨ahan⟩ n., ‘day’, is used only in the strong and weakest cases, the middle, with the nom. sing., coming from ⟨ahar⟩ or ⟨ahas⟩. Thus:

¶ **272. Compounds with the root ⟨añc⟩.** The adjectives formed from this root with prepositions and other words are quite irregular. Some of them have only two stem-forms: a strong in ⟨āñc⟩ and a weak in ⟨āc⟩; while others distinguish from the middle in ⟨ac⟩ a weakest stem in ⟨c⟩, before which the ⟨a⟩ is contracted with a preceding ⟨i⟩ (⟨y⟩) or ⟨u⟩ (⟨v⟩) into ⟨ī⟩ or ⟨ū⟩. The fem. is made with ⟨ī⟩ from the weakest (or weak) stem; thus, ⟨viṣūcī⟩. The principal stems of this sort are as follows:
