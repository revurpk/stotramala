## Lesson XXX
¶ **314. Infinitive.** The later language has a single infinitive, the ending of which is ⟨tum⟩ (or ⟨itum⟩). The root takes *guṇa*, when possible.
¶ **315. The ending ⟨tum⟩ is added directly:**
¶ 1. To almost all roots ending in vowels, except those in ⟨ū⟩ and changeable ⟨ṛ⟩. Thus, ⟨pā⟩, ⟨pātum⟩; ⟨dā⟩, ⟨dātum⟩; ⟨ji⟩, ⟨jetum⟩; ⟨nī⟩, ⟨netum⟩; ⟨śru⟩, ⟨śrotum⟩; ⟨kṛ⟩, ⟨kartum⟩.
¶ 2. To a number of roots ending in consonants. As root-
@141
¶ finals, ⟨k⟩, ⟨t⟩, ⟨p⟩ and ⟨s⟩ remain unchanged before ⟨tum⟩; thus, ⟨śak⟩, ⟨śaktum⟩; ⟨man⟩, ⟨mantum⟩; ⟨āp⟩, ⟨āptum⟩; ⟨kṣip⟩, ⟨kṣeptum⟩; ⟨lup⟩, ⟨loptum⟩; ⟨śap⟩ ‘curse’, ⟨śaptum⟩; 3⟨vas⟩ ‘dwell’, ⟨vastum⟩. — Other finals are changed according to the rules given in Lesson XXVIII for the conversion of final consonants before the participial suffix ⟨ta⟩. Thus, ⟨pac⟩, ⟨paktum⟩; ⟨tyaj⟩, ⟨tyaktum⟩; ⟨dṛś⟩, ⟨draṣṭum⟩∗; ⟨spṛś⟩, ⟨spraṣṭum⟩∗; ⟨kṛṣ⟩, ⟨kraṣṭum⟩∗; ⟨prach⟩, ⟨praṣṭum⟩; ⟨yaj⟩, ⟨yaṣṭum⟩; ⟨sṛj⟩, ⟨sraṣṭum⟩∗; ⟨krudh⟩, ⟨kroddhum⟩; ⟨labh⟩, ⟨labdhum⟩; ⟨ruh⟩, ⟨roḍhum⟩; ⟨vah⟩, ⟨voḍhum⟩; ⟨dah⟩, ⟨dagdhum⟩; ⟨nah⟩, ⟨naddhum⟩. — Final ⟨d⟩ becomes ⟨t⟩, and final ⟨m⟩, ⟨n⟩; thus, ⟨ad⟩, ⟨attum⟩; ⟨vid⟩ ‘know’, ⟨vettum⟩ (also ⟨veditum⟩); ⟨gam⟩, ⟨gantum⟩.
¶ **316.** The ending ⟨tum⟩ with ⟨i⟩ (in the form ⟨itum⟩) is taken by roots in final long ⟨ū⟩ and the root ⟨śī⟩, with a few other vowel-roots; by the majority of roots in consonants; and by verbs of the secondary conjugations. Thus, ⟨bhū⟩, ⟨bhavitum⟩; ⟨śī⟩, ⟨śayitum⟩; ⟨īkṣ⟩, ⟨īkṣitum⟩; ⟨vand⟩, ⟨vanditum⟩; ⟨guh⟩, ⟨gūhitum⟩ (cf. § 101).
¶ **317.** Causatives and denominatives in ⟨aya⟩ have ⟨ayitum⟩, the root being treated as in the present; thus, ⟨cur⟩, ⟨corayitum⟩; ⟨kath⟩, ⟨kathayitum⟩; ⟨taḍ⟩, ⟨tāḍayitum⟩.
¶ **318.** Some roots in consonants insert or reject ⟨i⟩ at pleasure; thus, ⟨mṛj⟩, ⟨mārjitum⟩ or ⟨mārṣṭum⟩∗∗. The root ⟨grah⟩ makes ⟨grahītum⟩.
¶ **319.** The rules for the use of ⟨i⟩ in the infinitive agree closely with those governing its use in the formation of the ⟨s⟩-future and of the *nomen agentis* in ⟨tṛ⟩.
¶ **320. Uses of the infinitive.** The chief use of the infinitive is as equivalent to an accusative, as the object of a verb, especially of the verbs ⟨śak⟩ ‘be able’, and ⟨arh⟩ ‘be worthy’, ‘have the right
@142
¶ or power’; thus, ⟨kathayituṃ śaknoti⟩ “he is able to tell”; ⟨śrotum arhati kumāraḥ⟩ “the prince ought to hear it”. ⟨arh⟩ is often thus used with the infinitive to express a respectful request or entreaty, as in the last example. The infinitive is also often found with verbs of motion, and with those meaning ‘desire’, ‘hope’, ‘notice’, ‘know’, and the like.
¶ **321.** But often the infinitive has a case-value not accusative. Thus, a dative value: ⟨bhavati bhoktum annam⟩ “there is food to eat” i. e. “for eating”; a genitive value: ⟨samartho gantum⟩ “capable of going”. Even a construction as nominative is not unknown.
¶ **322.** In certain connections the infinitive has a *quasi*-passive force. Thus, ⟨kartum ārabdhaḥ⟩ “begun to be made”; ⟨śrotuṃ na yujyate⟩ “it is not fit to be heard.” This is especially frequent along with the passive forms of ⟨śak⟩; thus, ⟨tyaktuṃ na śaknoti⟩ “he cannot abandon”, but ⟨tyaktuṃ na śakyate⟩ “he cannot be abandoned”; ⟨narau śakyāv ānetum⟩ “the two men can be brought hither.”
¶ **323. Future Passive Participle, or Gerundive.** Certain derivative adjectives, mostly secondary, have acquired a value quite like that of the Latin gerundive; thus, ⟨kārya⟩ (from ⟨kṛ⟩) ‘to be done’, *faciendum*. They may be made from every verb. The ordinary suffixes are three: ⟨ya⟩, ⟨tavya⟩, and ⟨anīya⟩.
¶ **324. A. Suffix ⟨ya⟩.**∗ *a.* Before this suffix final radical ⟨ā⟩ becomes ⟨e⟩; thus, from ⟨dā⟩, ⟨deya⟩; ⟨gā⟩, ⟨geya⟩. *b.* Other final vowels sometimes remain unchanged, sometimes have the *guṇa* or even the *vṛddhi*-strengthening; and ⟨e⟩ often, and ⟨o⟩ always, are treated before ⟨ya⟩ as before a vowel; thus, from ⟨ji⟩, ⟨jeya⟩ and ⟨jayya⟩; from ⟨bhī⟩, ⟨bheya⟩ and ⟨bhayya⟩; from ⟨śru⟩, ⟨śravya⟩ and ⟨śrāvya⟩; from ⟨kṛ⟩, ⟨kārya⟩; from ⟨dhū⟩, ⟨dhūya⟩; from ⟨bhū⟩, ⟨bhāvya⟩. — *c.* In a few instances, a short vowel adds ⟨t⟩ before the suffix; thus, ⟨itya⟩ (⟨i⟩), ⟨śrutya⟩ (⟨śru⟩), ⟨kṛtya⟩ (⟨kṛ⟩). *d.* Medial ⟨a⟩ remains

¶ unchanged in one class of words, and is lengthened in another class; thus, ⟨dabhya⟩, ⟨vandya⟩, ⟨sadya⟩, but ⟨mādya⟩ (⟨mad⟩), ⟨vācya⟩ (⟨vac⟩). *e.* Initial or medial *i*, *u*, and *ṛ*-vowels are sometimes unchanged, sometimes have the *guṇa*-strengthening; thus, ⟨īḍya⟩, ⟨guhya⟩, ⟨tṛdya⟩; ⟨vedya⟩, ⟨yodhya⟩, ⟨bodhya⟩. *f.* The root ⟨śās⟩ makes ⟨śiṣya⟩. A form ⟨vadhya⟩ (from the defective root ⟨vadh⟩) is assigned to ⟨han⟩. ⟨ā-labh⟩ makes ⟨ālabhya⟩ and ⟨ālambhya⟩. *g.* Causatives and denominatives in ⟨aya⟩ are treated as in the present, but omit the syllables ⟨aya⟩; thus, ⟨cur⟩, ⟨corya⟩.
¶ **325. B. Suffix ⟨tavya⟩.** This is a secondary adjective derivative from the infinitival noun in ⟨tu⟩. Hence, both as regards the form of root and the use or omission of ⟨i⟩, the rules are the same as for the formation of the infinitive; thus, ⟨vaktavya⟩, ⟨labdhavya⟩, ⟨vanditavya⟩, ⟨śayitavya⟩ (⟨śī⟩).
¶ **326. C. Suffix ⟨anīya⟩ [⟨aṇīya⟩].** Generally radical vowels will be found gunated before this suffix; causatives and denominatives in ⟨aya⟩ are treated as in the present-system, without the syllables ⟨aya⟩; thus, ⟨dānīya⟩ (⟨dā⟩), ⟨gānīya⟩ (⟨gā⟩), ⟨śravaṇīya⟩ (⟨śru⟩), ⟨bodhanīya⟩, ⟨coraṇīya⟩, ⟨gūhanīya⟩ (⟨gūhayati⟩).
¶ **327.** The gerundives in ⟨tavya⟩ are common in the impersonal passive construction described in Lesson X, and not seldom have a purely future sense; thus, ⟨tena tvayā sukhinā bhavitavyam⟩ “with that thou shalt be happy”.

E 20 | Who is able to stop the mighty wind? | mahāntaṃ vāyuṃ nirodhuṃ kaḥ samarthaḥ | బలమైన గాలిని ఆపడానికి ఎవరు సమర్థుడు?
E 21 | The gentlemen (*use* ⟨bhavant⟩) are to read this letter. | bhavadbhir idaṃ pattraṃ paṭhitavyam | అయ్యలు ఈ ఉత్తరం చదవాలి.
E 22 | Having finished the Veda, he went on to study the other sciences. | vedaṃ samāpyānyāni śāstrāṇy adhyetuṃ pravṛttaḥ | వేదం పూర్తిచేసి ఇతర శాస్త్రాలను అధ్యయనం చేయడానికి ప్రవృత్తుడయ్యాడు.
