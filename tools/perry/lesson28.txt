## Lesson XXVIII
¶ **293. Past Passive Participle, cont’d. B. With suffix ⟨ta⟩.** I. **Without union-vowel ⟨i⟩.** Much more commonly this participle is made by adding the suffix ⟨ta⟩ to the bare root; thus, ⟨jñāta⟩ from ⟨jñā⟩; ⟨jita⟩ from ⟨ji⟩; ⟨kṣipta⟩ from ⟨kṣip⟩; ⟨hṛta⟩ from ⟨hṛ⟩ (or ⟨hvā⟩); ⟨vṛtta⟩ from ⟨vṛt⟩.
@133
¶ **294.** If the root end in a consonant other than ⟨k⟩, ⟨t⟩, ⟨p⟩, ⟨s⟩, the ordinary rules of euphonic combination apply as follows:
¶ 1. Final ⟨c⟩ and ⟨j⟩ become ⟨k⟩; thus, ⟨sikta⟩ from ⟨sic⟩; ⟨yukta⟩ from ⟨yuj⟩; ⟨tyakta⟩ from ⟨tyaj⟩.
¶ 2. Final ⟨ś⟩ becomes ⟨ṣ⟩, after which, as also after radical final ⟨ṣ⟩, ⟨t⟩ becomes ⟨ṭ⟩; thus, ⟨dṛṣṭa⟩ from ⟨dṛś⟩; ⟨dviṣṭa⟩ from ⟨dviṣ⟩. ⟨sṛṣṭa⟩ and ⟨mṛṣṭa⟩ are made from ⟨sṛj⟩ and ⟨mṛj⟩, and ⟨iṣṭa⟩ from ⟨yaj⟩, contrary to 1. ⟨prach⟩ makes ⟨pṛṣṭa⟩; and ⟨takṣ⟩, ⟨taṣṭa⟩.
¶ 3. Final ⟨dh⟩ becomes ⟨d⟩, and ⟨bh⟩, ⟨b⟩; and the following ⟨t⟩ becomes ⟨dh⟩; thus, ⟨vṛddha⟩ from ⟨vṛdh⟩, ⟨labdha⟩ from ⟨labh⟩.
¶ 4. Final ⟨h⟩ is treated in various ways, according to its historical value. *a.* Sometimes ⟨h⟩ combines with ⟨t⟩ to form ⟨ḍh⟩, before which short vowels (except ⟨ṛ⟩) are lengthened; thus, ⟨gāḍha⟩ from ⟨gāh⟩, ⟨līḍha⟩ from ⟨lih⟩, ⟨rūḍha⟩ from ⟨ruh⟩; ⟨mūḍha⟩ from ⟨muh⟩; but ⟨dṛḍha⟩ from ⟨dṛh⟩. ⟨sah⟩ forms ⟨soḍha⟩. *b.* Where ⟨h⟩ represents original ⟨gh⟩, the combination is ⟨gdh⟩; thus, ⟨dagdha⟩ from ⟨dah⟩; ⟨digdha⟩ from ⟨dih⟩; ⟨dugdha⟩ from ⟨duh⟩. The root ⟨muh⟩ forms also ⟨mugdha⟩. *c.* ⟨nah⟩, where ⟨h⟩ represents original ⟨dh⟩, makes ⟨naddha⟩.
¶ **295.** The root before ⟨ta⟩ usually has its weakest form, if there is any where in the verbal system a distinction of strong and weak forms. Thus: 1. A penultimate nasal is dropped; e. g., ⟨akta⟩ from ⟨añj⟩; ⟨baddha⟩ from ⟨bandh⟩; ⟨srasta⟩ from ⟨sraṃs⟩ (or ⟨sras⟩); ⟨śasta⟩ from ⟨śaṃs⟩. 2. Roots which are abbreviated in the weak forms of the perfect suffer the same abbreviation here; e. g., ⟨ukta⟩ from ⟨vac⟩, ⟨upta⟩ from ⟨vap⟩, ⟨supta⟩ from ⟨svap⟩, ⟨ūḍha⟩ from ⟨vah⟩; ⟨iṣṭa⟩ from ⟨yaj⟩ (the same form from ⟨iṣ⟩); ⟨viddha⟩ from ⟨vyadh⟩; ⟨pṛṣṭa⟩ from ⟨prach⟩. 3. Final ⟨ā⟩ is weakened to ⟨ī⟩ in ⟨gīta⟩ from ⟨gā⟩ ‘sing’, ⟨pīta⟩ from 1⟨pā⟩ ‘drink’; to ⟨i⟩ in ⟨sthita⟩ from ⟨sthā⟩, ⟨hita⟩ from ⟨dhā⟩ ‘put’ (with ⟨dh⟩ also changed to ⟨h⟩), ⟨mita⟩ from
@134
¶ ⟨mā⟩ ‘measure’, and a few others. 4. A final ⟨m⟩ is lost after ⟨a⟩ in ⟨gata⟩, ⟨yata⟩, ⟨nata⟩, ⟨rata⟩ (from ⟨gam⟩ etc.); and likewise final ⟨n⟩ in ⟨khata⟩, ⟨tata⟩, ⟨mata⟩, ⟨vata⟩, ⟨hata⟩ (from ⟨khan⟩ etc.). 5. Isolated cases are ⟨śiṣṭa⟩ from ⟨śās⟩; ⟨dyūta⟩ from ⟨div⟩ ‘play’.
¶ **296.** More irregular are the following:
¶ 1. Some roots in ⟨am⟩ make participles in ⟨ānta⟩; thus, ⟨kānta⟩, ⟨krānta⟩, ⟨cānta⟩, ⟨tānta⟩, ⟨dānta⟩, ⟨śānta⟩, ⟨śrānta⟩, from ⟨kam⟩ etc.
¶ 2. ⟨jan⟩, ⟨khan⟩, and ⟨san⟩ make ⟨jāta⟩ etc.
¶ 3. The root 1⟨dā⟩, ‘give’, forms ⟨datta⟩ (from the derivative form ⟨dad⟩). The contracted form ⟨tta⟩ is widely found in composition, especially with prepositions; thus, ⟨pradatta⟩ or ⟨pratta⟩, ⟨nidatta⟩ or ⟨nītta⟩, etc.
¶ **297.** II. **With union-vowel ⟨i⟩.** The suffix with ⟨i⟩, or in the form ⟨ita⟩, is regularly used with the derivative verb-stems in secondary conjugation, also often with roots of derivative character (like ⟨jīv⟩, ⟨hiṃs⟩), and not infrequently with original roots.
¶ **298.** When ⟨ita⟩ is added to causative and denominative verb-stems the syllables ⟨aya⟩ are dropped; thus, ⟨cur⟩, pass. part. ⟨corita⟩; ⟨gaṇaya⟩, ⟨gaṇita⟩; ⟨taḍ⟩, ⟨tāḍita⟩; ⟨mṛ⟩, caus. ⟨mārayati⟩, caus. pass. part. ⟨mārita⟩; ⟨han⟩, caus. ⟨ghātayati⟩, ⟨ghātita⟩.
¶ **299.** Among the original roots taking ⟨ita⟩ may be noticed the following:
¶ ⟨pat⟩ ‘fall’, ⟨patita⟩; ⟨kup⟩, ⟨kupita⟩; ⟨vas⟩ ‘dwell’, ⟨uṣita⟩; ⟨śvas⟩, ⟨śvasita⟩; ⟨tṛṣ⟩ ‘thirst’, ⟨tṛṣita⟩; ⟨likh⟩, ⟨likhita⟩; ⟨īkṣ⟩, ⟨īkṣita⟩; ⟨vad⟩, ⟨udita⟩. ⟨grah⟩ makes ⟨gṛhīta⟩; ⟨śī⟩ ‘lie’ makes ⟨śayita⟩.
¶ **300.** A few roots form this participle either with or without the auxiliary ⟨i⟩; thus, ⟨matta⟩ and ⟨madita⟩ from ⟨mad⟩.
¶ **301.** The grammarians reckon as participles of the ⟨na⟩-formation a few derivative adjectives, coming from roots which do not make a regular participle; such are ⟨kṣāma⟩ ‘burnt’ (⟨kṣā⟩); ⟨kṛśa⟩ ‘thin’, ‘haggard’ (⟨kṛś⟩); ⟨pakva⟩ ‘ripe’ (⟨pac⟩); ⟨śuṣka⟩ ‘dry’ (⟨śuṣ⟩); ⟨phulla⟩ ‘expanded’ (⟨phull⟩).
@135
¶ **302. Past Active Participle in ⟨tavant⟩ (or ⟨navant⟩).** From the past pass. part. is made, by adding the possessive suffix ⟨vant⟩ (f. ⟨vatī⟩), a secondary derivative having the meaning and construction of a perfect active participle; thus, ⟨kṛtavant⟩, ⟨pratipannavant⟩.
¶ **303.** This participle is almost always used predicatively, and generally without expressed copula, i. e., with the value of a personal perfect-form. Thus, ⟨māṃ na kaścid dṛṣṭavān⟩ “no one has seen me”; or, with copula, ⟨mahatkṛcchraṃ prāptavaty asi⟩ “thou (fem.) hast come into great misery”. This participle comes to be made even from intransitives; thus, ⟨sā gatavatī⟩ “she has gone”.
