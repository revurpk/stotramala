## Lesson XXXIX
¶ **414. Verbs. Root-class, cont’d. Roots ending in consonants.** The endings of the 2nd and 3rd sing. impf. act. are generally dropped, and the resulting root-final treated according to the usual rules for finals. Cf. §§ 239, 242. But a root ending in a dental mute sometimes drops this final mute instead of the added ⟨s⟩ in the second person; and, on the other hand, a root or stem ending in ⟨s⟩ sometimes drops this ⟨s⟩ instead of the added ⟨t⟩ in the third person: in either case establishing the ordinary relation of ⟨s⟩ and ⟨t⟩ in the second and third persons.
¶ **415.** Roots in ⟨c⟩ and ⟨j⟩ substitute ⟨k⟩ for those letters before ⟨t⟩, ⟨th⟩ and ⟨s⟩ (which then becomes ⟨ṣ⟩); and ⟨g⟩ before ⟨dh⟩. Thus, ⟨vac⟩ ‘speak’: ⟨vacmi⟩, ⟨vakṣi⟩, ⟨vakti⟩ (only these three forms used).

¶ **422.** 1. ⟨īś⟩, ‘rule’ (mid.), inserts ⟨i⟩ before endings beginning with ⟨s⟩ and ⟨dh⟩; thus, 2nd sing. ⟨īśiṣe⟩. — 2. ⟨vaś⟩, ‘wish’ (act.), is in weak forms contracted to ⟨uś⟩; thus, 3rd. pl. ⟨uśanti⟩.

E 17 | Why dost thou consider (⟨vid⟩) me a Çūdra, *though* knowing (⟨jñā⟩ *ger.*) my learning-and-conduct? | śrutaṃ ca vṛttaṃ ca mama jñātvāpi māṃ śūdraṃ kasmāt tvaṃ vetsi | నా విద్యను, నడవడిని తెలిసికూడా నన్ను నువ్వు ఎందుకు శూద్రునిగా భావిస్తున్నావు?
E 18 | Do not hate the sons-of-Pāṇḍu. | pāṇḍavān na dviṣyāḥ | పాండవులను ద్వేషించకు.
E 19 | The women *whose*-sons-*were*-dead, having lamented greatly, wiped the tears from their eyes. | hataputrā nāryo bahu vilapya cakṣurbhyo 'śrūṇy apāmārjan | కొడుకులు చనిపోయిన స్త్రీలు చాలా విలపించి తమ కళ్ళ నుండి కన్నీళ్ళు తుడుచుకున్నారు.
E 20 | Thou, O Lord, rulest over bipeds and quadrupeds (*gen.*). | he īśvara tvaṃ dvipadāṃ catuṣpadāṃ ceśiṣe | ఓ ప్రభూ, నువ్వు ద్విపాదులను, చతుష్పాదులను పాలిస్తావు.
