## Lesson XLIV
¶ **475. Verbs. Future-System (and Conditional).** The verb has two futures: I. The simple, or *s*-future, which is by far the older, and much more common, than the other; and II. the periphrastic future.
¶ **476. I. Simple Future.** This tense contains an indicative mode and a participle, active and middle. It may be made from all verbs. The tense-sign is the syllable ⟨sya⟩, added to the root either directly, or by the union-vowel ⟨i⟩ (in the latter case becoming ⟨iṣya⟩). The root has the *guṇa*-strengthening when possible; and some roots with medial ⟨ṛ⟩ gunate with ⟨r⟩ instead of ⟨ar⟩. The inflection is precisely like that of the present indicative of a verb of the *a*-conjugation; thus, from ⟨bhū⟩, ⟨bhaviṣyati⟩, ⟨bhaviṣyate⟩.
¶ **477.** When ⟨i⟩ is not taken, final radical consonants suffer the same changes before ⟨sya⟩ as before ⟨s⟩ in the inflection of the root-class or reduplicating or nasal class. Thus, from ⟨duh⟩, ⟨dhokṣyati⟩; ⟨muc⟩, ⟨mokṣyati⟩; ⟨bhid⟩, ⟨bhetsyati⟩; ⟨rudh⟩, ⟨rotsyati⟩; ⟨nam⟩, ⟨naṃsyati⟩; ⟨lih⟩, ⟨lekṣyati⟩; ⟨dviṣ⟩, ⟨dvekṣyati⟩; ⟨dṛś⟩, ⟨drakṣyati⟩. The root ⟨vas⟩ ‘dwell’, makes ⟨vatsyati⟩.
¶ **478.** 1. Most roots ending in vowels reject ⟨i⟩; thus, ⟨dā⟩, ⟨dāsyati⟩; ⟨gā⟩, ⟨gāsyati⟩; ⟨ji⟩, ⟨jeṣyati⟩; ⟨śru⟩, ⟨śroṣyati⟩. 2. But all roots in ⟨ṛ⟩ take ⟨i⟩; thus, ⟨kṛ⟩, ⟨kariṣyati⟩; ⟨tṛ⟩, ⟨tariṣyati⟩; and also the roots ⟨śrī⟩ (⟨śrayiṣyati⟩) and ⟨bhū⟩ (⟨bhaviṣyati⟩). 3. ⟨grah⟩ makes ⟨grahīṣyati⟩.
@206
¶ **479.** In general, the verbs which take ⟨i⟩ in the infinitive and periphrastic future (see below), take it also in this tense. But the accordance is far from complete; and these parts should be learned, as a matter of usage, for any given verb.
¶ **480.** Stems of causative inflection, and denominatives in ⟨aya⟩, make their future-stems in ⟨ayiṣya⟩; thus, ⟨cur⟩, ⟨corayiṣyati⟩.
¶ **481. Participle.** The participles, act. and mid., are made from the future-stem precisely as from the present-stem; thus, ⟨dā⟩, ⟨dāsyant⟩ (f. ⟨dāsyantī⟩), ⟨dāsyamāna⟩; ⟨kṛ⟩, ⟨kariṣyant⟩, ⟨kariṣyamāṇa⟩. Cf. §§ 260, 262.
¶ **482. Conditional.** A tense called the conditional (indic. only) is made from the stem of the simple future precisely as the imperfect is made from the present-stem, and similarly inflected. Thus, ⟨adāsyam⟩, ⟨akariṣyam⟩; ⟨adāsye⟩, ⟨akariṣye⟩. It is of extremely rare occurrence.
¶ **483. II. Periphrastic Future.** This tense, which is allowed to be made from all verbs, contains a single indicative tense, active.∗ It is formed by the *nomen agentis* in ⟨tṛ⟩, having the value of a future active participle, to the nom. sing. of which (⟨tā⟩) are added, in the 1st and 2nd persons of all numbers, the corresponding inflected forms of the pres. of 1 ⟨as⟩ ‘be’. In the 3rd persons the *nomen agentis* is used alone, in the proper number, without the auxiliary.
¶ ∗ The Hindus also prescribe a middle formation; it has, however, practically no existence.
¶ **484.** The root has in most cases the same form before the suffix ⟨tṛ⟩ which it takes before the ⟨tum⟩ of the infinitive. Thus, ⟨gātṛ⟩; ⟨ji⟩, ⟨jetṛ⟩; ⟨stu⟩, ⟨stotṛ⟩; ⟨bhū⟩, ⟨bhavitṛ⟩; ⟨kṛ⟩, ⟨kartṛ⟩; ⟨kathaya⟩, ⟨kathayitṛ⟩.
¶ **485.** The inflection is then as follows:

## Aorist-System.
¶ **486.** The aorist comprises three quite distinct formations, each with certain sub-varieties; but all are bound together into one complex system by certain correspondences of form and meaning. In classical Sanskrit aorists are comparatively rare. Their value is quite that of impf. or pf. as tenses of narration. But they are used also (though not nearly so often as the prohibitive opt.) with the particle ⟨mā⟩, in prohibitions, the augment being then omitted; thus, ⟨mā dāḥ⟩ ‘do not give’; ⟨mā bhaiṣīḥ⟩ ‘do not fear’. With this exception the aorist always has the augment in classical Sanskrit. The tense comprises, in the later language, only an indicative mode.∗ The main varieties of aorist are three: I. Simple Aorist; II. Reduplicated Aorist; III. Sibilant Aorist.
¶ ∗ The precative is strictly a peculiar aor. optative; but it is so rare that its formation need not be explained here.
¶ **487. I. Simple aorist. (1) Root aorist.** This aorist is precisely like an imperfect of the root-class. It is limited to the active voice of a few roots in ⟨ā⟩, and of ⟨bhū⟩. E. g.
T  | 1 dā sing. | du. | pl. | bhū sing. | du. | pl.
R 1. | ⟨adām⟩ | ⟨adāva⟩ | ⟨adāma⟩ | ⟨abhūvam⟩ | ⟨abhūva⟩ | ⟨abhūma⟩
R 2. | ⟨adās⟩ | ⟨adātam⟩ | ⟨adāta⟩ | ⟨abhūs⟩ | ⟨abhūtam⟩ | ⟨abhūta⟩
R 3. | ⟨adāt⟩ | ⟨adātām⟩ | ⟨adus⟩ | ⟨abhūt⟩ | ⟨abhūtām⟩ | ⟨abhūvan⟩
¶ Like ⟨dā⟩: ⟨dhā⟩, ⟨adhāt⟩; ⟨sthā⟩, ⟨asthāt⟩; ⟨pā⟩, ⟨apāt⟩; ⟨gā⟩ ‘go’, ⟨agāt⟩.
¶ **488.** (2) The *a*-aorist. This is like an imperfect of the *a*-class, active and middle. Thus, from ⟨sic⟩, 1st persons ⟨asicam⟩, ⟨asicāva⟩, ⟨asicāma⟩; ⟨asice⟩, ⟨asicāvahi⟩, ⟨asicāmahi⟩. In general the root assumes a weak form; but three or four roots in final ⟨ṛ⟩ take *guṇa*. Thus, ⟨āp⟩, ⟨āpat⟩; ⟨gam⟩, ⟨agamat⟩; ⟨bhraṃś⟩, ⟨abhraśat⟩; ⟨muc⟩, ⟨amucat⟩; ⟨sad⟩, ⟨asadat⟩; ⟨śak⟩, ⟨aśakat⟩; ⟨sraṃs⟩, ⟨asrasat⟩. — Irregular: ⟨khyā⟩, ⟨akhyat⟩; ⟨hvā⟩, ⟨ahvat⟩; ⟨śvā⟩, ⟨aśvat⟩; ⟨śās⟩, ⟨aśiṣat⟩; 2 ⟨as⟩ ‘throw’, ⟨āsthat⟩ (anomalous). ⟨vac⟩ makes ⟨avocat⟩, and ⟨pat⟩, ⟨apaptat⟩, which, with one or two others, were doubtless originally reduplicated aorists.
@208
¶ **489.** II. Reduplicated Aorist (3). This aorist differs from all others in that it has come to be attached nearly always to the derivative (caus., etc.) conjugation in ⟨aya⟩, as its aorist. The connection is not formal, as the aorist is not made from the stem in ⟨aya⟩, but from the root. Its characteristic is a reduplication, of quite peculiar character.
¶ **490.** The reduplicated aorist is very unusual in classical Sanskrit, and it will be sufficient for the present to give an example or two of its formation. Thus, ⟨jan⟩ makes ⟨ajījanat⟩; ⟨spṛś⟩, ⟨apispṛśat⟩; ⟨sthā⟩, ⟨atiṣṭhipat⟩. The inflection is the usual one of imperfects of the *a*-conjugation.
¶ **491.** III. Sibilant Aorist, of four varieties. (4) The *s*-aorist. The tense-stem is made by adding ⟨s⟩ to the augmented root, which usually has its vowel strengthened. E. g. ⟨nī⟩: Act.: sing. 1. ⟨anaiṣam⟩, 2. ⟨anaiṣīs⟩, 3. ⟨anaiṣīt⟩; du. 1. ⟨anaiṣva⟩, 2. ⟨anaiṣṭam⟩, 3. ⟨anaiṣṭām⟩; pl. 1. ⟨anaiṣma⟩, 2. ⟨anaiṣṭa⟩, 3. ⟨anaiṣus⟩. Mid.: sing. 1. ⟨anaiṣi⟩, 2. ⟨anaiṣṭhās⟩, 3. ⟨anaiṣṭa⟩; du. 1. ⟨anaiṣvahi⟩, 2. ⟨anaiṣāthām⟩, 3. ⟨anaiṣātām⟩; pl. 1. ⟨anaiṣmahi⟩, 2. ⟨anaiḍḍhvam⟩, 3. ⟨anaiṣata⟩. — ⟨labh⟩ (mid. only): sing. 1. ⟨alapsi⟩, 2. ⟨alabdhās⟩, 3. ⟨alabdha⟩ etc.
¶ **492.** (5) The *iṣ*-aorist. The tense-stem is made by adding ⟨s⟩ by means of an inserted ⟨i⟩. The root is generally strengthened. E. g. ⟨pū⟩ ‘purify’: Act.: sing. 1. ⟨apāviṣam⟩, 2. ⟨apāvīs⟩, 3. ⟨apāvīt⟩; du. 1. ⟨apāviṣva⟩, 2. ⟨apāviṣṭam⟩, 3. ⟨apāviṣṭām⟩; pl. 1. ⟨apāviṣma⟩, 2. ⟨apāviṣṭa⟩, 3. ⟨apāviṣus⟩. — Mid, sing. 1. ⟨apāviṣi⟩, 2. ⟨apāviṣṭhās⟩, 3. ⟨apāviṣṭa⟩; du. 1. ⟨apāviṣvahi⟩, 2. ⟨apāviṣāthām⟩, 3. ⟨apāviṣātām⟩; pl. 1. ⟨apāviṣmahi⟩, 2. ⟨apāviḍhvam⟩, 3. ⟨apāviṣata⟩.
@209
¶ — This is the only aorist of which forms are made in the secondary and denominative conjugations (but for causatives and denominatives in ⟨aya⟩, cf. § 489).
¶ **493.** (6) The *siṣ*-aorist is active only, the corresponding middle being of the *s*-form. An example will suffix here. ⟨yā⟩: sing. 1. ⟨ayāsiṣam⟩ etc., quite like the inflection of the *iṣ*-aorist.
¶ **494.** (7) The *sa*-aorist. ⟨diś⟩: Act. sing. 1. ⟨adikṣam⟩, 2. ⟨adikṣas⟩, 3. ⟨adikṣat⟩; and so on, like an impf. of the *a*-conjugation. But in the mid. the grammarians prescribe the 1st. sing. ⟨adikṣi⟩, and 2nd and 3rd du. ⟨adikṣāthām⟩ and ⟨adikṣātām⟩.
¶ **495. Aorist Passive.** Generally the middle forms of aorists 4, 5, or 7, are used also for the passive. Roots which do not ordinarily take aorists of these forms, may make them like 4 or 5 especially for the passive.
¶ **496.** But a 3rd pers. sing., of peculiar formation, has become a recognized part of the passive conjugation. It is formed by adding ⟨i⟩ to the root, which takes also the augment, and is usually strengthened, in some cases by *guṇa*, in others by *vṛddhi*. After final ⟨ā⟩ is added ⟨y⟩. Thus, ⟨nī⟩, ⟨anāyi⟩; ⟨śru⟩, ⟨aśrāvi⟩; ⟨kṛ⟩, ⟨akāri⟩; ⟨vac⟩, ⟨avāci⟩; — but ⟨dam⟩, ⟨adami⟩; ⟨dṛś⟩, ⟨adarśi⟩; ⟨dā⟩, ⟨adāyi⟩.
