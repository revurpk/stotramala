## Lesson XLIII
¶ **447. Verbs. Perfect-System.** In the later language the perfect-system comprises only an indicative mode and a participle, each both active and middle. Its formation is essentially alike in all verbs; its characteristics are: 1. reduplication; 2. distinction of strong and weak forms; 3. endings in some respects peculiar; 4. the frequent use of the union-vowel *i*.
¶ **448. Reduplication.** 1. Initial consonants are reduplicated according to the rules given in Less. XLI for the reduplicated present-stem.
¶ 2. Medial and final vowels, short and long, are represented by the corresponding short vowel, diphthongs by their second element; but ⟨ṛ⟩ (or ⟨ar⟩) is represented always by ⟨a⟩, never by ⟨i⟩ as in the reduplicated present-stem. Thus, ⟨kram⟩, ⟨cakram⟩; ⟨sthā⟩, ⟨tasthā⟩; ⟨sic⟩, ⟨siṣic⟩; ⟨sev⟩, ⟨siṣev⟩; ⟨gā⟩, ⟨jagā⟩; ⟨kṛ⟩, ⟨cakṛ⟩.
¶ 3. Initial ⟨a⟩, followed by a single consonant, becomes ⟨ā⟩ (through ⟨a-a⟩); thus, ⟨ad⟩, ⟨ād⟩.
¶ 4. Initial ⟨i⟩ and ⟨u⟩ follow the same analogy; but in the strong forms, where the root-vowel is gunated, the reduplicating vowel is protected from combination by the insertion of ⟨y⟩ or ⟨v⟩; thus, ⟨iṣ⟩, strong perfect-stem ⟨iyeṣ⟩ (*i-y-eṣ*) weak ⟨īṣ⟩ (i. e. *i-iṣ*); ⟨uc⟩, strong ⟨uvoc⟩ (*u-v-oc*), weak ⟨ūc⟩ (*u-uc*).
¶ 5. Roots beginning with vowels long by nature or position do not in general make the ordinary perfect-system, but use instead a periphrastic formation (see below). But ⟨āp⟩ is an exception, making the constant perfect-stem ⟨āp⟩; and a few roots with initial ⟨a⟩ or ⟨ṛ⟩ show the anomalous reduplication ⟨ān⟩ in the perfect.∗
¶ ∗ The grammarians prescribe (doubtless falsely) this reduplication for all verbs beginning with ⟨a⟩ or ⟨ṛ⟩ followed by more than one consonant.
¶ **449. Strong and weak forms.** In the three persons sing. act. the root-syllable is accented, and exhibits usually a stronger form. As regards the strengthening:
¶ 1. In roots with medial vowels long by nature or position, and in those with initial ⟨a⟩, the difference of strong and weak forms does not appear, except in accented texts.
¶ 2. Medial and initial vowels are gunated, if possible, in the strong forms; thus, ⟨bhid⟩, w. ⟨bibhid⟩, s. ⟨bibhed⟩; ⟨iṣ⟩, w. ⟨īṣ⟩, s. ⟨iyeṣ⟩; ⟨uc⟩, w. ⟨ūc⟩, s. ⟨uvoc⟩ (§ 448, 4).
¶ 3. Medial ⟨a⟩ before a single final consonant is vriddhied in the 3rd pers., and optionally in the 1st; thus, from ⟨pac⟩, in 1st sing. either ⟨papac⟩ or ⟨papāc⟩, in 2nd ⟨papac⟩, in 3rd ⟨papāc⟩.
¶ 4. A final vowel takes either *guṇa* or *vṛddhi* in the 1st person, *guṇa* in the 2nd, *vṛddhi* in the 3rd; thus, from ⟨nī⟩, in 1st ⟨nine⟩ or ⟨ninai⟩, 2nd ⟨nine⟩, 3rd ⟨ninai⟩.
¶ **450.** The root ⟨bhū⟩ makes, irregularly, the perfect-stem ⟨babhū⟩, and adds ⟨v⟩ before a vowel-ending.
¶ **451.** Some roots, instead of strengthening the vowel in the strong forms, weaken it in the weak forms; some few even do both. See below.
¶ **452. Personal Endings.** The perfect-endings are these:
T  | Active sing. | du. | pl. | Middle sing. | du. | pl.
R 1. | ⟨a⟩ | ⟨va⟩ | ⟨ma⟩ | ⟨e⟩ | ⟨vahe⟩ | ⟨mahe⟩
R 2. | ⟨tha⟩ | ⟨athus⟩ | ⟨a⟩ | ⟨se⟩ | ⟨āthe⟩ | ⟨dhve⟩
R 3. | ⟨a⟩ | ⟨atus⟩ | ⟨us⟩ | ⟨e⟩ | ⟨āte⟩ | ⟨re⟩
¶ But roots ending in ⟨ā⟩ take ⟨au⟩ in 1st and 3rd sing. act.; thus, ⟨sthā⟩, ⟨tasthau⟩.
¶ **453. Union-vowel.** The endings beginning with consonants are in classical Sanskrit usually joined to the base by the union-vowel ⟨i⟩. The most important rules for the use of ⟨i⟩ are as follows:
¶ 1. The ⟨re⟩ of 3rd pl. mid. always has ⟨i⟩ before it.
¶ 2. The other endings beginning with consonants, except ⟨ya⟩, take it in nearly all verbs. But it is rejected throughout (except from ⟨re⟩) by eight verbs: viz. 1 ⟨kṛ⟩ ‘make’, ⟨bhṛ⟩ ‘bear’, ⟨sṛ⟩ ‘go’, 2 ⟨vṛ⟩ ‘choose’, ⟨dru⟩ ‘run’, ⟨śru⟩ ‘hear’, ⟨stu⟩ ‘praise’, ⟨sru⟩ ‘flow’.
¶ 3. For its use or omission in 2nd sing. act. the rules are too complicated to be given here.
¶ **454.** With the union-vowel ⟨i⟩ a final radical ⟨i⟩ or ⟨ī⟩ is not combined into ⟨ī⟩, but becomes ⟨y⟩ or (if more than one consonant precede) ⟨iy⟩; thus, from ⟨nī⟩, ⟨ninyiva⟩ *ni-ny-i-va*.

## Periphrastic Perfect.
¶ **472.** Most roots beginning with a vowel long by nature or position adopt a periphrastic formation in the perfect tense; the same is also taken by the secondary conjugations, and optionally by a few primary roots not falling in the above category. It is made as follows:
¶ **473.** To the accusative of a derivative noun-stem in ⟨ā⟩, made from the present-stem which is the general basis of each conjugation, are added, for the active, the perfect active forms of ⟨kṛ⟩ or ⟨as⟩ (or, very rarely, of ⟨bhū⟩); for the middle, only the perfect middle forms of ⟨kṛ⟩. Thus, from ⟨corayati⟩, pf. ⟨corayāmāsa⟩ or ⟨corayāṃcakāra⟩; from ⟨īḍ⟩, ⟨īḍāṃcakre⟩.

## Force of the Perfect.
¶ **474.** In classical Sanskrit the perfect coincides in meaning with the imperfect, as a tense of narration, but is less often met with.
