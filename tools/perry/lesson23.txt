## Lesson XXIII
¶ **255. Declension. Comparative Adjectives.** Comparative adjectives of primary formation have a double form of stem for masculine and neuter: a stronger in ⟨yāṃs⟩ (usually ⟨īyāṃs⟩), in the strong cases, and a weaker in ⟨yas⟩ (usually ⟨īyas⟩), there being no distinction of middle and weakest cases. The voc. sing. masc. ends in ⟨yan⟩. The feminine-stem is made with ⟨ī⟩ from the weak stem-form. Thus, ⟨śreyāṃs⟩, ‘better’:

¶ **256.** Stems in ⟨ant⟩ (or ⟨at⟩) fall into two divisions: A. those made with the suffix ⟨ant⟩ (⟨at⟩), being, with few exceptions, active participles, present and future; and B. those made with the possessive suffixes ⟨mant⟩ (or ⟨mat⟩) and ⟨vant⟩ (or ⟨vat⟩). They are masc. and neuter only, the fem. being formed with ⟨ī⟩.
¶ **257. A. Participles in ⟨ant⟩ (or ⟨at⟩).** E. g. ⟨jīvant⟩ m., n., ‘living.’

¶ **258.** The strong form of these participles is obtained, mechanically, by cutting off the final ⟨i⟩ from the 3rd pl. pres. (or fut.) ind. act.; thus, ⟨nayanti⟩ gives strong form of pres. act. part. ⟨nayant⟩, weak ⟨nayat⟩; ⟨tiṣṭhanti⟩, ⟨tiṣṭhant⟩ and ⟨tiṣṭhat⟩; — ⟨nahyanti⟩, ⟨nahyant⟩ and ⟨nahyat⟩; — ⟨daṇḍayanti⟩, ⟨daṇḍayant⟩ and ⟨daṇḍayat⟩; — ⟨bhaviṣyanti⟩ (fut.), ⟨bhaviṣyant⟩ and ⟨bhaviṣyat⟩.
¶ **259.** But those verbs which in the 3rd. pl. act. lose the ⟨n⟩ of the usual ⟨nti⟩ (as e. g. the verbs following the reduplicating class in the present-system), lose it also in the present participle, and have no distinction of strong and weak stem. Thus, from ⟨hu⟩, 3rd pl. pres. ind. act. ⟨juhvati⟩, part. (only stem-form) ⟨juhvat⟩: nom.-voc. sing. masc. ⟨juhvat⟩, acc. ⟨juhvatam⟩; nom.-voc.-acc. du. ⟨juhvatau⟩, pl. ⟨juhvatas⟩; nom.-voc.-acc. sing. neut. ⟨juhvat⟩, du. ⟨juhvatī⟩, pl. ⟨juhvati⟩.∗
¶ **260.** Only the present participles of verbs of the ⟨a⟩-class, the ⟨ya⟩-class, and causatives, invariably insert ⟨n⟩ in nom.-voc.-acc. du. neut. Present participles of the ⟨a⟩-class, of the root-class when the root ends in ⟨ā⟩, and *all* future participles, may either take or reject it; thus, neut.-sing. ⟨kirat⟩, du. ⟨kiratī⟩ or ⟨kirantī⟩; ⟨kariṣyat⟩ (fut.), du. ⟨kariṣyatī⟩ or ⟨kariṣyantī⟩; ⟨yāt⟩ (pres. part. from ⟨yā⟩, ‘go’), du. ⟨yātī⟩ or ⟨yāntī⟩. Participles of all other verbs, and all other stems in ⟨at⟩, leave out the ⟨n⟩ in the du. neut.; thus, ⟨adat⟩ (⟨ad⟩ ‘eat’, root-class), du. ⟨adatī⟩.
¶ **261.** The adj. ⟨mahant⟩, ‘great’, takes in strong cases the stem-
@115
¶ form ⟨mahānt⟩: nom.-sing. masc. ⟨mahān⟩ (see § 239, 2), acc. ⟨mahāntam⟩, voc. ⟨mahan⟩; du. neut. ⟨mahatī⟩, pl. ⟨mahānti⟩. Otherwise the inflection is like that of participles.
¶ **262.** The feminine of participles and adjectives in ⟨ant⟩ (or ⟨at⟩) is always made with ⟨ī⟩, and the form is always identical with the nom. dual neuter.
