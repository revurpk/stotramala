## Lesson XXVII
¶ **285. Demonstrative Pronouns.** Two demonstrative-declensions are made up with particular irregularity: they are those of the pronouns ⟨ayam⟩ and ⟨asau⟩ (for which the natives give the stem-forms as ⟨idam⟩ and ⟨adas⟩ respectively). The first is a more indefinite demonstrative: ‘this’ or ‘that’; the other signifies especially the remoter relation.

¶ **288.** There is a defective pronominal stem ⟨ena⟩, which is accentless, and hence used only in situations where no emphasis falls upon it. The only forms are the following: Sing. acc. m. ⟨enam⟩, n. ⟨enad⟩, f. ⟨enām⟩; instr. m., n., ⟨enena⟩, f. ⟨enayā⟩. Du. acc. m. ⟨enau⟩, f., n., ⟨ene⟩; gen.-loc. m., f., n., ⟨enayos⟩. Pl. acc. m. ⟨enān⟩, n. ⟨enāni⟩, f. ⟨enās⟩. — These forms may be used only when the person or object to which they refer has already been indicated by a form of ⟨ayam⟩ or ⟨eṣa⟩. Thus, ⟨anena kāvyam adhītam enaṃ vyākaraṇam adhyāpaya⟩ “this one has read the art of poetry; teach him grammar”.
¶ **289. Past Passive Participle in ⟨ta⟩ or ⟨na⟩.** By the suffix ⟨ta⟩ — or, in a comparatively small number of verbs, ⟨na⟩ — is formed, directly from the root of the verb, and unconnected with any tense-stem, a verbal adjective called the past passive participle. The fem. ends always in ⟨ā⟩. When this participle is made from transitive verbs, it qualifies something as having endured the action expressed by the verb; thus, ⟨datta⟩ ‘given’; ⟨ukta⟩ ‘spoken’. When made from an intransitive or neuter verb, the same par-
@130
¶ ticiple has no passive, but only an indefinite past, sense; thus, ⟨gata⟩, ‘gone’, ⟨bhūta⟩, ‘been’; ⟨patita⟩, ‘fallen’.
¶ **290.** This participle is often used as an adjective. Very commonly, also, it supplies the place of a finite verb, when some form of ⟨as⟩, ‘be’, or ⟨bhū⟩ is to be supplied; thus, ⟨sa gataḥ⟩: “he is gone”; ⟨mayā pattraṃ likhitam⟩ “by me a letter was written”. The neuter is frequently used as a substantive; thus, ⟨dattam⟩ ‘a gift’; ⟨dugdham⟩ ‘milk’; and also as *nomen actionis*. Sometimes it has a present signification, particularly when made from neuter verbs; thus, ⟨sthita⟩ (from ⟨sthā⟩) often ‘standing’.
¶ **291. A. With suffix ⟨na⟩ [⟨ṇa⟩].** The suffix ⟨na⟩ is taken by a number of roots. Thus:
¶ 1. Certain roots in ⟨ā⟩, and in *i* and *u*-vowels; thus, ⟨pyā⟩ or ⟨pī⟩ ‘swell, be fat’, ⟨pīna⟩; ⟨hā⟩ ‘abandon’, ⟨hīna⟩; ⟨glā⟩ ‘wither’, ⟨glāna⟩; ⟨kṣi⟩ ‘destroy’, ⟨kṣīṇa⟩; ⟨śvā⟩ or ⟨śvi⟩ ‘swell’, ⟨śūna⟩; ⟨lū⟩ ‘cut’, ⟨lūna⟩.
¶ 2. The roots in variable ⟨ṛ⟩ (so-called ṝ-roots), which before the suffix becomes ⟨īr⟩ or ⟨ūr⟩, as in the pres. pass.; thus, 2⟨kṛ⟩ (⟨kir⟩), ⟨kīrṇa⟩; ⟨tṛ⟩, ⟨tīrṇa⟩; 1⟨pṛ⟩ (⟨pūr⟩) ‘fill’, ⟨pūrṇa⟩.
¶ 3. A few roots ending in ⟨j⟩ (which becomes ⟨g⟩ before the ⟨na⟩); thus, ⟨bhañj⟩ ‘break’, ⟨bhagna⟩; 1⟨bhuj⟩ ‘bend’, ⟨bhugna⟩; ⟨majj⟩ ‘sink’, ⟨magna⟩; ⟨ruj⟩ ‘be sick’, ⟨rugṇa⟩; ⟨vij⟩ ‘fear’, ⟨vigna⟩. Also one or two others which exhibit a guttural before the ⟨na⟩: ⟨lag⟩ ‘attach’, ⟨lagna⟩; ⟨vraśc⟩ ‘cut up’, ⟨vṛkṇa⟩.
¶ 4. A number of roots, some of them very common, in ⟨d⟩ (which becomes ⟨n⟩ before ⟨na⟩): ⟨sad⟩, ⟨sanna⟩; (⟨ni-sad⟩, ⟨niṣaṇṇa⟩;) ⟨bhid⟩ ‘cut’, ⟨bhinna⟩.∗
¶ **292.** Some few verbs make double forms; thus, ⟨tvar⟩ ‘hasten’, ⟨tūrṇa⟩ or ⟨tvarita⟩; ⟨vid⟩ ‘acquire’, ⟨vinna⟩ or ⟨vitta⟩.
¶ ∗ Commonest exceptions: ⟨khādita⟩ from ⟨khād⟩ ‘eat’; ⟨matta⟩ from ⟨mad⟩; ⟨mudita⟩ from ⟨mud⟩ ‘rejoice’; ⟨rudita⟩ from ⟨rud⟩ ‘weep’; ⟨udita⟩ from ⟨vad⟩ ‘speak’; ⟨vidita⟩ from ⟨vid⟩ ‘know’.
