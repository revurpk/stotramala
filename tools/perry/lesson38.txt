## Lesson XXXVIII
¶ **407. Verbs. Root-class, cont’d.∗** Roots ending in an ⟨i⟩-vowel or an ⟨u⟩-vowel (except ⟨i⟩ ‘go’) change these into ⟨iy⟩ and ⟨uv⟩ before vowel-endings in weak forms, when not gunated.
¶ **408.** Root ⟨i⟩ ‘go’ (act., but used in mid. with the prep. ⟨adhi⟩: ‘go over for oneself’, i. e. ‘repeat, learn, read’; the ⟨i⟩ then becomes ⟨iy⟩, as above).

¶ **409.** The root ⟨śī⟩ (mid.), ‘lie’, has *guṇa* throughout; thus, ⟨śaye⟩, ⟨śeṣe⟩, ⟨śete⟩, ⟨śevahe⟩ etc.; impf. ⟨aśayi⟩, ⟨aśethās⟩ etc.; opt. ⟨śayīya⟩ etc., part. ⟨śayāna⟩. Other irregularities are the 3rd persons pl.: indic. ⟨śerate⟩, imv. ⟨śeratām⟩, impf. ⟨aśerata⟩.
¶ **410.** The roots of this class ending in ⟨u⟩ have in their strong forms the *vṛddhi* instead of the *guṇa*-strengthening before an ending beginning with a consonant.

¶ **413. Emphatic Pronoun.** The uninflected pronominal word ⟨svayam⟩ signifies ‘self’, ‘own self’. It is oftenest used as a nominative, along with words of all persons and numbers; but not seldom it represents other cases also.
¶ ∗ Special irregularities in this verb are occasionally met with, such as ⟨brūmi⟩, ⟨bravīhi⟩. Some of the verbs in ⟨u⟩ are allowed to be inflected like ⟨brū⟩, but forms so made are rare.

S 26a | puṣpāṇīva vicinvantam anyatra gatamānasam | As upon one gathering flowers, whose mind has gone elsewhere, | పువ్వులను ఏరుకుంటున్నవానిపై వలె, మనస్సు మరెక్కడికో పోయిన
S 26b | anavāpteṣu kāmeṣu mṛtyur abhyeti mānavam | while his desires are still unfulfilled, death comes upon the man. | కోరికలు తీరకుండానే మనిషి మీదికి మృత్యువు వస్తుంది.
¶ Into English (the translations under each sentence are the editor’s, in English and Telugu):
S 1 | bho duṣkṛtakāriṇaḥ asmād vanād apeteti krodhād ṛṣir āśramahariṇavadhodyatān vyādhān abravīt | “Ho, evil-doers, go away from this forest!”: so the seer, in anger, said to the hunters who were ready to kill the deer of the hermitage. | “ఓ దుష్టకర్ములారా, ఈ అడవి నుండి వెళ్ళిపోండి!” అని ఋషి కోపంతో ఆశ్రమ జింకలను చంపడానికి సిద్ధమైన వేటగాళ్ళతో అన్నాడు.
