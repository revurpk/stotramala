## Lesson X
¶ **168. Verbs. Passive Inflection.** A certain form of present-stem, inflected with middle endings, is used only with a passive meaning, and is formed from all roots for which there is occasion to make a passive conjugation. Its sign is an accented ⟨ya⟩ added to the root, without any reference to the classes according to which the active and middle forms are made. The inflection is precisely like that of other ⟨a⟩-stems. Thus, ⟨tanye⟩, ⟨tanyase⟩, ⟨tanyate⟩, etc.
¶ **169.** Outside the present-system middle forms may be used in a passive sense; but there is a special form for the aor. pass. in the 3rd sing.
¶ **170.** The form of root to which the passive-sign is appended is usually a weak one. Thus a penultimate nasal is dropped; and certain abbreviations which are made in the weak forms of the perfect, or in the past passive participle, are found also in the passive present-system. E. g. from ⟨añj⟩, pass. ⟨ajyate⟩; from ⟨bandh⟩, ⟨badhyate⟩.
¶ **171.** In the roots ⟨vac⟩, ⟨vad⟩, ⟨vap⟩, ⟨vas⟩, ⟨vah⟩, and ⟨svap⟩, the ⟨va⟩ becomes ⟨u⟩ in the pres.; thus, ⟨ucyate⟩, ⟨upyate⟩, ⟨uṣyate⟩ (see note to § 102), ⟨supyate⟩. Similarly, ⟨yaj⟩ makes ⟨ijyate⟩, and ⟨grah⟩ and ⟨prach⟩ make ⟨gṛhyate⟩ and ⟨pṛcchyate⟩; ⟨śās⟩ makes ⟨śiṣyate⟩.

¶ **172.** Final ⟨i⟩ and ⟨u⟩ of roots are generally lengthened; thus, ⟨ji⟩, ⟨jīyate⟩; ⟨stu⟩, ⟨stūyate⟩.
¶ **173.** Final ⟨ṛ⟩ is in general changed to ⟨ri⟩; thus, ⟨kṛ⟩, ⟨kriyate⟩; but if preceded by two consonants it takes *guṇa*; thus, ⟨smṛ⟩, ⟨smaryate⟩. The roots in “variable ⟨ṛ⟩”, which the natives write with ⟨ṝ⟩, change ⟨ṛ⟩ to ⟨īr⟩, or, if a labial letter precede, to ⟨ūr⟩; thus, ⟨tṛ⟩, ⟨tīryate⟩; ⟨kṛ⟩, ‘strew’, ⟨kīryate⟩; but ⟨pṛ⟩, ⟨pūryate⟩.
¶ **174.** Final ⟨ā⟩ of roots is usually changed to ⟨ī⟩; thus, ⟨dā⟩, ⟨dīyate⟩; ⟨gā⟩, ⟨gīyate⟩; ⟨dhā⟩, ⟨dhīyate⟩. But ⟨dhyā⟩ makes ⟨dhyāyate⟩; and so some other roots in ⟨ā⟩.
¶ **175.** The roots ⟨tan⟩ and ⟨khan⟩ usually form their passives from parallel roots in ⟨ā⟩; thus, ⟨tāyate⟩. But ⟨tanyate⟩ and ⟨khanyate⟩ occur.
¶ **176.** Verbs of causative inflection, and denominatives in ⟨aya⟩, form their passive by adding ⟨ya⟩ to the causative or denominative stem after ⟨aya⟩ has been dropped; thus, ⟨coryate⟩ ‘is stolen’; ⟨gaṇyate⟩ ‘is counted’.
¶ **177.** The personal passive construction, with the logical subject in the instrumental, is particularly common with transitive verbs; and not less so the impersonal passive construction, both with transitive and intransitive verbs. Thus, ⟨nareṇa svargo labhyate⟩ ‘Heaven is reached by the man’; ⟨āgamyate⟩ ‘one comes hither’; ⟨supyate⟩ ‘one sleeps’; ⟨śrūyate⟩ ‘it is heard’, i. e. ‘they say’. The predicate to the instrumental subject of such a construction is of course also instrumental; thus, ⟨rāmeṇarṣiṇā jīvyate⟩ ‘Rāma lives as a seer’.
