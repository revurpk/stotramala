## Lesson XI
¶ **178. Verbs. Imperfect Active, ⟨a⟩-conjugation.** The imperfect is formed from the present-stem by prefixing the augment ⟨a⟩, and adding a set of secondary endings.
¶ **179.** If the present-stem begin with a vowel, the augment unites with it to form always the *vṛddhi*-vowel, not the *guṇa*: thus ⟨a⟩ + ⟨i⟩ or ⟨ī⟩ or ⟨e⟩ = ⟨ai⟩; ⟨a⟩ + ⟨u⟩ or ⟨ū⟩ = ⟨au⟩; ⟨a⟩ + ⟨ṛ⟩ = ⟨ār⟩.
¶ **180.** If a preposition be prefixed, the augment comes between preposition and verb, as in Greek; thus, from ⟨upa-nī⟩, impf.-stem ⟨upānaya⟩, i. e. ⟨upa⟩ + ⟨a⟩ + ⟨naya⟩; ⟨vi-nī⟩, impf.-stem ⟨vyanaya⟩.
¶ **181.** The inflection in the active is as follows:
T  | Sing. | Dual. | Plural.
R 1. | ⟨avadam⟩ | ⟨avadāva⟩ | ⟨avadāma⟩
R 2. | ⟨avadas⟩ | ⟨avadatam⟩ | ⟨avadata⟩
R 3. | ⟨avadat⟩ | ⟨avadatām⟩ | ⟨avadan⟩
¶ **182.** The imperfect is the tense of narration; it expresses past time simply, without any further implication.
¶ **183. Polysyllabic Feminines in ⟨ī⟩,** declined like ⟨nadī⟩, ‘river.’

¶ **184. Final nasals.** The nasals ⟨ṅ⟩, ⟨ṇ⟩, and ⟨n⟩, occurring as finals *after a short vowel*, are doubled before any initial vowel: thus, ⟨atiṣṭhan atra⟩ becomes ⟨atiṣṭhann atra⟩.
