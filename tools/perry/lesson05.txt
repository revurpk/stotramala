## Lesson V
¶ **124. Verbs. Unaccented ⟨ya⟩-class.** Roots of this class form their present-stem by adding ⟨ya⟩ to the root, which bears the accent. Thus from ⟨nah⟩ is made the present-stem ⟨nahya⟩; from ⟨lubh⟩, ⟨lubhya⟩.
¶ **125.** The inflection of stems of this class follows the model of ⟨vad⟩.
¶ **126.** Certain ⟨ā⟩-roots, because of their peculiar exchanges with ⟨i⟩ and ⟨ī⟩-forms, especially in the formation of the present-stem, are given by the Hindu grammarians as ending in ⟨e⟩ or ⟨ai⟩ or ⟨o⟩ (cf. § 132), and by them assigned to the ⟨bhū⟩, or ⟨a⟩-class. Thus ⟨dhā⟩, ‘suck’ (Hindu ⟨dhe⟩), forms ⟨dhayati⟩; the root ⟨hū⟩ or ⟨hvā⟩ (Hindu ⟨hve⟩) forms ⟨hvayati⟩; ⟨gā⟩ (Hindu ⟨gai⟩) makes ⟨gāyati⟩.
¶ **127.** For the root ⟨dṛś⟩, ‘see’, is substituted in the present-system another root ⟨paś⟩, which makes ⟨paśyati⟩.

¶ Masculine adjectives in ⟨u⟩ are similarly declined.
¶ **129. Euphonic Changes of ⟨s⟩, cont’d.** 1. Final ⟨s⟩, the dental sibilant, whether original or representing final ⟨r⟩, before the palatal surd mutes [⟨c⟩, ⟨ch⟩], is assimilated, becoming palatal ⟨ś⟩. Thus ⟨naras carati⟩ becomes ⟨naraś carati⟩; ⟨naras chalena⟩ becomes ⟨naraś chalena⟩. 2. Before a lingual surd mute [⟨ṭ⟩, ⟨ṭh⟩], in like manner, it would become lingual ⟨ṣ⟩, but the case almost never occurs. 3. Before the dental surd mutes [⟨t⟩, ⟨th⟩], since it is already of the same class with them, it of course remains unchanged; thus, ⟨rāmas tiṣṭhati⟩.
¶ **130.** The preposition ⟨ā⟩ is sometimes used with the ablative (much less often with the accusative), in the sense of ‘hither from’, ‘all the way from’; but far more usually to signify ‘all the way to’, ‘until’. As a prefix to verbs, ⟨ā⟩ means ‘to’, ‘unto’, ‘at’.
