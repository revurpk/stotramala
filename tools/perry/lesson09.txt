¶ **163.** Adjectives in ⟨a⟩ are declined in the masc. like ⟨deva⟩, in the fem. like ⟨senā⟩, in the neuter like ⟨phala⟩. But often the fem. stem ends in ⟨ī⟩, and is declined like ⟨nadī⟩ (in Less. XI).
¶ **164.** Final ⟨ai⟩ and ⟨au⟩, according to § 159, become ⟨āy⟩ and ⟨āv⟩ respectively before any following vowel or diphthong. The ⟨y⟩ or ⟨v⟩ may then be dropped, leaving a hiatus. The ⟨y⟩ is in fact always dropped, but the ⟨v⟩ not often. Thus, ⟨senāyai atra⟩ becomes, through the medium of ⟨senāyāy atra⟩, ⟨senāyā atra⟩; ⟨devau atra⟩ becomes ⟨devāv atra⟩.
¶ **165.** Initial ⟨ch⟩, after short vowels, the preposition ⟨ā⟩, and the prohibitive particle ⟨mā⟩, becomes ⟨cch⟩: thus, ⟨atra chāyā⟩ becomes ⟨atra cchāyā⟩; ⟨ā⟩ + ⟨chādayati⟩ = ⟨ācchādayati⟩.
@68
¶ **166.** An initial ⟨n⟩ of a root generally becomes ⟨ṇ⟩ after a verbal prefix containing ⟨r⟩, either original or representing ⟨s⟩; such as ⟨antar⟩ ‘between’, ⟨nis⟩, ⟨parā⟩, etc. Thus, ⟨praṇayati⟩, ⟨nirṇayati⟩.
¶ **167.** The following prefixes are often used before verbs: ⟨anu⟩ ‘after, along, toward’; ⟨ava⟩ ‘down, off’; ⟨ud⟩ ‘up, up forth or out’; ⟨upa⟩ ‘to, toward’; ⟨ni⟩ ‘down; in, into’; ⟨nis⟩ ‘out, forth’; ⟨parā⟩ ‘to a distance, away’; ⟨pari⟩ ‘round about, around’; ⟨pra⟩ ‘forward, forth’; ⟨sam⟩ ‘along with, completely.’
