## Lesson XXXVI
¶ **392. Verbs. Class IV. B. ⟨u⟩-class.** The few roots (only six) of this sub-class end already in ⟨n⟩ — except one, ⟨kṛ⟩, of considerable irregularity — and so add only ⟨u⟩ as class-sign. The inflection is quite that of the ⟨nu⟩-class, the ⟨u⟩ being gunated in the strong forms, and dropped (optionally, but in fact nearly always) before ⟨v⟩ and ⟨m⟩ of 1st dual and plural.
¶ **393.** Thus ⟨tan⟩, ‘stretch’, makes ⟨tanomi⟩, ⟨tanoṣi⟩, etc.; 1st du. ⟨tanvas⟩ (or ⟨tanuvas⟩), 1st pl. ⟨tanmas⟩ (or ⟨tanumas⟩); mid. ⟨tanve⟩, ⟨tanvahe⟩, ⟨tanmahe⟩, etc. — all like a vowel-root of the ⟨nu⟩-class.
¶ **394.** The root 1⟨kṛ⟩, ‘make’, makes the strong stem ⟨karo⟩, weak ⟨kuru⟩; the class-sign ⟨u⟩ is always dropped before ⟨v⟩ and ⟨m⟩ in 1st du. and 1st pl., and also before ⟨y⟩ of the opt. active. Thus:

¶ Participle. ⟨kurvant⟩, f. ⟨kurvatī⟩; ⟨kurvāṇa⟩, f. ⟨-ā⟩.
¶ **395.** This root sometimes assumes (or retains from a more original condition) an initial ⟨s⟩ after the prefix ⟨sam⟩; thus, ⟨saṃskaroti⟩, ⟨saṃskurute⟩, ⟨samaskurvan⟩.
¶ **396.** The adverbial prefixes ⟨āvis⟩ and ⟨prādus⟩, ‘forth to sight’, ⟨ārāt⟩ ‘in view’; ⟨tiras⟩ ‘through’, ‘out of sight’; ⟨puras⟩ ‘in front, forward’; and the purely adverbial ⟨alam⟩ ‘enough, sufficient’, are often used with ⟨kṛ⟩, and with one or two other verbs, oftenest ⟨as⟩ ‘be’ and ⟨bhū⟩ ‘become’.
¶ **397.** Any noun or adjective-stem is liable to be compounded with verbal forms or derivatives of the roots ⟨kṛ⟩ and ⟨bhū⟩, in the manner of a verbal prefix. If the final of the stem be an ⟨a⟩-vowel or an ⟨i⟩-vowel, it is changed to ⟨ī⟩; if an ⟨u⟩-vowel, to ⟨ū⟩. Consonantal stems take the form which they have before consonant-endings — of course with observance of the usual euphonic rules; but stems in ⟨an⟩ change those letters to ⟨ī⟩. Thus, ⟨svīkaroti⟩ ‘he makes his own’, ‘appropriates’; ⟨bhasmīkaroti⟩ (⟨bhasman⟩) ‘he changes to ashes’, i. e. ‘burns’; ⟨stambhībhavati⟩ ‘becomes a post’ (⟨stambha⟩); ⟨śucībhavati⟩ ‘becomes pure’ (⟨śuci⟩); ⟨sādhūkaroti⟩ ‘makes holy’.
¶ **398.** The suffixes ⟨tā⟩ (f.) and ⟨tva⟩ (n.) are very extensively used to make abstract nouns meaning ‘the state or quality of being so-and-so’, from both adjectives and nouns. Thus, ⟨kṣatriyatā⟩ f., ⟨kṣatriyatva⟩ n., ‘the rank of a Kṣatriya’.

E 17 | The king-of-the-Kaliṅgas wounded his enemy in the breast with an arrow. | kaliṅgarājaḥ śareṇa śatrum urasi kṣaṇute sma | కళింగ రాజు బాణంతో శత్రువును ఛాతిలో గాయపరిచాడు.
