! In the paradigm of viśvapā (§ 213) Perry prints, in the dual of the accusative row, the form viśvapābhyām and, in the dual of the instrumental row, viśvapos (with the transliteration viśvap-os); both are misplaced (the first belongs to the instrumental and the second is the genitive-locative). They are kept as printed.
! § 212 reads “those in ā are rare that it is not possible …” in this scan (so rare, evidently); it is kept as printed. Perry’s āi and āu are written ai and au; accents on the verbs and on the nouns are not carried.

Exercise XVII opens with a verse Perry numbers ॥ 6 ॥ (nābhinandeta maraṇaṃ …, “Let him not rejoice in death nor in life, let him await his time, as a servant his orders”), with his footnote ∗ (“Rule for an ascetic …”); his sentences are then numbered 1–11, and the English ones 12–22. ## Lesson XVII
¶ **210. Verbs, ⟨a⟩-conjugation. Present Optative Middle.** The optative middle (and passive) of ⟨a⟩-stems, formed as shown in the preceding lesson, is inflected as follows:
T  | Sing. | Dual. | Plural.
R 1. | ⟨labheya⟩ | ⟨labhevahi⟩ | ⟨labhemahi⟩
R 2. | ⟨labhethās⟩ | ⟨labheyāthām⟩ | ⟨labhedhvam⟩
R 3. | ⟨labheta⟩ | ⟨labheyātām⟩ | ⟨labheran⟩
¶ Similarly ⟨coryeya⟩, ⟨saṃgaccheya⟩, etc.
¶ **211. Declension.** The stem ⟨nau⟩ f., ‘ship, boat’, is entirely regular, taking throughout the normal endings, as given in § 90. Thus: ⟨naus⟩, ⟨nāvam⟩, ⟨nāvā⟩, etc.; ⟨nāvau⟩, ⟨naubhyām⟩, etc.; ⟨nāvas⟩, ⟨naubhis⟩, etc.

¶ **212.** The stems ending in long vowels (⟨ā⟩, ⟨ī⟩, ⟨ū⟩) fall into two well-marked classes: A. **root-stems — mostly monosyllabic — and their compounds,** with a comparatively small number of others inflected like them; B. **derivative feminine stems** in ⟨ā⟩ and ⟨ī⟩, with a few in ⟨ū⟩, inflected like ⟨jāyā⟩, ⟨nadī⟩ and ⟨vadhū⟩. The stems of class A take the normal endings throughout, with optional exceptions in dat., abl.-gen., and loc. sing. fem., and with ⟨n⟩ inserted before ⟨ām⟩ of the gen. pl. The simple words are as nouns with few exceptions fem.; as adjectives (rare), and in adjective compounds, they coincide in masc. and fem. forms. The declension of the simple words in ⟨ī⟩ and ⟨ū⟩ has been given (in §§ 189, 197); those in ⟨ā⟩ are rare that it is not possible to make up a whole scheme of forms in actual use.
¶ **213.** When any root in ⟨ā⟩ or ⟨ī⟩ or ⟨ū⟩ is found as final member of a compound word, these root-finals are treated as follows: 1. Roots in ⟨ā⟩ lose that vowel before vowel-endings, except in the strong cases and in the acc. pl., which is like the nominative. Thus, ⟨viśva-pā⟩ m., f., ‘all-protecting’:
T  | Sing. | Dual. | Plural.
R N.V. | ⟨viśvapās⟩ ⟨-pās⟩ | ⟨viśvapau⟩ ⟨-pau⟩ | ⟨viśvapās⟩ ⟨-pās⟩
R A. | ⟨viśvapām⟩ ⟨-pām⟩ | ⟨viśvapābhyām⟩ | „ „
R I. | ⟨viśvapā⟩ ⟨viśvap-ā⟩ | ⟨viśvapos⟩ ⟨viśvap-os⟩ | ⟨viśvapābhis⟩ etc.
¶ **214.** 2. Roots in ⟨ī⟩ and ⟨ū⟩ change their final vowel, before vowel-endings, into ⟨y⟩ and ⟨v⟩, if but one consonant precede the final vowel; but if two or more consonants precede, the change is into ⟨iy⟩ and ⟨uv⟩. Thus, ⟨yava-krī⟩ m., f., ‘corn-buying’: nom.-voc. ⟨yavakrīs⟩, acc. ⟨yavakriyam⟩; ⟨khala-pū⟩ m., f., ‘street-sweeper’: nom. sing. ⟨khalapūs⟩, acc. ⟨khalapvam⟩.
