64 | People gladly give milk to children, yet are afraid to take poison; know how harmful kindness can be, and show some fear in the right place, wise one. | 
65 | When kings go beyond bounds, when a widow’s house is judged, even if the accountant is a Vedic scholar, it ends in deadly trouble and does not stop, wise one. | 
66 | Poetic gatherings adorned with the nine sentiments and sweet singing, however much told to a senseless man, are like blowing a conch for the deaf, certainly, wise one. | 
67 | Do not laugh in an assembly; do not laugh with your mother, father or husband; do not laugh with another man’s wife; do not laugh at eminent brahmins — this is the rule, wise one. | 
68 | Water is the support of life; the mouth is the source of all sweet speech; a woman is a jewel to men; and a sari is a woman’s adornment, they say, certainly, wise one. | 
69 | Do not nurse enmity with anyone; do not grieve when poverty comes; do not mock in assemblies; do not give your heart to a woman on earth, wise one. | 
70 | The wise never go empty-handed to their husband, to the wife who is theirs, to the deity, to a good teacher, or to their son — that is the path of conduct, wise one. | 
71 | Be a servant at work, a Rambhā in pleasure, a counsellor in advice, a mother when serving food — such should one’s wife be, wise one. | 
72 | He is the greatest who treats other women as sisters, does not covet others’ wealth, is a friend to others, does not swell when praised, and does not get angry when others are angry, wise one. | 
73 | Do not long for another man’s wife, do not covet others’ wealth, do not think ill of others, do not keep wrong company, and do not go to relatives after losing your fortune, wise one. | 
74 | A man who keeps company with other men’s wives is blamed on earth even if he were Bhīṣma; a good-natured woman is blamed if she is found in another man’s company, wise one. | 
75 | Do not tell others your wishes; do not go into neighbours’ houses without business; do not embrace a wife who has approached another; and do not rely on a vicious horse, wise one. | 
76 | Do not approach your wife on holy days; do not swell with pride trusting a king’s favour; do not let a wife grow proud; and do not stay where there is no management, wise one. | 
77 | The price of a betel roll prepared after cleaning one’s teeth, the sleep of a day when one has washed one’s head, and the union with a woman after a quarrel cannot be told, listen, wise one. | 
78 | Serving a master who does not know good and bad, the love of a woman unknown to the household, friendship that angers the world — each is like swimming against the current, wise one. | 
79 | Water mingled with milk looks like milk, but it spoils the taste of the milk; so avoid the company of a base man, wise one. | 
80 | Even when a wise man, you should not pity a wicked man and relieve his trouble: does one who has caught a scorpion by the tail know the good done to it? wise one. | 
81 | Do not go to tasks you have not been invited to, to a woman’s house who does not welcome you, to a court where the king is not seen, to a feast you were not invited to, nor make friendships that do not suit, wise one. | 
82 | A father’s joy in a son does not arise when the son is born; he feels it when people see his son and praise him, wise one. | 
83 | The merchant is the life of a town, water the life of rice, the trunk the life of an elephant, and woman the life of fortune, certainly, wise one. | 
84 | Even if one brings tiger’s milk, cuts out one’s heart for her, or pours out wealth from head to foot, a courtesan has no love, listen, wise one. | 
85 | In the days when one is given, anything comes even from the wilderness; in the days when one is not, what use is it to climb up to a golden mountain? wise one. | 
86 | When an enemy lives next door, when a mere writer becomes lord, when a landlord speaks slander — the accountants have no livelihood, wise one. | 
87 | Do not pawn gold; do not run from battle if you are clever; do not spend the market rations; and make no friends with a fool, listen, wise one. | 
88 | Is it good to dispute with many, saying “I am strong”? A powerful snake is killed by the biting ants, wise one. | 
89 | When a woman loves someone in her heart, a cruel lover wanders on without stopping; does a parrot caught by a cat read from its cage on earth? wise one. | 
90 | Governing without an able minister before the lord of a province is like a mountain-large elephant in rut without a trunk, wise one. | 
91 | The kingdom of a man with a minister mostly keeps its order and stands in the world; the kingdom of one without a minister goes on like a machine with its pin loosened, wise one. | 
92 | Truth is the life of speech, a host of good soldiers the life of a fort, honour the life of a woman, and a signature the life of a document, certainly, wise one. | 
93 | To take shelter with a man who has lost his self-respect and fallen low is like an elephant hiding its body in a measure of water, know it, wise one. | 
94 | A kingdom whose king makes friends of a dirty man who does not see good, a washerman, a doorman or a barber will merge into the earth and not flourish, wise one. | 
95 | By serving a king who does not call you with “Come, go,” can there be enjoyment or liberation? It is like playing in a house with no lamp, certainly, wise one. | 
96 | Do not lie after speaking with care; do not speak ill in your heart of a helpful relative; do not serve an angry king; and keep far from a sinful country, wise one. | 
97 | Think: a man of right conduct is stronger than a merely big man, like a mahout who climbs a mountainous elephant on earth, wise one. | 
98 | Do not plough a flooded field; in famine do not go to your kinsmen; do not tell secrets to others; and do not make a coward a commander, wise one. | 
99 | A village without rice-fields, a village without a lord, a road without a companion, a house on earth without a husband — each could be called a cremation-ground, wise one. | 
100 | One should listen to whatever anyone says, but not act hastily after hearing — consider carefully; a man who finds out what is false and what true is, on earth, a man of right conduct, wise one. | 
101 | A mouth that does not chew betel, that does not taste the lip-nectar of young women, and that cannot sing is a wasted hole, fit only for ashes, wise one. | 
102 | There is no affection in a prostitute; and if there were, it would not last; a flower will not grow on a road many people walk, and if it did, it would not flourish, wise one. | 
103 | Do not rely on the vows of a courtesan, on the friendship of a doorman’s patron, or on riches seen in a dream, listen, wise one. | 
104 | Whether he is of a base caste, a worthless idler, or the son of a maid — the man who has coins is king, wise one. | 
105 | Learning that brings no good, songs that do not reach the flavour of raga with proper expression, a feast without bustle, and words the assembly does not praise are all quickly spent, wise one. | 
106 | Pleasure leads to displeasure, full happiness to greater pain, growth to breaking, and falling of price to rising — certainly, wise one. | 
107 | Fortune comes easily, like coconut water; and goes away when she goes, like a wood-apple swallowed by an elephant, wise one. | 
108 | Do not argue with women; do not befriend and speak with boys; do not give up good qualities; and never blame a lord who rules you, wise one. | 
109 | Do not serve at courts that give only false hope; do not make a temple-festival for a jewel with the wicked; do not make close friendships; and do not wander into a forest after hunting-grounds, wise one. (A variant of verse 4.) |
