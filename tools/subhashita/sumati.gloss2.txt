31 | Food eaten in anger after not following the village accountants goes sour; even a god’s chariot will not run if the axle is not greased, wise one. | 
32 | People take little account of an accountant who is mild, an elephant whose rut has stopped, a snake that does not bite, or a scorpion that does not sting, wise one. | 
33 | Biting an unripe fruit, will its sharp raw taste not remain without turning sweet? A man who goes to young girls while there are ripe young women is a beast, wise one. | 
34 | A poem by a non-poet, the love of women without the nine sentiments, and skill in many weapons that cannot shoot a pursuing boar — all are in vain, wise one. | 
35 | Bad company does no good; Fame does not leave once she has embraced one; lending is not of any use; and women’s love is nowhere, not even a trace, wise one. | 
36 | For a libertine to long for the girl another lover has satisfied and left is surely like ants swarming eagerly on a sugar-cane husk, wise one. | 
37 | A smile without cause, a woman without a household, a pastry without filling, and a wedding without feasting are all meaningless, wise one. | 
38 | Never quarrel with your wife, nor make up a false charge; where the tears of a sweet-voiced woman fall, Fortune will not stay in the house, wise one. | 
39 | In days of affection no faults ever appear; but when affection turns sour, only faults appear, it is true, wise one. | 
40 | Harm comes from the company of a petty man: a bed is spoiled even by a tiny bug that bites, wise one. | 
41 | Even one who has read the whole Kokaśāstra, who is handsome, a moon among kings, will not win a courtesan unless he gives much money, certainly, wise one. | 
42 | A worthless son’s birth is not just a defect; it ruins the father’s virtues — as when the tip of a sugar-cane flowers, all its sweetness is spoiled, wise one. | 
43 | A woman’s trust, friendship with snakes, love of other men’s wives, the sweetness of neem, and the promises of kings are all false, wise one. | 
44 | Women, among themselves, bow at every step to a man who has wealth; seeing a man without it they laugh, “A walking corpse has come,” wise one. | 
45 | Do not brood over what has passed; never believe in your heart that women love you; and keep away from secret talks with the women of the inner palace — this is the rule, wise one. | 
46 | As an ant-hill built by ants becomes a home for snakes, so gold gathered by a fool ends up in the hands of kings, wise one. | 
47 | People who are not your kin say “We are your kin,” and with pleasing manners press themselves on you for shelter when you have plenty of wealth, wise one. | 
48 | Giving is the ornament of the hands; truthfulness is the ornament of kings on earth; justice is the ornament of everyone; modesty is the ornament of a woman, wise one. | 
49 | Rushing in haste, with no patience or restraint, does the work succeed? What was spoiled gets done when one waits and endures, wise one. | 
50 | One’s anger is one’s own enemy, one’s calm is one’s own protection, compassion is one’s kin, one’s own joy is heaven, and one’s own sorrow is hell, they say; it is certain, wise one. | 
51 | No one praises in his heart his own village ascetic’s penance, his own son’s learning, his own wife’s beauty or his own backyard plant’s medicine, wise one. | 
52 | One’s own wealth is Indra’s pleasure, one’s own poverty the poverty of heaven, one’s own death the flood of dissolution, and the woman one loves is Rambhā — certainly, wise one. | 
53 | A person should not stay in a place with no kin, a place with hardly any people, a place of quarrels, or a place of suspicion, on this earth, wise one. | 
54 | A mouth without betel, the distress of friendship with the hostile, a pool without lotuses and a night without the moon are poor things, wise one. | 
55 | The snake has poison in its head, the scorpion in its tail; the wicked man has poison from head to foot, with no head or tail to it, wise one. | 
56 | There is no truth in the courtesan on whom you have poured wealth from head to foot; do not trust a courtesan who swears with her head touched, listen, wise one. | 
57 | Even beloved husbands are loathed by good wives when their hair, body and clothes are dirty — it is firmly so on earth, wise one. | 
58 | Wealth one does not eat oneself reaches kings in part, and part goes underground — like honey that flies gather in the forest which goes to another, certainly, wise one. | 
59 | A king who favours the words of a close tale-bearing minister and then harms his own subjects is like one who cuts down a wish-tree for charcoal, wise one. | 
60 | Even when Kubera was his friend, Śiva had to beg for alms; however much your kin have, your own fortune is yours alone, certainly, wise one. | 
61 | A kindness done to the courageous is like coconut water: it brings honour first and great happiness afterwards, in the world, wise one. | 
62 | Do not walk a road alone, do not eat lovingly at an enemy’s house, do not tie up others’ wealth, and do not speak to hurt others’ minds, wise one. | 
63 | Do not trust a tax-collector, a gambler, a doorman or a courtesan, nor a shopkeeper; and never trust the left hand on earth, wise one. |
