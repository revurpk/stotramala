51 | A good deed done with a pure heart, even if small, is not lacking: how big is the seed of a banyan tree? Hear, Vema. |
52 | Why study, why become a renouncer, why die caught in the six systems? Worship Him and know Him within yourself. Hear, Vema. |
53 | Through learning, high office, milch cows and women’s lips — those who have ceased to crave in these are the liberated. Hear, Vema. |
54 | It is not in studies, nor in the śāstras, nor in the Vedas or in differences of gods; one who looks for liberation outside cannot find it. Hear, Vema. |
55 | How can liberation come to a hypocrite who reads all the books and becomes very clever, like the dog in the ash-pit that wants to think? Hear, Vema. |
56 | A stone in the shoe, a fly in the ear, a speck in the eye, a thorn in the foot, a quarrel in the house — each is no small trouble. Hear, Vema. |
57 | How can one take up renunciation when death comes? In the end one’s first birth is lost; there is only sin and no fruit. Hear, Vema. |
58 | When a man gains knowledge his mind does not waver; compassion and God fix the mind on Him; can darkness stand before the burning sun? Hear, Vema. |
59 | Without having earned it, a gift is not obtained; blaming God is madness; why do the creatures of karma not understand karma? Hear, Vema. |
60 | Bodies are impermanent; dharma is eternal; the dharma one does gives a post that never fails; people who do not see and hear and learn are human beasts. Hear, Vema. |
61 | When one leaves the body and departs, neither wife, sons nor kinsmen go with him but only the breath; only his own good deeds go with him. Hear, Vema. |
62 | There are crowds of people who look for others’ faults; every person has faults; those who look for faults do not know their own. Hear, Vema. |
63 | Foolish people on earth trust that the wealth they gather is theirs; what they give to another is all it is, and how does the rest become theirs? Hear, Vema. |
64 | When fear and reverence are present towards parents, the poor, people who do not trust you, and rulers, one gets the fruit of this world, and prospers. Hear, Vema. |
65 | Think: parents are one’s first teachers; Pārvatī and Śiva are the supreme gurus; to call hired men “gurus” is a wrong. Hear, Vema. |
66 | Without knowledge, without understanding what is firm, the embodied wanders unsteadily in the world; the ignorant does not realise “Why wander?” Hear, Vema. |
67 | Can liberation be found through a thief’s cleverness? One should not do what one cannot; do not call one blind but call him lacking in quality. Hear, Vema. |
68 | Can liberation be had by deceitful talk? The talk of the incapable never counts; the fault is not the guru’s but a lack of quality in oneself. Hear, Vema. |
69 | Charity, righteousness, kindness, truth, justice, humility, courage, heroism, generosity — for a king these are royal yogas. Hear, Vema. |
70 | A man who, unable to bring himself to give, gathers great wealth and buries it, and in the end pays a fine and loses even his capital. Hear, Vema. |
71 | Of gifts the gift of food is greatest; of songs the Sāma chant is most pleasing; of meditations meditation on Śiva is best. Hear, Vema. |
72 | Without making clear the dress and speech of the lands, while God is within the self, people quarrel and tangle; look at the disguise — it is for the sake of a meal. Hear, Vema. |
73 | He who heaps up money and does no good with it, hides it and does not eat it himself, is like a bee that collects honey that goes to a passer-by. Hear, Vema. |
74 | Without money, courage does not last; there is no wealth without courage; wealth is courage for kings on earth. Hear, Vema. |
75 | Poverty is a forest-fire: it ruins the man himself and ruins those nearby; poverty, if you consider it, is a sin. Hear, Vema. |
76 | The man bound to karma who does not keep to righteousness and truth cannot see his own good; the pure-hearted man lives in His form. Hear, Vema. |
77 | Knowing truth and falsehood does not make one eternal; keeping the word safe within speech and a drop (of seed) safe, one finds joy. Hear, Vema. |
78 | One must know truth in order and become eternal; guard the word within speech; look at who is speaking, and consider. Hear, Vema. |
79 | The whole world blames the man who speaks the truth; do not tell the truth to a base man; speak truth to the truly great. Hear, Vema. |
80 | Why bathe in the water, why tread on treasures, why bow to the gods, while deceit and impurity are in the belly? Hear, Vema. |
81 | A crocodile in water can seize an elephant; out of it, even a dog defeats him; it is the strength of the place, not his own. Hear, Vema. |
82 | Washing it with water, always examining it, giving it food and clothing it — what pains people take for this body! Hear, Vema. |
83 | See the strength of boats on water, of carts on dry land, and of a woman in her speech. Hear, Vema. |
84 | Sin and virtue, grown thick, are known to the yogi on earth; do they not forge metals by seeing the inner temper? Hear, Vema. |
85 | The mouth of the man who always says what is dear and true on earth is a lake of nectar, without equal; the mouth that speaks falsehood daily is no mouth but a pit below. Hear, Vema. |
86 | Milk, sugar and plantains poured on a bitter fruit give no taste; how can crooked people have good qualities? Hear, Vema. |
87 | Consider: the mind is a treasure beyond worship; the heart is firmer than speech; character matters more than caste. Hear, Vema. |
88 | One who knows how to talk, melts the heart and does not speak lovingly — will money come to him from another for nothing? Hear, Vema. |
89 | One can speak; one cannot hold the mind still; one can teach; one cannot know oneself; one can hold a knife; one cannot be a hero. Hear, Vema. |
90 | Why blame an outcaste again and again? Is not the flesh and blood of his body the same? What caste, then, is the man who lives with him? Hear, Vema. |
91 | With foolish anger and the pursuit of delusion, cruelty grows great; in the end people bow to harshness and to Death. Hear, Vema. |
92 | Worship with heart pleases the foe of Love; when the mind is still one becomes great; worship without a heart is like clay. Hear, Vema. |
93 | Those corrupt people who take the form of Rudra and blame Rudra, walk the path of the fallen, like women who wander in adultery. Hear, Vema. |
94 | In times of lack the mind thinks of lack, like a tigress with cubs which knows nothing; what wealth can come to a hard mind? Hear, Vema. |
95 | It is not Vedic learning, nor martial skill, nor the learning of oppression, nor breadth — the learning that steadies a sweet mind is true learning. Hear, Vema. |
96 | Whether a madman, a masquerader, a sick man or a great yogi — the mind delights as soon as it sees women. Hear, Vema. |
97 | A twice-born man on earth, wearing the form of a brahmin, thinks he is not low in nature; when trouble comes his twice-born state disappears. Hear, Vema. |
98 | Never trust a masquerader; the fault of disguise is of one kind — was not Rāvaṇa’s disguise once disgraceful? Hear, Vema. |
99 | Where there is truth there is knowledge; where there is knowledge there is truth; one who has knowledge and truth in equal measure is a true twice-born. Hear, Vema. |
100 | “I am a Somayājin,” he says, dressed finely, and burns up the flesh he eats; what radiance of a twice-born is there in him? Hear, Vema. |
