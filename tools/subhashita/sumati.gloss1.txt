1 | By the grace of Śrī Rāma, so that all people may cry “Wonderful!”, I shall tell, O wise one, rich counsels of conduct that make the mouth water with their savour. | 
2 | A kinsman who is no use in need, a god who gives no boon when worshipped, a horse that will not run when ridden into battle — drop these at once, O wise one. | 
3 | Better to plough a field with a pair of strong bullocks and live by it than to serve a proud, arrogant lord who will not pay the wages one asks, and so wither. | 
4 | Do not serve at hopeless courts, do not make a temple-festival for a jewel’s sake, do not keep close friendship with the wicked, and do not wander into a forest after hunting-grounds, wise one. | 
5 | A man who, with lips moving or not, gives up sweet words and takes a vow of silence, sick with the disease of authority — he is like a deaf and blind corpse; it is a pity to see. | 
6 | Wealth made by borrowing, a young wife in old age, a fool’s austerity, a king’s rule that knows no right and wrong — these are all troublesome and bring harm in the end, wise one. | 
7 | A moneylender, a physician, a never-failing stream, and a brahmin — live in a town where you find these; never enter a town where they are lacking, wise one. | 
8 | A son-in-law’s goodness, a farmer’s love of literature, a woman’s truthfulness, rice pounded from husk, and white crows — none of these exist; know it, wise one. | 
9 | Food when hungry is nectar; he who gives without being asked is the true giver on earth; he who bears insult is a real man; he who is bold is the jewel of his family, wise one. | 
10 | A belly that is never satisfied, a courtesan’s trade that never ends, a well whose water is thick with weeds, and a goat-herd’s milk — all are loathsome in the world, wise one. | 
11 | Learning that gives to others, courage that enters the battle, what the great poets praise as worth learning, and what comes to harm in the world — know these, wise one. | 
12 | The mouth that does not read cheerfully, that does not call “Mother!” to ask for food, and that does not call its own brothers — is like a potter’s dug-out pit, wise one. | 
13 | Does not the monitor lizard live a hundred years, does not the snake lie for ten hundred, does not the crane stay in the pool? A man must be bent on the aims of life, wise one. | 
14 | Can good qualities ever come to a base man in any way? Does brass become gold if melted and poured, wise one? | 
15 | A horse that drinks water, an elephant swelling with rut, a bull beside a cow — do not go near those, nor near an ignorant base man, wise one. | 
16 | Returning a kindness to one who has done a kindness is not unusual; the skilful one is he who does good to one who has done him harm, with no thought of pretext, wise one. | 
17 | Like sugar-cane, sweet at the beginning and with knots further on, the company of the deceitful low-born looks for faults in the end, wise one. | 
18 | Say whatever is fitting for the occasion, without hurting others’ minds or being hurt oneself, and slip away: blessed is the man who goes about so, wise one. | 
19 | Never serve a man who is always looking for faults: it is like a frog living in the shade of a cobra’s hood, wise one. | 
20 | Whenever one has wealth, kinsfolk arrive; it is like ten thousand frogs gathering when a tank fills to the brim, wise one. | 
21 | Do not climb unripe fruit trees, do not blame the faults of kinsfolk, do not run away in battle, do not disobey your teacher’s command in this world, wise one. | 
22 | If one village has only one accountant and one judge it is well; when there are many, will it not be all confusion and everything be ruined? So it is natural, wise one. | 
23 | Leave a wife who thinks of another man; do not honour women who talk back; why keep a fearless soldier; do not often approach a woman — it is not right, wise one. | 
24 | He who will not leave a wife who is unwilling, a husband who is unwilling, or a friend who is unwilling — he is the true herdsman, for does every herdsman become a herdsman by nature? wise one. | 
25 | Boats come on carts, and boats are well loaded on those carts; as boats and carts alternate, so do wealth and poverty on earth, wise one. | 
26 | However powerful a man is, if he lets a young woman stay long in the house where she was born, he is sending her himself to the market as a prostitute, wise one. | 
27 | Seat a dog on a golden throne and crown him at an auspicious hour — will his old nature ever go, listen, wise one? | 
28 | A frog with a lame leg, a snake with a disease, a harlot-wife, a poor man in his old age — sorrow is certain for these, know it, wise one. | 
29 | Like lotuses withering when parted from water, when touched by the sun’s rays, so friends away from their own places become enemies, it is certain, wise one. | 
30 | If one trusts a village accountant, death follows and he does not survive; an accountant must not trust another accountant, and must live without revealing secrets, wise one. |
