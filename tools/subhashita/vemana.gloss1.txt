1 | The mind can be held steady only by fixing it on Śiva within, not by imagining Śiva outside. Hear, Vema, delight of the world. | 
2 | Of all gifts the gift of food is greatest; none is greater than one’s parents; and think — none above the teacher. Hear, Vema. | 
3 | By giving food a man gains great wealth and is honoured even in the world of the gods; food is Brahman, but people cannot see it. Hear, Vema. | 
4 | Do not claim greatness in a place that does not suit you; being small there is no defect — does not even a mountain look small in a mirror? Hear, Vema. | 
5 | One who goes about thinking of someone as his guru just because it occurs to him cannot know himself; he is the son of a useless slave. Hear, Vema. | 
6 | If a man of petty mind is given authority, he drives out all the great; does a dog that gnaws shoes know the sweetness of sugar-cane? Hear, Vema. | 
7 | A man who craves petty pleasures suffers great sorrow and cannot live in the happiness beyond. Hear, Vema. | 
8 | The base man always speaks with a show of noise, the good man speaks softly: does gold ring as bronze does? Hear, Vema. | 
9 | Those who break their word are without self-respect; those who know no limit are but a few; those who join to do harm are cruel, think. Hear, Vema. | 
10 | What use is conduct without a pure self? What use is cooking in an unclean pot? What use is worship of Śiva without a pure heart? Hear, Vema. | 
11 | One is not freed from the noose until desires cease; one freed is still no sage; and even a sage, till delusions leave him is not at peace. Hear, Vema. | 
12 | There is no greater sorrow than desire; without keeping the gaze steady there is no joy; and without steadying the mind there is no liberation. Hear, Vema. | 
13 | If iron breaks, the smith heats it again and again and joins it; if a mind is broken, can it be joined again? Hear, Vema. | 
14 | If one gives this body to the guru here, he will show the beyond and the marks of it, and keep the mind from going wrong, firmly. Hear, Vema. | 
15 | The knower of truth remains though ages pass; at the dissolution there is no loss of his life; and when life ends he merges in the Supreme. Hear, Vema. | 
16 | The best of the best is the knower of the truth; the middling shows miracles; the pretender who feeds his belly is the lowest. Hear, Vema. | 
17 | If a worthless son is born to a good man, he ruins the whole family — like the flower-stalk on a sugar-cane spoils all its sweetness. Hear, Vema. | 
18 | Salt and camphor look alike, but as you look closely their tastes differ; so among men the virtuous are different. Hear, Vema. | 
19 | One who respectfully serves his guru with devotion, who knows how to repeat the name within the heart, and who does austerities, becomes a yogi who knows the truth. Hear, Vema. | 
20 | He who seeks a guru in wonder does not speak falsely in haste; with a steady mind he finds freedom from sorrow. Hear, Vema. | 
21 | Those who scorn the water of the village well and run after “holy foot-water” — what fruit will they get from holy foot-waters? Hear, Vema. | 
22 | The yogi who wanders in the sun is only a wanderer; the real yogi is always in the world, and knowledge is not within the glutton’s reach. Hear, Vema. | 
23 | However much one reads and hears, a base man cannot give up his faults — can coal be made clean by washing in milk? Hear, Vema. | 
24 | How is a buffalo any less than a bull? Having no manure he wandered, and his worth fell; would anyone call a clumsy man a true yogi? Hear, Vema. | 
25 | What does it matter how many pūjās one performs anywhere? A pūjā without devotion bears no fruit; a pūjā with devotion has many causes of good. Hear, Vema. | 
26 | A man who lacks knowledge, whatever he reads, is virtuous only while he reads, like a frog sitting on a lotus. Hear, Vema. | 
27 | However many years you serve a master without understanding, you cannot live by it; it is only delusion — can one hope for milk from a barren cow? Hear, Vema. | 
28 | Worship of Śiva done with understanding is never lost; hold on from the start and never let go; can a wall be built from the top, leaving its foundation? Hear, Vema. | 
29 | To the yogi who knows the one syllable, the Beyond takes form; all forms and everything else he becomes himself. Hear, Vema. | 
30 | All is in one mind, yet people, in their confusion, cannot know it; one who knows the confusion and walks on is enough. Hear, Vema. | 
31 | Teachers are not those who teach the letter “ka” or read texts; the guru who shows the root of the path to liberation is the true guru. Hear, Vema. | 
32 | Why put on false disguise and suffer, why wander through forests and tire? Liberation is near through the example; one must see it. Hear, Vema. | 
33 | For the yogi whose mind is like camphor, the flame of the lamp of knowledge dances; the Self becomes visible, steady, forever. Hear, Vema. | 
34 | Through anger one’s dignity grows small, one suffers much; if anger is quelled, desires are fulfilled. Hear, Vema. | 
35 | Through anger one falls into the pit of hell; through anger virtue shrinks; through anger life shrinks. Hear, Vema. | 
36 | Even if one hears new teachings from a guru, can the old rules and works of ritual, yoga and the like drop away? Hear, Vema. | 
37 | Action is far from the knower; the knower is far from action in the Kali age; for the pure one who has given up action and knowledge, liberation is certain. Hear, Vema. | 
38 | Śiva knows what is false and what true; water knows low ground; a mother alone knows her son’s birth. Hear, Vema. | 
39 | If a toddy-pot is smeared with sacred ash, will the stink inside leave its bottom? Does a thread round the neck make a brahmin shine? Hear, Vema. | 
40 | The man who dresses like a yogi in saffron, wanders about seeking desires but gives none up, is a fake ascetic. Hear, Vema. | 
41 | Those who boast of caste and gotra, who swagger in learning, are slaves’ sons of the man who has gold. Hear, Vema. | 
42 | If one in a family is virtuous, the family shines through his virtue — like sandalwood in a forest. Hear, Vema. | 
43 | A man without caste flourishes by wealth; a man without wealth loses caste; wealth matters more than caste. Hear, Vema. | 
44 | No need for quarrels about high and low caste; all castes arose from the same stock; how could such talk of high and low be known? Hear, Vema. | 
45 | Through caste some, through strength some, through wealth some, through pride — people fall into vice by reading vice. Hear, Vema. | 
46 | A spoonful of milk from a sacred cow is enough; what use is a potful of an ass’s milk? A handful of food given with devotion is enough. Hear, Vema. | 
47 | Make the guru your food, virtue the wick, discipline of work the oil, and light the one lamp in the corner — look. Hear, Vema. | 
48 | Without a guru, knowledge is not found with certainty; without a king the land has no order; without learning can a guru be a great twice-born? Hear, Vema. | 
49 | Seeing the dead, knowing that death is certain, and thinking of the truth in the self, the liberated man is fearless. Hear, Vema. | 
50 | A yogi with a steady mind is with Śiva; the false yogi is like a locust; the eternal yogi is like you. Hear, Vema. |
