> suruciranavaratnapīṭhasthite susthite  ratnapadmāsane ratnasiṃhāsane śaṅkhapadmadvayopāśrite  tatra vighneśadurgāvaṭukṣetrapālairyute  mattamātaṅgakanyāsamūhānvite mañjulāmenakādyaṅganāmānite bhairavairaṣṭabhirveṣṭite  devi vāmādibhiḥ śaktibhiḥ sevite  dhātrilakṣmyādiśaktyaṣṭakaiḥ saṃyute  mātṛkāmaṇḍalairmaṇḍite  yakṣagandharvasiddhāṅganāmaṇḍalairarcite  pañcabāṇātmike  pañcabāṇena ratyā na saṃbhāvite  prītibhājā vasantena cānandite  bhaktibhājāṃ paraṃ śreyase kalpase  yogināṃ mānase dyotase  chandasāmojasā bhājase  gītavidyāvinodātitṛṣṇena kṛṣṇena saṃpūjyase  bhaktimaccetasā vedhasā stūyase  viśvahṛdyena vādyena vidyādharairgīyase  ‖ 4 ‖  
>
> *Seated on a jewelled seat, steady, on a lotus throne, a throne of jewels, attended by a pair of treasures, conch and lotus; there, accompanied by Vighneśa, Durgā, Baṭuka and Kṣetrapāla; attended by crowds of intoxicated Mātaṅga maidens; honoured by the lovely Menakā and others; surrounded by the eight Bhairavas; served by the Śaktis Vāmā and the rest; joined by the eight Śaktis beginning with Dhātrī and Lakṣmī; adorned with the circles of Mothers; worshipped by hosts of yakṣa, gandharva and siddha women; made of the five arrows; not (merely) esteemed by Love with Rati; gladdened by Spring, who has Prīti at his side; you bring supreme good to your devotees; you shine in the mind of yogis; you gleam with the power of the metres; you are worshipped by Kṛṣṇa, who delights in the arts of song; praised by Brahmā, whose mind is full of devotion; sung by the Vidyādharas with the music loved by the whole world.*

**Issue.** The page reads pañcabāṇena ratyā na saṃbhāvite (“not honoured by Love and Rati”), and the translation strains to make sense of it (“not (merely) esteemed”). The usual text is ratyā ca saṃbhāvite, “honoured by Love and by Rati”, parallel to the next phrase about Spring.

- A. Emend na to ca and translate “honoured by the five-arrowed Love and by Rati”.
- B. Keep na with the present translation.
- C. Decide when the Gita Press text is compared.

**Recommended.** A, or C if that comparison is near.

>
> *Victory, Mother — you who love to dwell in the Kadamba grove, in the midst of the forest of bilva trees and wishing-trees growing on the jewel-island that rises from the ocean of nectar; dear to the one clad in a hide, dear to all the worlds; whose crest is bound with a garland of nīpa flowers as you attentively begin the music; daughter of the Mountain; adorned with the glow of the crescent moon in your hair and with rows of dark, glossy curls; honoured by the worlds; whose eyes could be taken for clusters of flowers on the creepers of your brows, brows like the bow of Love at play; who sprinkle the nectar of speech; fair with the dot of fresh gorocanā; Surāmā, Ramā; the low notes of whose lute are deepened by the song of the female bees, excited by the fragrance of the leaf-lines of musk drawn on your cheeks, cheeks that glow in the moonlight of the rows of pearls in your ear-rings — sweet-voiced, bright; wearing earrings of palm-leaf as you play the vallakī, attended by the perfected ones; the beauty of whose rolling eyes, playful with the swell of divine wine, puts to shame the single blue lotus at your ear; granting the fruit of every wish of the worlds, Śrīphala; whose pearl nose-ring seems to be a drop of the sweat that shines on your forehead; Kālikā, soul of the whole universe; in whose noble mouth, with its innocent gentle smile, gleam bits of areca, betel and camphor; making the gesture of knowledge, giver of every prosperity, with hands bright as lotuses; with a rosy lip and pure teeth bright as jasmine; bearer of the lovely vīṇā, with lips like ripe bimba fruit.*
