### 2. [mahishasura-mardini](../../../stotra/devi/mahishasura-mardini-iast.html) · unit 4 · text
<!-- id: mahishasura-mardini:4:text:235d7e -->

> ayi śatakhaṇḍavikhaṇḍitaruṇḍavituṇḍitaśuṇḍagajādhipate  
> ripugajagaṇḍavidāraṇacaṇḍaparākramaśuṇḍamṛgādhipate  
> nijabhujadaṇḍanipātitakhaṇḍavipātitamuṇḍabhaṭādhipate  
> jaya jaya he mahiṣāsuramardini ramya kapardini śailasute  ‖ 4 ‖  
>
> *O conqueror of the elephant-king whose trunk was hewn and head split into a hundred pieces; whose lion, of fierce valour, tore with its paws the temples of the foe-elephants; who felled the warrior-hosts, their severed heads struck down and split by the staff of your own arm.*
>
> <sub>ayi = O · śata-khaṇḍa-vikhaṇḍita-ruṇḍa-vituṇḍita-śuṇḍa-gaja-adhipate = who cut the enemy's lordly elephants, trunks and trunkless bodies, into a hundred pieces · ripu-gaja-gaṇḍa-vidāraṇa-caṇḍa-parākrama-śuṇḍa-mṛga-adhipate = whose lion is fierce in valour, tearing the temples of the enemy's elephants · nija-bhuja-daṇḍa-nipātita-khaṇḍa-vipātita-muṇḍa-bhaṭa-adhipate = who with her own staff-like arms struck down the warrior-chiefs, their heads cut off and split in pieces (many texts read caṇḍa for khaṇḍa: the demons Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa) · jaya jaya = victory, victory · he = O · mahiṣa-asura-mardini = slayer of the buffalo demon · ramya-kapardini = of lovely braided hair · śaila-sute = daughter of the mountain</sub>

**Issue.** Line 3 reads nipātitakhaṇḍavipātitamuṇḍa; most texts read nipātitacaṇḍa…, naming the demons Caṇḍa and Muṇḍa.

- A. Keep khaṇḍa (as the source).
- B. Change to caṇḍa and translate with the two demons’ names.

**Recommended.** A unless your recitation text has caṇḍa; both are transmitted.

### 3. [mahishasura-mardini](../../../stotra/devi/mahishasura-mardini-iast.html) · unit 6 · text
<!-- id: mahishasura-mardini:6:text:47711b -->

> ayi śaraṇāgatavairivadhūvaravīravarābhayadānakare  
> tribhuvanamastakaśūlavirodhiśirodhikṛtāmalaśūlakare  
> dumidumitāmaradundubhinādamaho mukharīkṛtatigmakare  
> jaya jaya he mahiṣāsuramardini ramya kapardini śailasute  ‖ 6 ‖  
>
> *O you whose hand grants fearlessness even to the brave lords and husbands of the surrendered foe's women; whose hand plants the spotless trident on the heads of the foes who are a spike in the head of the three worlds; who make the very sun ring with the mighty ‘dumi-dumi’ of the gods' kettledrums.*
>
> <sub>ayi = O · śaraṇa-āgata-vairi-vadhū-vara-vīra-vara-abhaya-dāna-kare = whose hand grants freedom from fear to the noble heroes, husbands of the enemies' wives, who come for refuge · tri-bhuvana-mastaka-śūla-virodhi-śiraḥ-adhi-kṛta-amala-śūla-kare = whose hand sets her spotless trident on the heads of the foes who are a spike in the head of the three worlds · dumi-dumi-tāmara-dundubhi-nāda-mahaḥ-mukharī-kṛta-tigma-kare = who makes the sun resound with the mighty dumi-dumi of the gods' kettledrums · jaya jaya = victory, victory · he = O · mahiṣa-asura-mardini = slayer of the buffalo demon · ramya-kapardini = of lovely braided hair · śaila-sute = daughter of the mountain</sub>

**Issue.** Line 3 ends mukharīkṛtatigmakare (“who makes the sun resound”); the common text is mukharīkṛtadiṅmakare (“who fills the quarters with sound”).

- A. Keep tigmakare.
- B. Change to diṅmakare and retranslate.

**Recommended.** A unless your recitation text differs.
