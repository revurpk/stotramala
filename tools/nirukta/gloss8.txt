# English key to Nirukta adhyāya 8, one khaṇḍa a line: number | heading | gloss. Original.
1 | Draviṇodas | Draviṇodas, "giver of draviṇa", which means wealth ("what men run to") or strength ("with which they run").
2 | Who is Draviṇodas? | Ṛgveda 1.15.7: the priests, stones in hand, praise the god Draviṇodas at the sacrifices. Krauṣṭuki says Indra, the greatest giver of strength and wealth, who drinks soma. Śākapūṇi says this Agni, for the Draviṇodas sayings are in the Agni hymns, and each argument for Indra fits Agni too: he is born of strength when churned, the priests are the "givers of wealth" who beget him, and he drinks soma.
3 | The season-cup verse | Ṛgveda 2.37.3: let your bearers grow fat; firm yourself, lord of the forest; drink the soma from the neṣṭṛ's cup with the seasons, Draviṇodas. Dhiṣaṇā, vanaspati.
4 | The Āprī hymns; Idhma | The Āprī hymns, "for winning" or "for pleasing" the gods. The first of their deities is Idhma, the kindling.
5 | Idhma; Tanūnapāt | Ṛgveda 10.110.1: kindled today in the house of man, you worship the gods, Jātavedas. The kindling is the sacrificial fuel (Kātthakya) or Agni (Śākapūṇi). Tanūnapāt: the ghee, offspring of the cow (Kātthakya), or Agni, offspring of the waters through the plants (Śākapūṇi).
6 | Tanūnapāt; Narāśaṃsa | Ṛgveda 10.110.2: Tanūnapāt, anoint the paths of order with honey. Narāśaṃsa is the sacrifice, "where men sit and praise" (Kātthakya), or Agni, "praised by men" (Śākapūṇi).
7 | Narāśaṃsa; Īḷ | Ṛgveda 7.2.2: we praise the greatness of Narāśaṃsa, of the gods who enjoy both kinds of oblation. Īḷ, the praised.
8 | The Īḷ verse; barhis | Ṛgveda 10.110.3: invoked and praised, come, Agni, with the Vasus; you are the great hotṛ of the gods. Yahva, yajīyas. Barhis, the sacred grass.
9 | The sacred grass; the doors | Ṛgveda 10.110.4: the grass is strewn eastward at dawn, spread wide, a soft seat for the gods and Aditi. Syona, "pleasant". The doors.
10 | The divine doors; Uṣāsānaktā | Ṛgveda 10.110.5: let the wide doors open like wives adorning themselves for their husbands. The doors of the house (Kātthakya) or Agni (Śākapūṇi). Uṣāsānaktā, dawn and night.
11 | Dawn and night; the two divine priests | Ṛgveda 10.110.6: let dawn and night sit in their place, divine women, bright, bearing beauty. Śukra, peśas. The two divine hotṛs: this Agni and the Agni of the mid-air.
12 | The divine hotṛs; the three goddesses | Ṛgveda 10.110.7: the two divine priests, first, fair-spoken, measuring out the sacrifice. The three goddesses.
13 | Bhāratī, Iḷā, Sarasvatī; Tvaṣṭṛ | Ṛgveda 10.110.8: let Bhāratī come quickly, Iḷā awakening us, and Sarasvatī; let the three goddesses sit on the grass. Tvaṣṭṛ, "quickly pervading", or from "shine" or "fashion".
14 | Tvaṣṭṛ | Ṛgveda 10.110.9: Tvaṣṭṛ, who adorned heaven and earth, the begetters, and all the worlds with forms. Tvaṣṭṛ is of the mid-air, some say; Agni, says Śākapūṇi.
15 | Tvaṣṭṛ's son | Ṛgveda 1.95.5: he grows bright in them; both feared Tvaṣṭṛ as he was born, and turning, they welcomed the lion: heaven and earth, day and night, or the two fire-sticks.
16 | Vanaspati | Vanaspati, the lord of the forest, already explained.
17 | The lord of the forest | Ṛgveda 10.110.10: release the oblations to the gods in season; let Vanaspati, the Śamitṛ and Agni sweeten the offering with honey and ghee. Vanaspati is the sacrificial post (Kātthakya) or Agni (Śākapūṇi).
18 | Anointing the post | Ṛgveda 3.8.1: the god-seekers anoint you, lord of the forest, with heavenly honey; whether you stand upright or rest in your mother's lap, give us wealth.
19 | The gold-leaved one | A verse: carry the oblations to the gods, golden-leaved Vanaspati, by the straightest paths.
20 | Bound with the girdle; the svāhā-calls | Another verse: Vanaspati, bound with the fairest girdle, carry the oblations to the gods and proclaim the giver among the immortals. Svāhākṛti, "well said", "his own speech", or "well offered".
21 | The svāhā verse; the prayājas and anuyājas | Ṛgveda 10.110.11: born at once, Agni measured out the sacrifice and became leader of the gods; let the gods eat the oblation offered with svāhā. The Āprī deities are done. To whom belong the fore- and after-offerings? To Agni, say some.
22 | The fore- and after-offerings; the eleven Āprī hymns | Ṛgveda 10.51.8–9 and the Brāhmaṇas assign them to Agni, the metres, the seasons, the cattle, the breaths or the self; Agni is the rule, the rest figure. Of the eleven Āprī hymns, four have Narāśaṃsa, three both, the rest Tanūnapāt.
