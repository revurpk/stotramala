# English key to Nirukta adhyāya 12, one khaṇḍa a line: number | heading | gloss. Original.
1 | The deities of the sky; the Aśvins | Now the deities of the sky, first the Aśvins, "who pervade all", one with juice, one with light, or (Aurṇavābha) "having horses". Who are they? Heaven and earth, day and night, sun and moon, or two holy kings (the story-tellers). Their time is after midnight, as light begins to break.
2 | The two of one time | Ṛgveda 5.73.2 (or a like verse): you two wander among the dwellings like the black pair; when did you come to the gods? Half a verse treats them apart: one is called Vāsatya, the other son of Dawn.
3 | Born here and there | Ṛgveda 1.181.4: born here and there, they are praised with spotless bodies and names; the victorious one is the middle god, the other the fortunate son of the sky, the sun.
4 | The early-yoked | Ṛgveda 1.22.1: wake the two early-yoked Aśvins; let them come to drink this soma.
5 | Worship them at dawn; Uṣas | Ṛgveda 5.77.2: worship the Aśvins at dawn; the evening is not for the gods. Their time lasts till sunrise. Uṣas, dawn, "the longing one"; the other dawn, of the mid-air, from "to shine".
6 | Dawn | Ṛgveda 1.92.13: bring us that bright wealth, dawn, by which we may nourish sons and grandsons.
7 | The dawns; Sūryā | Ṛgveda 1.92.1: these dawns have raised their banner in the eastern half of the sky. The plural honours one. Sūryā, the sun's wife.
8 | Sūryā's bridal car; Vṛṣākapāyī | Ṛgveda 10.85.20: mount, Sūryā, the fair car of śalmali wood, golden, many-formed; make your husband a happy bridal. "Savitṛ gave Sūryā to King Soma or to Prajāpati". Vṛṣākapāyī, Vṛṣākapi's wife.
9 | Vṛṣākapāyī; Saraṇyū | Ṛgveda 10.86.13: rich Vṛṣākapāyī with good sons and daughters-in-law; let Indra eat your bulls. Snuṣā, ukṣan. Saraṇyū.
10 | Saraṇyū and the story of her twins | Ṛgveda 10.17.2: they hid the immortal from mortals and gave her likeness to Vivasvat; she bore the Aśvins and left both pairs. The story: Saraṇyū, Tvaṣṭṛ's daughter, bore twins to Vivasvat, left a likeness in her place and fled as a mare; Vivasvat followed as a horse; the Aśvins were born, and Manu of the likeness.
11 | Tvaṣṭṛ's daughter's wedding | Ṛgveda 10.17.1: Tvaṣṭṛ makes a wedding for his daughter, and all the world comes; Yama's mother, being wed, vanished: night, which vanishes at sunrise.
12 | Savitṛ | Savitṛ's time is when the sky is cleared of darkness and filled with rays.
13 | Savitṛ who puts on all forms; Bhaga | Ṛgveda 5.81.2: the poet puts on all forms, brings good to two-footed and four-footed; Savitṛ lit the vault. The animal lists: the black-bellied ram and the cock belong to Savitṛ, and why. Bhaga, whose time is before sunrise.
14 | Bhaga; Sūrya | Ṛgveda 7.41.2: let us call Bhaga the early winner, son of Aditi, whom poor and king alike pray to for a share. Tura, Yama. "Bhaga is blind"; "Bhaga goes to the people". Sūrya, the sun.
15 | The sun's banners | Ṛgveda 1.50.1: his rays carry up the god Jātavedas, the sun, for all to see.
16 | The bright face of the gods; Pūṣan | Ṛgveda 1.115.1: the bright face of the gods has risen, the eye of Mitra, Varuṇa, Agni; the sun is the self of all that moves and stands. When he nourishes the rays, he is Pūṣan.
17 | Pūṣan's two forms | Ṛgveda 6.58.1: one form of yours is bright, one worshipful; you are like the sky in day and night; may your gift be favourable here, Pūṣan.
18 | Pūṣan, lord of the paths; Viṣṇu | Ṛgveda 6.49.8: Pūṣan, lord of every path; may he give bright riches and further every thought. When he pervades, he is Viṣṇu.
19 | Viṣṇu's three steps | Ṛgveda 1.22.17: Viṣṇu strode over this; three times he set his foot; it is hidden in his dust. On earth, mid-air and sky (Śākapūṇi), or at the rising, the zenith and Gayaśiras (Aurṇavābha); pāṃsu.
20 | Viśvānara | Viśvānara appears in passing in a verse to Indra (Ṛgveda 8.68.4).
21 | Viśvānara's lord; Varuṇa | Ṛgveda 8.68.4: I call the lord of Viśvānara's unbending might for help. Varuṇa.
22 | The eye with which you see | Ṛgveda 1.50.6: with that eye, purifier, with which you look on the busy people, Varuṇa; the sentence is completed "we praise it". Bhuraṇyu. Or with the next verse.
23 | With the next verse | Ṛgveda 1.50.7: you cross the sky and the wide region, measuring the days with the nights, watching the generations, sun.
24 | With the previous verse | Ṛgveda 1.50.5: you rise facing the clans of gods and men, facing all, that the sky be seen.
25 | Or by itself; Keśin | Or the verse stands alone: "with it you look on our people". Keśin, "the long-haired", the sun with his rays.
26 | Keśin | Ṛgveda 10.136.1: the long-haired holds fire, the long-haired holds water, the long-haired holds heaven and earth; he is called this light. The other two lights too are long-haired, Agni with smoke, the middle god with dust.
27 | The three long-haired ones; Vṛṣākapi | Ṛgveda 1.164.44: three long-haired ones appear in season: one shears the earth in the year (Agni), one looks on all with powers (the sun), the motion of one is seen, not his form (the middle god). Vṛṣākapi, the sun shaking with his rays.
28 | Vṛṣākapi; Yama | Ṛgveda 10.86.21: come again, Vṛṣākapi; you who destroy sleep go home by the path again. Yama.
29 | Yama under the leafy tree; Aja Ekapād | Ṛgveda 10.135.1: under the leafy tree where Yama drinks with the gods, our father, lord of the clans, longs for the ancients. Aja Ekapād, "the one-footed unborn".
30 | Aja Ekapād; Pṛthivī | Ṛgveda 10.65.13: Pāvīravī the thunderer, Aja Ekapād upholder of the sky, the river, the waters: let all the gods hear my words. Pavi, pavīravat. The earth, in an Indra-Agni verse.
31 | The highest earth; Samudra | Ṛgveda 1.108.9: whether, Indra and Agni, you are on the highest, middle or lowest earth, come from there. Samudra, in a verse of the Pavamāna book.
32 | The ocean | Ṛgveda 9.73.3: the bright ones sit round speech; their ancient father guards the law; Varuṇa hid the great ocean.
33 | Ahi Budhnya, Aja Ekapād, earth, ocean together; Dadhyañc | Ṛgveda 10.64.4 (or a like verse): may Ahi Budhnya hear us, Aja Ekapād, earth and ocean, all the gods. Dadhyañc, "toward whom meditation turns". Atharvan, Manu.
34 | Atharvan, Manu and Dadhyañc | Ṛgveda 1.80.16: the thought that Atharvan, father Manu and Dadhyañc spread; in it the prayers and hymns met with Indra as of old.
35 | The groups of the sky; the Ādityas | Now the groups of gods of the sky, first the Ādityas. Ṛgveda 2.27.1: I offer these songs dripping ghee to the Ādityas; let Mitra, Aryaman, Bhaga, Varuṇa, Dakṣa and Aṃśa hear.
36 | The seven seers | The seven seers, already explained.
37 | The seven seers in the body | Vājasaneyi 34.55: seven seers are set in the body and guard it unwearied: the rays in the sun, or the six senses and the mind in the self; two gods watch while it sleeps: wind and sun, or the knowing self and the radiant self.
38 | The cup with its opening sideways | Atharvaveda-like verse: a cup with its opening sideways and its base above, in which all glory is set; there sit the seven seers: the sun with its rays, or the head with the senses. The gods.
39 | The gods' favour; the Viśvedevas | Ṛgveda 1.89.2: may the kind favour of the gods be with us; may the gods lengthen our life. The Viśvedevas, all the gods.
40 | The All-gods; the Sādhyas | Ṛgveda 1.3.7: come, All-gods, helpers, upholders of men. The one Vaiśvadeva triplet in the ten books; Śākapūṇi on what counts as Vaiśvadeva; the unlabelled two-pāda verses and others. The Sādhyas.
41 | The Sādhyas; the Vasus | Ṛgveda 10.90.16: with sacrifice the gods sacrificed to sacrifice; those were the first laws; the great ones reached the vault where the former Sādhyas are. A group of the sky, say the etymologists; the former age of gods, say the story-tellers. The Vasus of earth, mid-air and sky.
42 | The Vasus | Vājasaneyi verse: we have made easy paths for you, gods; eat and drink and give us wealth, Vasus.
43 | The Vasus at play; the Vājins | Ṛgveda 7.39.3: the bright Vasus played here on earth and in the wide mid-air; make your paths toward us. The Vājins.
44 | The Vājins; the gods' wives | Ṛgveda 7.38.7: may the swift Vājins be kind to us at our calls, crushing the serpent, wolf and demons. The wives of the gods.
45 | The gods' wives | Ṛgveda 5.46.7: may the eager wives of the gods help us, those of the earth and of the waters; give us shelter, goddesses.
46 | Indrāṇī, Agnāyī, Aśvinī, Rāṭ, Rodasī, Varuṇānī | Ṛgveda 5.46.8: may the divine wives come, Indrāṇī, Agnāyī, Aśvinī, Rāṭ, Rodasī, Varuṇānī. The Daivata-kāṇḍa and the second half end.
