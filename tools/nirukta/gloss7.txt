# English key to Nirukta adhyāya 7, one khaṇḍa a line: number | heading | gloss. Original.
1 | The Daivata-kāṇḍa begins: what makes a deity | The second half opens with the deities. A mantra belongs to the deity whom the seer, wishing for something, praises. Hymns are of three kinds: addressed in the third person, addressed directly, and spoken by the deity of itself; the first are used with every case of the noun and the third person of the verb.
2 | Direct address and self-praise | Examples of the third-person mantras of Indra, of direct address ("you, Indra"), of praisers addressed directly with the praised in the third person, and of hymns in the first person: Indra Vaikuṇṭha, the Lava hymn, the speech of Vāc daughter of Ambhṛṇa.
3 | What the mantras do | Most mantras are of the first two kinds, few of the third. Some only praise, some only pray ("may I have good eyes"), some swear or curse, some describe a state ("there was neither death nor immortality"), some lament, some blame and praise (the gambler's hymn). So the seers saw mantras with many purposes.
4 | Mantras without a named deity; the one self | Where a mantra names no deity, it belongs to the deity of its sacrifice; outside the sacrifice, to Prajāpati (the ritualists) or to Narāśaṃsa (the etymologists), or to the deity wished for. Things not gods are praised as gods. Because of the deity's greatness the one self is praised in many ways; the other gods are its limbs; its chariot, horse, weapons and arrows are the self.
5 | Three deities | There are only three deities, say the etymologists: Agni on earth, Vāyu or Indra in the mid-air, Sūrya in the sky; each has many names through greatness or through its different works, as one man is hotṛ, adhvaryu, brahman and udgātṛ. Or they are separate, since their praises and names differ; places and enjoyments are shared, as in one kingdom.
6 | The form of the gods: like men | Are the gods shaped like men? Yes, for they are praised as conscious, with human limbs, possessions (horses, wives, homes) and acts (eating, drinking, hearing).
7 | Or not like men | No: what is seen of them (fire, wind, sun, earth, moon) is not human, and unconscious things too, the pressing-stones and the rivers, are praised with limbs, possessions and acts. Or both; or the forms are the works of beings shaped like men, as the sacrifice is the sacrificer's. So the tradition.
8 | What belongs to Agni | The three deities' associations. Agni's: this world, the morning pressing, spring, the gāyatrī, the trivṛt stoma, the rathantara sāman, the gods of the first place and the goddesses Agnāyī, Pṛthivī, Iḷā. His work: carrying the oblations and bringing the gods. His companions in praise: Indra, Soma, Varuṇa, Parjanya, the seasons.
9 | Pūṣan and Agni in the funeral verse | Ṛgveda 10.17.3: may Pūṣan who knows, whose cattle are never lost, guardian of the world, lead you on; may he hand you to the fathers, Agni to the gods. Whether the third pāda refers to Pūṣan or to Agni. Suvidatra.
10 | What belongs to Indra | Indra's: the mid-air, the midday pressing, summer, the triṣṭubh, the fifteenfold stoma, the bṛhat sāman, the gods and goddesses of the middle place. His work: releasing the waters and killing Vṛtra, and every deed of strength. His companions: Agni, Soma, Varuṇa, Pūṣan, Bṛhaspati, Brahmaṇaspati, Parvata, Kutsa, Viṣṇu, Vāyu.
11 | What belongs to the sun | The sun's: the yonder world, the third pressing, the rains, the jagatī, the seventeenfold stoma, the vairūpa sāman, the gods and goddesses of the highest place. His work: taking up the juices and holding them in his rays. Autumn, winter and the cool season with their metres, stomas and sāmans are assigned to earth, mid-air and sky in turn.
12 | Mantra, chandas and the metres | Mantra "from thinking", chandas "from covering", yajus, sāman; the metres derived: gāyatrī, uṣṇih, kakubh, anuṣṭubh, bṛhatī, paṅkti, triṣṭubh.
13 | Jagatī, virāj; incidental names | Jagatī and virāj derived. Most deities have hymns, oblations and verses; some appear only incidentally. Epithets like "Vṛtra-slayer" are not separate deities but marks of the one named, as one says "give rice to the hungry Brāhmaṇa".
14 | Agni | The enumeration begins with Agni, the deity of the earth: "the leader" (agraṇī), led first in the sacrifice; Sthaulāṣṭhīvi's "not drying"; Śākapūṇi's three roots.
15 | The first verse of the Ṛgveda | Ṛgveda 1.1.1: I praise Agni, the purohita, god of the sacrifice, the priest, the hotṛ, best giver of treasure. Īḷe, deva ("from giving, shining, or dwelling in the sky"), hotṛ, ratnadhātama.
16 | Agni praised by old and new seers | Ṛgveda 1.1.2: Agni, to be praised by the former seers and the new, may he bring the gods here. Not only this fire: the two higher lights are also called Agni, as in the next verse.
17 | Agni of the mid-air | Ṛgveda 4.58.8: the streams of ghee flow to Agni like smiling women; Agni of the middle region. The sun too: "Agni is all the gods".
18 | The one called by many names | Ṛgveda 1.164.46: they call him Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Agni, and the heavenly bird Garutmat; the wise speak of the one being in many ways. The Agni who has the hymn and the oblation is this one; the higher lights share the name only incidentally.
19 | Jātavedas | Jātavedas: "who knows all born things", "whom the born know", "found in all born things", "possessed of wealth or wisdom from birth"; the Brāhmaṇa's derivation from finding the cattle.
20 | The Jātavedas verse; the sun as Jātavedas | In brackets, Ṛgveda 1.99.1: let us press soma for Jātavedas; may he carry us over all difficulties as a ship over a river. Ṛgveda 10.188.1: urge on Jātavedas like a swift horse. The Jātavedas hymns of the ten books are this Agni's; the sun is Jātavedas only incidentally.
21 | Vaiśvānara | Vaiśvānara: "who leads all men", "whom all men lead", or Viśvānara himself, entered into all beings.
22 | Who is Vaiśvānara? | Ṛgveda 1.98.1: may we be in Vaiśvānara's favour; born from here, he looks on all this and vies with the sun. The teachers say Vaiśvānara is the god of the mid-air, since he is praised for rain.
23 | The case for the mid-air or the sun, and Śākapūṇi's answer | Ṛgveda 1.59.6 on Vaiśvānara slaying the Dasyu and Śambara. The older ritualists take him for the sun, citing the order of the rite, the twelve-potsherd cake and the Brāhmaṇa; Śākapūṇi holds he is this fire, born of the other two lights (lightning, and the sun's rays focused through a crystal).
24 | The arguments answered | Each argument answered: Vaiśvānara is praised in the Agni hymns, never for rising and setting; even the rain verse (Ṛgveda 1.164.47) and the Brāhmaṇa fit this fire.
25 | Haviṣpānta | Ṛgveda 10.88.1: the oblation was offered in the fire that reaches heaven; the gods spread it out with food for upholding and sustaining.
26 | Mātariśvan brings Vaiśvānara | Ṛgveda 6.8.4: in the lap of the waters the great ones seized him; Mātariśvan brought Vaiśvānara from afar, from Vivasvat. Vivasvat, Mātariśvan.
27 | Head of the world by night | Ṛgveda 10.88.6: Agni is the head of the world by night; from him the sun is born at dawn.
28 | Agni made threefold | Ṛgveda 10.88.10: the gods made Agni threefold, on earth, in the mid-air and in the sky; his third form is the sun.
29 | Agni as the sun | Ṛgveda 10.88.11: when the gods set him in the sky as the sun, son of Aditi, and the pair (dawn and sun) appeared. Mithuna derived.
30 | The two divine priests | Ṛgveda 10.88.17: when the lower and the higher priest dispute which knows more, who will decide?
31 | The Brāhmaṇa priest sits below | Ṛgveda 10.88.19: as long as the dawns are clothed in light, the Brāhmaṇa priest sits below the hotṛ. The hotṛ's prayer naming Savitṛ and "father Vaiśvānara". The Vaiśvānara of the hymns is this fire.
