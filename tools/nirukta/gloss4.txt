# English key to Nirukta adhyāya 4, one khaṇḍa a line: number | heading | gloss. Original.
1 | The Naigama-kāṇḍa begins; jahā | The synonyms are done; now come words of one form with several senses, and words of the hymns whose formation is unclear: the "single words" (aikapadika). Jahā means "he struck".
2 | Jahā, mamarya, nidhā | Ṛgveda 8.45.37: what friend, unprovoked, has struck his friend? Maryā (men, or limits) and methati ("to revile"). Nidhā is a net, a mass of snares.
3 | Nidhā; śitāma | Ṛgveda 10.73.11: the birds came to Indra asking to be freed as from a net; cakṣus. Śitāma in "from the sides, from the hips, from the śitāma": the arm, the womb, the liver or the fat, as various teachers hold.
4 | Mehanā; damūnas | Ṛgveda 5.39.1: whatever bright wealth there is, Indra, bring it with both hands. Mehanā read as "what is with you" or as three words; adri, rādhas. Damūnas: "of tamed mind", "of giving mind", or "minded to the house".
5 | Damūnas, atithi, duroṇa; mūṣ | Ṛgveda 5.4.5: Agni, the welcome house-friend and guest, come to our sacrifice. Atithi, duroṇa. Mūṣ, a mouse.
6 | Trita in the well | Ṛgveda 1.105.8: the ribs torment me like rival wives, cares gnaw me as mice gnaw threads. The hymn came to Trita lying in a well; Trita, "the most outstanding in wisdom", or the third of three brothers.
7 | Iṣira; the suffix -tana; jaṭhara | Ṛgveda 8.48.7: with eager mind may we enjoy the soma as ancestral wealth. Vāsara. The meaningless additions in kartana, hantana, yātana. Jaṭhara, the belly.
8 | Jaṭhara in use | Ṛgveda 3.47.1: drink the soma, Indra, with the Maruts; pour the wave of sweetness into your belly. Madhu.
9 | Titau, the sieve | Titau is a sieve for winnowing: "stretched", "pierced", or "pierced with holes the size of sesame".
10 | The sieve of speech | Ṛgveda 10.71.2: where the wise fashioned speech with the mind as if cleaning barley with a sieve, there friends know friendship, and good fortune is set in their speech. Saktu, bhadra, and the many derivations of lakṣmī.
11 | Simasmai; the sun's work | Ṛgveda 1.115.4: this is the sun's godhood, that he draws in the work left spread out; when he yokes his bays, night spreads her garment over all.
12 | Mandū | Ṛgveda 1.6.7: you are seen joined with the fearless Indra, both gladdening, of equal splendour.
13 | The horses of the sun | Ṛgveda 1.163.10: slim-flanked, broad in the middle, heroic, the heavenly coursers fly in line like swans. Haṃsa, śreṇi; "from the sun you fashioned the horse".
14 | Kāyamāna; lodha; śīra | Ṛgveda 3.9.2: Agni, desiring the woods, you went to the waters, your mothers. Lodha, the greedy seer led off like a beast; śīra, "lying in", or "eating".
15 | Two maidens on new stands | Ṛgveda 4.32.23: like two maidens on new wooden stands, the two bays shine on their courses. Kanyā, dāru; suvāstu and tugva; kuvit nasante.
16 | Śundhyu, nodhas, admasad | Ṛgveda 1.124.4: Dawn has shown her breast like the bright one; like Nodhas she made her treasures plain. Śundhyu: the sun, a water-bird, the waters. Vāśī, "speech"; paritakmyā is left for later.
17 | Suvita; dayati; nū cit | Suvita, "well-gone", or "in offspring". Dayati in several senses: to give, divide, burn, destroy. Nū cit, "of old and now".
18 | Akūpāra; śiśīte | Akūpāra: the sun, the ocean, and the tortoise, "whose bounds are far". Kacchapa. Śiśīte, "he sharpens"; rakṣas; sutuka; suprāyaṇa.
19 | Aprāyu; Cyavana; rajas and haras; viṣuṇa | The gods are tireless guardians; Cyavana made young again like a chariot. Rajas and haras both mean light, water, the worlds, and blood and day. Juhure, vyanta, vīti, usriyā, hari, and Ṛgveda 7.21.5 against the phallus-worshippers.
20 | Jāmi | Ṛgveda 10.10.10: there will come later ages when sisters do what is not fit for sisters; seek another husband than me. Jāmi.
21 | Heaven my father, earth my mother | Ṛgveda 1.164.33: heaven is my father, here is my navel; earth is my mother. Bandhu, nābhi, the kin (sanābhi). Śaṃyu, "wishing happiness"; ares and rapas.
22 | Aditi | Aditi, the undiminished mother of the gods.
23 | Aditi is everything | Ṛgveda 1.89.10: Aditi is heaven, mid-air, mother, father and son, all the gods, the five peoples, what is born and what will be born. Erire.
24 | The thief and the hawk | Ṛgveda 4.38.5: men shout after him in battle as after a cloak-thief, as after a swooping hawk. Tāyu, bhara, yūtha; mandin.
25 | The name of the cow in the moon's house | Ṛgveda 1.84.15: here they found the hidden name of Tvaṣṭṛ's cow in the house of the moon. Gātu, daṃsas, aṃhati, viyute, ṛdhak. The accent of asyā: accented on first mention, unaccented on later mention.
26 | Asya vāmasya | Ṛgveda 1.164.1: of this lovely grey priest the middle brother is the eater; the third has ghee on his back; there I saw the lord of the clans with seven sons. Bhrātṛ; the seven rays.
27 | The wheel of the year | Ṛgveda 1.164.2: seven yoke the one-wheeled chariot drawn by one horse of seven names; the wheel has three naves, unaging. The year in three, five, six and twelve parts, and its three hundred and sixty days and nights, seven hundred and twenty counted apart.
