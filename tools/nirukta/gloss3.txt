# English key to Nirukta adhyāya 3, one khaṇḍa a line: number | heading | gloss. Original.
1 | Work and offspring | The twenty-six names of work and fifteen of offspring. Apatya, "offspring", because it is spread out from the begetter, or because by it one does not fall; verses on this sense follow.
2 | No son from another | Ṛgveda 7.4.7: the wealth of a stranger is to be shunned; may we be masters of our own; there is no offspring born of another, Agni. Araṇa, rekṇas ("wealth"), śeṣa ("offspring").
3 | The adopted son | Ṛgveda 7.4.8: a stranger, however kind, is not to be taken or even thought of as one's son; he goes back to the home he came from. Oka ("home"). This verse is cited both for the daughter's inheritance and for the son's.
4 | The daughter's son; who inherits | Ṛgveda 3.31.1: the father, honouring the law, went to his daughter's son. Duhitṛ, "daughter", derived. The verses "you are born from limb after limb" and Manu's "sons and daughters inherit alike"; against them, "the man inherits, not the woman". Women may be given, sold or abandoned, not men (some say men too, citing Śunaḥśepa). The brotherless girl.
5 | The brotherless girl | Ṛgveda 1.124.7: like a brotherless girl going back to her fathers, like one mounting the stand for wealth. Garta: the seat in the assembly, the burial mound, the chariot. One should not marry a brotherless girl, for her son belongs to her father.
6 | The sister's portion | Ṛgveda 3.31.2: the son did not leave the inheritance to his sister; jāmi, "sister". Of the two children, the son carries on the line; the daughter is given to another.
7 | Men; the five peoples | The twenty-five names of man: manuṣya, "they sew their works having thought". The five peoples (pañca janāḥ), with Ṛgveda 10.53.4.
8 | Who the five peoples are; arms and fingers | Ūrj, "food". The five peoples: gandharvas, ancestors, gods, asuras and rākṣasas, say some; the four classes with the niṣāda as fifth, says Aupamanyava. The twelve names of the arms and twenty-two of the fingers, derived.
9 | The fingers as charioteers; strength, wealth, battle | Ṛgveda 10.94.7: to the ten fingers like reins, girths and yokes. Then the lists of desire, food, eating, strength, wealth, cows, anger, motion (a hundred and twenty-two), swiftness, nearness, and battle (forty-six); khala, "battle".
10 | The numbers; Indra alone against many | Ṛgveda 10.48.7: I overcome them one by one; what can two or three do to me? The numbers from one to a million derived; arbuda, "cloud". Verbs of pervading and of killing; ākhaṇḍala; taḷit, "near" and "striking".
11 | Far and near; thunderbolt; lord | Ṛgveda 2.23.9 with taḷitaḥ; Śākapūṇi takes taḷit as lightning, but it means "near": "though far, you shine as if near". The eighteen names of the thunderbolt (kutsa among them); four verbs of ruling and four names of the lord; ina.
12 | The birds and the wise lord | Ṛgveda 1.164.21: where the fair-winged ones sing unceasingly of their share of immortality, there the lord of all the world, the wise, entered me, the simple one. Read of the sun's rays (adhidaivata) and of the senses and the self (adhyātma).
13 | Many, small, great … ; comparison | The lists of many, small, great, house, worship, happiness, form, praiseworthy, wisdom, truth, seeing, and the mixed words. Comparison: Gārgya's definition; the greater compared with the less, or the less with the greater.
14 | The two arms as robbers | Ṛgveda 10.4.6: like two robbers who risk their bodies, the two arms working the fire-drill with ten cords. Taskara, "thief".
15 | The Aśvins; the particles of comparison | Ṛgveda 10.40.2: where are you, Aśvins, by night and by day? as a widow draws her husband's brother to her bed. Vidhavā, devara, marya, yoṣā. Yathā as a particle of comparison, with examples.
16 | More particles of comparison | Ā, as in "like a lover to his lady"; jāra, the sun as lover of the dawn. The comparisons of being (meṣa), form (agni), manner (-thā) and the established type (-vat).
17 | Like Priyamedha, like Atri | Ṛgveda 1.45.3: hear Praskaṇva's call as you heard Priyamedha, Atri, Virūpa and Aṅgiras. The names of the seers derived: Bhṛgu, Aṅgiras, Atri, Vaikhānasa, Bhāradvāja.
18 | Implied comparisons; animal names | Comparisons with the particle dropped: lion and tiger for praise, dog and crow for blame. Whether kāka is an imitation of sound; tittiri, kapiñjala, śvan, siṃha, vyāghra derived.
19 | Praise, the wise, priests, sacrifice … | Verbs of praise (forty-four); the wise, singers, sacrifice (yajña), priests (ṛtvij); verbs of asking, giving, urging and sleeping; well, thief, the hidden, far, old and new, each derived.
20 | Words in pairs | The twenty-six words that come in pairs, each pair explained with its Vedic use: prapitva and abhīka, dabhra and arbhaka, tiraḥ and sataḥ, tva and nema, ṛkṣa and stṛ (the stars), vamrī and upajihvikā (ants), ūrdara and kṛdara (the granary).
21 | More pairs | Rambha and pināka (staff), menā and gnā (women), śepa and vaitasa, ayā and enā, siṣaktu and sacata, bhyasate and rejate (fear and trembling). The twenty-four names of heaven and earth together.
22 | Heaven and earth | Ṛgveda 1.185.1: which of the two came first, which later? how were they born? who knows, you seers? They bear all by themselves, and turn like two wheels, day and night. The greatness of heaven and earth.
