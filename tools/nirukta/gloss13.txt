# English key to the Nirukta pariśiṣṭa (adhyāya 13, with the second numbering 14.1–14.35 from khaṇḍa 14), one khaṇḍa a line: number | heading | gloss. Original.
1 | The supplement: the over-praises | These are called "over-praises" (atistuti), or simply plain statements, from the greatness of the deity. First of Agni: "you, Agni, from the heavens…"; of Varuṇa: "not even the twinkling of an eye is far from you". Now one of Indra.
2 | Indra beyond measure | Ṛgveda 8.70.5: if you had a hundred heavens and a hundred earths, Indra, a thousand suns could not equal you, nor heaven and earth. Now one of the sun.
3 | The sun as Vṛṣākapi | Ṛgveda 10.86.22: when you went up to the house, Vṛṣākapi, where was that much-eating beast? To whom did the people-rouser go? Now one of the sun's rays.
4 | The rays and Vṛṣākapi; the Aśvins | Ṛgveda 10.86.1: they let flow the pressing, and did not think Indra a god, where Vṛṣākapi rejoiced. Now one of the Aśvins.
5 | The Aśvins as goads | Ṛgveda 10.106.6: like two goads, sustainers and smiters, like Naitośa's sons, like water-born jewels; make my aging body unaging. Now one of Soma.
6 | Soma; the sacrifice | Ṛgveda 9.58.1: the joyful one crosses over, the stream of pressed soma runs. Now one of the sacrifice.
7 | The bull with four horns | Ṛgveda 4.58.3: four horns, three feet, two heads, seven hands, the bull bound three ways roars; the great god entered mortals. The Vedas, the three pressings, the opening and closing rites, the seven metres, mantra, Brāhmaṇa and ritual.
8 | Those who go to heaven | Atharvaveda/Taittirīya verse: going to heaven they do not look back, they mount heaven over earth and sky, who spread the sacrifice that flows on every side. Now one of speech.
9 | The four quarters of speech | Ṛgveda 1.164.45: speech is measured in four quarters; the wise Brāhmaṇas know them; three are hidden, men speak the fourth. Seers, grammarians, ritualists, etymologists and others on what the four are; the Brāhmaṇa on speech divided among the worlds. Now one of the imperishable.
10 | The imperishable syllable | Ṛgveda 1.164.39: the verses rest on the imperishable in the highest heaven, where all the gods sit; what will one who does not know it do with the verse? Śākapūṇi: it is Om.
11 | The sun or the self | Śākapūṇi's son: it is the sun, whom all beings praise; the gods are its rays. In the self: the body is the verse, the imperishable part is the self, the gods are the senses that become one in it.
12 | Akṣara; the seer of reasoning | Akṣara, "not perishing". This reflection on the mantras' sense is reasoning, from scripture and from logic; mantras are explained in context, not one by one. When the seers were passing away, men asked the gods who their seer would be; the gods gave them reasoning as a seer. So whatever a learned man reasons out is as a seer's.
13 | The end of the old work | Ṛgveda 10.71.8: when Brāhmaṇa friends sacrifice together with thoughts shaped in the heart, they leave some behind in knowledge. This knowledge is learning, thought and insight, to be crossed by austerity; it is not to be expounded by one who wants long life. The rest of the hymns is left aside. Whoever names a deity attains its nature. Here the manuscripts close a chapter.
14 | The upward path; the great self speaks | The deities and sacrifice are explained; now the path upward. "The sun is the self". The hymns proclaim the great self (Ṛgveda 1.164.46); the great self, wishing to know itself, declared itself in two verses.
15 | "I am Agni" | Verses: I am Agni, Jātavedas by birth; ghee is my eye, immortality in my mouth; I am the firstborn of order, the navel of immortality; I who am food eat the eater of food. Knowing this, he appeared.
16 | The great self and the three qualities | Ṛgveda 1.164.31: I saw the herdsman who never rests, going to and fro on the paths. This great self is pure being: the highest Brahman, real, unmanifest, without touch, form, taste or smell. It is threefold, sattva, rajas and tamas; sattva stands pure in the middle.
17 | The elements and the day and night of Brahmā | Space has sound; wind, sound and touch; light, form also; water, taste; earth, smell. This day lasts a thousand ages, then all is drawn back, earth into water up to nature, which sleeps a night of a thousand ages; "those who know the day of Brahmā…".
18 | The person and his parts | As it turns, the other follows; the creator imagines himself apart. From the elements come breath, eye and speech; three parts from the mother (skin, flesh, blood), three from the father (bone, sinew, marrow), and food and drink: so the person is made of all.
19 | Birth and rebirth | What he follows he becomes: god by dharma, immortal by knowledge, fallen by desire. The growth of the embryo from the first night to the ninth month; the verses of the one in the womb (I have died and been born again); in the tenth month he is born, and touched by the wind forgets birth and death.
20 | The body and the release | The bones, skull, sinews, vital points and hairs counted. Knowledge and deeds follow him; sunk in the darkness of ignorance, beset by pairs (age and death, hunger, grief, anger, greed, fear, joy, envy), he leaves the body in an instant, takes a fiery body, enjoys the fruit of his deeds, and returns.
21 | The path of smoke | Those who practise austerity relying on violence, or Vedic rites, go to smoke, night, the waning fortnight, the southern course, the world of the fathers, the moon, wind, rain and plants, and come back to this world.
22 | The path of light | Those who give up violence and rely on knowledge go to flame, day, the waxing fortnight, the northern course, the world of the gods, the sun, lightning, the mind-born person and the world of Brahman, and do not return.
23 | You do not know him | Ṛgveda 10.82.7: you do not know him who made these; another is within you; wrapped in mist the hymn-singers wander. The knower and the friend; whoever knows the self as Brahman becomes Brahman, a mere witness, unbound. Now the names of the great self.
24 | The names of the great self | A list of names of the great self: haṃsa, gharma, yajña, vena, soma, bhūta, bhuvana, āpaḥ, vyoma, yaśaḥ, ṛta, satya, pūrṇa, amṛta, ākāśa, samudra, brahma, ātmā and many more. These hymns and verses proclaim it.
25 | Soma the begetter | Ṛgveda 9.96.5: Soma flows, begetter of thoughts, of heaven and earth, of Agni, the sun, Indra and Viṣṇu.
26 | Brahmā of the gods | Ṛgveda 9.96.6: Brahmā of the gods, path-finder of poets, seer of sages, buffalo among beasts, falcon among vultures, axe among trees, Soma passes the filter. Read of the sun among its rays, and of the self among the senses.
27 | The three voices | Ṛgveda 9.97.34: the bearer raises three voices, the vision of order, the thought of Brahman: verses, formulas and chants (the sun), or learning, thought and insight (the self).
28 | The cows to Soma | Ṛgveda 9.97.35: the cows, the singers, the hymns go to Soma: the rays to the sun, or the senses to the self, where they become one.
29 | Soma the king who stepped over the ocean | Ṛgveda 9.97.40: the king stepped over the ocean, begetting creatures; the drop grew great on the woollen filter: the sun by rain, or the self by knowledge.
30 | Soma, the waters' germ | Ṛgveda 9.97.41: great is what Soma did, the waters' germ, who chose the gods, gave Indra strength, begot light in the sun. The drop is the sun, and the self.
31 | The old one swallows the young | Ṛgveda 10.55.5: the grey one swallowed the young moon; see the god's wisdom: who died yesterday lives today. The sun and moon, or the self and the mind.
32 | The seven born together | Ṛgveda 1.164.15: of the seven born together, the seventh is born alone; six are twins, god-born seers. The six rays and the sun, or the six senses and the self.
33 | Women who are men | Ṛgveda 1.164.16: they told me those women were men; the sighted sees, the blind does not; the son who is a poet knows, and becomes his father's father.
34 | Half-germs of Viṣṇu | Ṛgveda 1.164.36: the seven half-germs, seed of the world, stand by Viṣṇu's ordinance; wise in thought and mind, they surround all.
35 | I do not know what I am | Ṛgveda 1.164.37: I do not know what this is that I am; secret, bound, I wander with my mind. The sun, and the self.
36 | The immortal with the mortal | Ṛgveda 1.164.38: the immortal goes back and forth with the mortal, bound by its own nature; men see one and not the other: the sun with the moon, the self with the mind.
37 | The eldest in the worlds | Ṛgveda 10.120.1: that was the eldest in the worlds, from which the fierce one was born; as soon as born he destroys his foes.
38 | Who yokes the oxen of order? | Ṛgveda 1.84.16: who today yokes to the pole of order the eager, fiery steeds? Who knows them lives long.
39 | Who goes, who fears? | Ṛgveda 1.84.17: who goes, who gives, who fears, who thinks Indra present? Who speaks for son, for wealth, for people?
40 | Who honours Agni? | Ṛgveda 1.84.18: who honours Agni with oblation and ghee, with the ladle and the fixed seasons? For whom do the gods bring the offering quickly?
41 | No other comforter | Ṛgveda 1.84.19: you, mighty god, shall praise the mortal; there is no other comforter than you, Maghavan; I speak my word to you.
42 | Two birds on one tree | Ṛgveda 1.164.20: two birds, companions, cling to one tree; one eats the sweet fig, the other looks on without eating. The self and the supreme self in the body. In brackets, a khila verse and its comment, absent from the shorter recension.
43 | We call Agni | Ṛgveda 8.11.6: we mortals call the inspired god Agni with songs for help. A mystical reading of the syllables and breaths; "Agni is all the gods".
44 | The swan in the light | Ṛgveda 4.40.5: the swan dwelling in brightness, the Vasu in the mid-air, the priest at the altar, the guest in the house; born of waters, cows, order and rock: the sun and the supreme self.
45 | Tryambaka | Ṛgveda 7.59.12: we worship the three-eyed one, fragrant, increasing prosperity; like a cucumber from its stalk may I be freed from death, not from immortality. Tryambaka is Rudra.
46 | Jātavedas again | Ṛgveda 1.99.1: let us press soma for Jātavedas; may he carry us over all difficulties as a boatman over a river. In brackets, a further verse on the waters, absent from part of the manuscripts.
47 | Live a hundred autumns | Ṛgveda 10.161.4: live a hundred autumns growing, a hundred winters, a hundred springs; Indra, Agni, Savitṛ and Bṛhaspati have given him back with an oblation of a hundred years.
48 | Homage to Yāska | A verse: let your gifts and help never fail us. This is the inquiry into the self and into all beings; who knows it reaches the world, form and company of Brahman. Homage to Brahman, to the great being, to Yāska.
