# English key to Nirukta adhyāya 11, one khaṇḍa a line: number | heading | gloss. Original.
1 | The falcon | The falcon, already explained, has this verse.
2 | The falcon brings the soma; Soma | Ṛgveda 4.26.7: the falcon carried off the soma, a thousand pressings and ten thousand together; there the wise one left the niggards behind in the joy of soma. Some take it as Indra's, since the hymn is Indra's. Soma the plant, "from pressing"; its incidental use is common, its use as the chief deity rare.
3 | Soma purified | Ṛgveda 9.1.1: flow, Soma, in your sweetest, most gladdening stream, pressed for Indra to drink. A verse of the moon, or of this soma.
4 | The soma the Brāhmaṇas know | Ṛgveda 10.85.3: one thinks he has drunk soma when they crush the plant; the soma the Brāhmaṇas know no one eats. Read of the sacrifice (no non-sacrificer eats it) and of the gods (the moon, which no one but the gods eats).
5 | The moon waxing again; the moon | Ṛgveda 10.85.5: when they drink you, god, you swell again; Vāyu is the guardian of soma, the moon the shaper of years. The moon, candramas, derived.
6 | Ever new; Death | Ṛgveda 10.85.19: new and new he is born, the banner of the days, going before the dawns; he gives the gods their share and lengthens life. Mṛtyu, death, "who makes die" or (Śatabalākṣa Maudgalya) "who removes the dead".
7 | Death; Indra and Viṣṇu's soma | Ṛgveda 10.18.1: go, Death, by your own path, other than the path of the gods; harm not our children or our heroes. Ṛgveda 1.155.2 on Indra and Viṣṇu.
8 | Viśvānara | Viśvānara, already explained.
9 | Viśvānara and Indra | Ṛgveda 10.50.1: sing to the great, joyful Viśvānara, giver of food, whose might heaven and earth serve. Who but the god of the mid-air?
10 | Viśvānara Savitṛ; Dhātṛ | Ṛgveda 7.76.1: Viśvānara, god Savitṛ, raised the immortal light for all men. Dhātṛ, the disposer of all.
11 | Dhātṛ; Vidhātṛ | Atharvaveda-like verse: may Dhātṛ give the giver unfailing life; may we gain the favour of the god of true law. Vidhātṛ, explained with Dhātṛ, appears in passing in a verse to many gods.
12 | The many-god verse | Ṛgveda 10.167.3: by the law of King Soma and Varuṇa, in the shelter of Bṛhaspati and Anumati, praised by you, Maghavan, Dhātṛ and Vidhātṛ, I have drunk the soma-jars. Kalaśa.
13 | The groups of the mid-air; the Maruts | Now the groups of gods of the mid-air, first the Maruts: "of measured sound", "of measured light", or "running greatly". Ṛgveda 1.88.1: come, Maruts, with your lightning chariots, horse-winged, like birds with abundant food.
14 | The Rudras | The Rudras, already explained.
15 | The Rudras; the Ṛbhus | Ṛgveda 5.57.1: come, Rudras, with Indra, on golden chariots, as springs of heaven to the thirsty. The Ṛbhus: "shining widely", "shining by order", "being by order".
16 | The Ṛbhus who won immortality | Ṛgveda 1.110.4: by their quick works the mortal Ṛbhus, sons of Sudhanvan, won immortality and in a year joined the gods. Ṛbhu, Vibhvan and Vāja were the three sons of Sudhanvan Āṅgirasa; the sun's rays too are called Ṛbhus. The Aṅgirases.
17 | The Aṅgirases; the fathers | Ṛgveda 10.62.5: various are the seers, of deep power; they are sons of Aṅgiras, born of Agni. The fathers, pitaraḥ.
18 | The fathers; Atharvans and Bhṛgus | Ṛgveda 10.15.1: let the lower, higher and middle soma-loving fathers rise; may they help us at our calls. Yama is of the mid-air, so the fathers are too. Atharvan, "without wavering".
19 | Aṅgirases, Navagvas, Atharvans, Bhṛgus; the seers | Ṛgveda 10.14.6: our fathers the Aṅgirases, Navagvas, Atharvans and Bhṛgus; may we be in their favour. A group of the mid-air, say the etymologists; the fathers, say the story-tellers. The seers too are praised.
20 | The Vasiṣṭhas; the Āptyas | Ṛgveda 7.33.8: like the sun is their light, like the ocean their depth; none can follow your praise, Vasiṣṭhas. The Āptyas.
21 | The Āptyas | Ṛgveda 10.120.6: I praise the many-formed, the mightiest of the Āptyas, who splits the seven Dānus by his strength.
22 | The goddesses of the mid-air; Aditi | Now the women of the mid-air, first Aditi. Ṛgveda 10.64.5: at the birth of Dakṣa, Aditi, you serve the two kings Mitra and Varuṇa; Aryaman of the unhurried path. Dakṣa is the sun; Aditi daughter of Dakṣa, and Dakṣa son of Aditi.
23 | How Dakṣa and Aditi beget each other; Agni as Aditi | They may be born of each other by the nature of gods. Agni too is called Aditi.
24 | Aditi; Saramā | Ṛgveda 1.94.15: to whom you give good things, Aditi, sinless in every rite. Āgas, enas, kilbiṣa. Saramā.
25 | Saramā and the Paṇis | Ṛgveda 10.108.1: seeking what has Saramā come here? The way is long. What is your errand, how did you cross the waters of the Rasā? The divine bitch sent by Indra parleyed with the Paṇis. Sarasvatī.
26 | Sarasvatī | Ṛgveda 1.3.10: may pure Sarasvatī, rich in rewards, desire our sacrifice.
27 | Sarasvatī as speech; Vāc | Ṛgveda 1.3.12: Sarasvatī lights up the great flood and rules all thoughts; so speech of the mid-air. Vāc.
28 | Speech the queen | Ṛgveda 8.100.10: when Speech, queen of the gods, sat down, she milked the four quarters for food; where has her highest gone?
29 | All creatures speak her; Anumati and Rākā | Ṛgveda 8.100.11: the gods begot divine Speech; creatures of all forms speak her. Anumati and Rākā: wives of gods, or the two full-moon days. Anumati.
30 | Anumati; Rākā | Vājasaneyi verse: favour us, Anumati, give happiness, food and long life. Rākā, "the giver".
31 | Rākā; Sinīvālī and Kuhū | Ṛgveda 2.32.4: I call Rākā; may she sew the work with an unbreaking needle and give a hero worth a hundred. Sinīvālī and Kuhū: wives of gods, or the two new-moon days. Sinīvālī.
32 | Sinīvālī; Kuhū | Ṛgveda 2.32.6: broad-hipped Sinīvālī, sister of the gods, give us offspring. Kuhū, "where was she?".
33 | Kuhū; Yamī | Atharvaveda 7.47.1: I call Kuhū of good deeds; may she give us the fame of the fathers. Yamī.
34 | Yamī | Ṛgveda 10.10.14: embrace another, Yamī, as a creeper a tree. Yamī desired Yama, and he refused her; so the story.
35 | Urvaśī | Urvaśī, already explained.
36 | Urvaśī; the earth | Ṛgveda 10.95.10: she who flashed like lightning, bringing me the waters' treasures; may Urvaśī lengthen life. Pṛthivī.
37 | The earth; Indrāṇī | Ṛgveda 5.84.1: truly, earth, you bear the breaking of the mountains (clouds). Indrāṇī, Indra's wife.
38 | Indrāṇī | Ṛgveda 10.86.11: of these women I have heard Indrāṇī most blessed; her husband shall not die of age.
39 | Indra and Vṛṣākapi; Gaurī | Ṛgveda 10.86.12: I do not delight, Indrāṇī, without my friend Vṛṣākapi. Gaurī, "the shining".
40 | Gaurī of a thousand syllables | Ṛgveda 1.164.41: the buffalo-cow lowed, shaping the waters, one-footed, two-footed, four-, eight-, nine-footed, of a thousand syllables in the highest heaven: the quarters, the half-quarters, the sun.
41 | From her the oceans flow | Ṛgveda 1.164.42: from her the oceans flow and the four quarters live; from her flows the imperishable, by which all lives. Go, the cow.
42 | The lowing cow | Ṛgveda 1.164.28: the cow lowed after her calf, licking its head; speech of the mid-air, or the cow that yields the gharma milk. Dhenu, the milch cow.
43 | The milch cow; Aghnyā | Ṛgveda 1.164.26: I call this easily milked cow; may Savitṛ quicken the best impulse. Aghnyā, "not to be killed".
44 | Aghnyā | Ṛgveda 1.164.40: be fortunate, eating good pasture; eat grass, Aghnyā, and drink pure water at all times.
45 | The lowing cow; Pathyā Svasti | Ṛgveda 1.164.27: lowing, mistress of riches, she came seeking her calf. Pathyā Svasti, "wellbeing of the road".
46 | Svasti; Uṣas | Ṛgveda 10.63.16: Svasti is best on the road; may she guard us at home and abroad. Uṣas, dawn.
47 | Dawn fled the cart | Ṛgveda 4.30.10: Dawn fled from the crushed cart, afraid, when the bull struck it. Anas, the cart or the cloud.
48 | Dawn's broken cart; Iḷā | Ṛgveda 4.30.11: Dawn's cart lies crushed by the Vipāś; she fled far. Iḷā.
49 | Iḷā; Rodasī | Ṛgveda 5.41.19: may Iḷā, mother of the herd, and Urvaśī with the rivers praise us. Rodasī, Rudra's wife.
50 | Rodasī | Ṛgveda 5.56.8: we call the swift chariot of the Maruts, on which Rodasī stands with the Maruts, bearing delights.
